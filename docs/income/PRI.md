# Puerto Rico — earned income, nomad and expat dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026

## Summary

Puerto Rico taxes residents at 0% to USD 9,000, then 7%, 14%, 25% and 33% above USD 61,500, with the total tax set at 95% of the regular amount (92% up to USD 100,000 gross), so the effective top rate at USD 150,000 is about 31.35%. Employees pay 7.65% FICA (OASDI cap USD 184,500), the self-employed 15.3%. There is no nomad visa; the relevant incentive is the Act 60 export services decree (4%, 15+15 years). US citizens who are bona fide residents exclude Puerto Rico-source income from US federal tax.

## Income tax and social security

Deloitte 2026 schedule: 0% to USD 9,000; 7% to 25,000; 14% to 41,500; 25% to 61,500; 33% above; 5% surtax above USD 500,000 net taxable income; total tax 95% of regular tax (92% for gross income up to USD 100,000); long-term capital gains 15%; foreign tax credit available. Social security: OASDI 6.2% up to USD 184,500 plus Medicare 1.45% for employees; self-employed pay twice that.

## Digital nomad visa

None. Puerto Rico follows US immigration law, and US citizens need no visa. Remote workers employed by foreign employers must make estimated tax payments where no withholding applies (Deloitte).

## Expat regimes

Act 60 export services decree: 4% on eligible export-service income of a Puerto Rico business serving clients outside the island, for 15 years renewable for 15, with dividends to resident owners exempt (DDEC Incentives Code brochure: export of services from Puerto Rico, 4% income tax rate, 100% exemption on dividends; InCorp and Taxes for Expats for the conditions). The services must be performed in Puerto Rico for clients outside it, so the relief covers work done while living there (covers_local_work true), but only through a business with a decree. It does not apply to ordinary salary. Interaction with US citizenship: IRC 933 exclusion applies to bona fide residents' Puerto Rico-source income; self-employment tax and US-source income remain taxable; IRS residency scrutiny is active. The Act 60 Individual Resident Investor decree (investment income) is not an earned-income regime.

## Living costs

Zillow San Juan rentals, first results page (41 of 498 listings, asking rents in USD, furnishing not stated; building 'from' prices excluded): 1-bed median USD 2,500 from 3 units (Isla Verde/Ocean Park 1,800; Condado 2,500 and 2,990); 2-bed median USD 4,000 from 5 units (Condado 2,800 to 4,700; Old San Juan 3,775). Mostly Condado, the premium end: a typical San Juan rent is lower (single units in Hato Rey, Santurce and Rio Piedras appear at USD 745 to 1,900). Low confidence.

## Residence routes

US citizens can move freely and work. Citizens of the EU and other countries need US immigration status (visa or green card) because Puerto Rico follows US immigration law; there is no remote-worker visa and no nomad tax regime. Act 60 does not give immigration rights. Act 60 export services decrees (4%) are for businesses serving clients outside Puerto Rico; the separate Individual Resident Investor decree concerns investment income, not earned income. US citizenship interaction: a US citizen who becomes a bona fide resident of Puerto Rico (presence, tax home and closer connection tests) excludes Puerto Rico-source income from US federal income tax (IRC 933), so Puerto Rico is the one place where moving lowers US tax; a salary for work done in Puerto Rico is Puerto Rico-source, but US-source income is not covered, Social Security and Medicare (or self-employment) tax still apply, and US estate and gift tax continues to apply to mainland-born citizens.

## Open questions

- The 95%/92% adjustment factor is described by Deloitte as 'total tax equals 95% of the regular tax plus the surtax (92% for gross income up to USD 100,000)'; how it interacts with deductions and credits (and the alternate basic tax) was not researched, and it is not folded into the brackets (it lowers the modelled tax by 5% to 8%).
- US citizens living in Puerto Rico also owe US federal tax on non-Puerto Rico-source income, and self-employment tax; see regimes.
- The DDEC brochure confirms the 4% rate and dividend exemption for export activities; the statutory text of Act 60 Chapter 3, the eligible-services list and the 15+15-year term were taken from the practitioner guide (InCorp) and not checked against the Act. Act 38-2026 reportedly rewrote the individual resident investor chapter but not the export services chapter.
- Whether a freelance individual can qualify without incorporating, and any minimum owner salary, are not answered by the sources.
- US citizens: a bona fide Puerto Rico resident (IRC 933 / 937 tests) generally excludes Puerto Rico-source income from US federal income tax, but self-employment tax (15.3%) still applies and US-source income, dividends and interest stay US-taxable. Green card holders are US taxpayers; EU and other non-US citizens need US immigration status to work in Puerto Rico. The IRS scrutinises residency claims.
- Rents rest on 8 listings from one results page, mostly Condado; low confidence.
- Statutory text of Act 60 Chapter 3 and Act 38-2026 not checked.
- Federal-tax interaction summarised from practitioner guides and the repo's earlier research (data/jurisdictions/PRI.json), not from IRS pages opened in this pass.
- Puerto Rico: social_security_deductible left null. The PR Internal Revenue Code (OGP text rev. 18 June 2026) lets an individual in trade or business deduct 50% of the federal self-employment tax (s. 1033.01(b)(3)), but no clear deduction for employee FICA was found in s. 1033.15(a); (a)(6) covers government retirement systems only, and the Hacienda booklet is silent. Employee FICA is likely not deductible (0) and half of SE tax is for the self-employed.
