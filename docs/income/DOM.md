# Dominican Republic — earned income, nomad and expat dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026

## Summary

The Dominican Republic taxes on a territorial basis: Dominican-source income is taxed at 0% up to DOP 416,220, then 15%, 20% and 25%; foreign-source income is generally not taxed, except foreign investments and financial gains, and new residents are taxed on these after the third year. Employees withhold 2.87% pension and 3.04% health.

- Self-employed notes: No mandatory contribution found for independent workers: the SIPEN FAQ (opened 2026-10-15) says the contributory-subsidised pension regime for independents 'aun no ha entrado en vigor' (has not yet taken effect), so the 0% rate means 'no contribution currently required', not a confirmed exemption; voluntary or subsidised enrolment exists for low earners. Invoicing within the country requires an RNC registration (per Bolsillo Practico).
- Local tax note: No municipal income tax. Pension contributions are capped at 20 minimum wages and health at 10 minimum wages; the minimum wage amount was not stated, so no peso cap is recorded. The 0.5% technical-education levy applies to bonuses only. Employee rate 0.0591 = 2.87% + 3.04%.
- Confidence: medium; verification: partially_verified.

## Digital nomad visa

No dedicated digital nomad visa was found for this country (see Residence routes).

## Expat regimes

**Territorial taxation - foreign-source income of residents** (other). Dominican law taxes only Dominican-source income. Foreign-source income, including remote work for foreign clients, is generally outside the income tax base; foreign investments and financial gains of residents are taxed, and for individuals who become residents, foreign-source items of that kind only after the third year. Caveat: Art. 272 of the Codigo Tributario (Ley 11-92) treats income from personal work and professional practice as Dominican-source and Art. 269 taxes residents on all Dominican-source income, so pay for work physically done in the DR is not clearly outside the base; only income from work genuinely performed abroad is.

- Treatment: exempt; duration: open-ended years. Permanent feature of the tax system, not time-limited.
- Covers work physically performed in the country (covers_local_work): no.
- Maximum income: none.

## Residence routes

No formal digital nomad visa as of April 2026 per one source. EU, US and many nationals enter on a tourist card/visa and may extend stays to about 150 days; longer stays use the rentista residence (foreign passive income of at least USD 2,000 a month) or temporary residence with a bank certification (RT-9). A skilled employee needs a work permit and temporary residence sponsored by a Dominican employer.

## Open questions

- Official migration pages were not reached; routes are from one secondary source.
- 2-bedroom rent not found (Numbeo gives only 1- and 3-bedroom; Cuanto Me Cuesta has no DR page; Expatistan and Supercasas could not be used).
- Peso caps on contributions (20 minimum wages for pension, 10 for health, per PwC) not converted because the cotizable minimum wage was not retrieved (CNSS/TSS/Ministry of Labour pages blocked or not found); the employee rate is therefore recorded uncapped, slightly overstating contributions at high incomes.
- Self-employed: per the SIPEN FAQ the independent-workers contributory regime is not yet in force; the TSS/CNSS resolutions were not reachable to confirm nothing else applies.
- Remote work performed while living in the DR: Art. 272 (c) of Ley 11-92 lists income 'del trabajo personal, ejercicio de profesiones u oficios' as Dominican-source without a territorial qualifier, and Art. 269 taxes residents on Dominican-source income; a secondary blog (Bolsillo Practico) says foreign-client income 'en principio' is untaxed. No DGII ruling on remote work for foreign clients was found.
- PwC shows a different scale from 2027 (zero up to DOP 480,000 and a 27% top bracket above DOP 4.8 million); 2026 figures are used here.
- Not a visa. As of April 2026 one source reports no formal digital nomad visa (a Senate bill is pending); remote workers use tourist stays (about 150 days), the rentista residence (foreign passive income of at least USD 2,000 a month under Law 171-07) or ordinary temporary residence.
- Treatment of local-source income of the Expat persona's Dominican employer is the ordinary scale.
- covers_local_work=false is a reading of Art. 272(b)-(c) and Art. 269 of Ley 11-92 (personal work income is Dominican-source; no explicit exemption for remote work done in the DR); no DGII ruling was found, so treat as low-medium confidence.
