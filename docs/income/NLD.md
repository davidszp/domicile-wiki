# Netherlands — earned income, nomad and expat dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026

## Summary

Box 1 income tax for 2026 is 8.10% to EUR 38,883, 37.56% to EUR 78,426 and 49.5% above; the first bracket also carries 27.65% national insurance premiums (AOW, Anw, Wlz), shown as social security here. Employee insurance premiums (WW, WIA) and the 6.10% income-related health levy are paid by the employer; the employee pays a nominal health premium of about EUR 1,900 a year to the insurer. General and labour tax credits reduce the bill but could not be verified.

## Expat regimes

### 30% ruling (expat scheme)

An employer can pay a recruited-from-abroad or transferred employee with scarce expertise up to 30% of wages tax-free in 2026 (2025 and 2026), based on wages up to the WNT cap of EUR 262,000. From 1 January 2027 the maximum falls to 27%: employees who first used the ruling in 2024 move to 27% from 2027 but keep the old salary norms; those starting in 2025 or later also get the higher norms from 2027 (EUR 50,436 general, subject to indexation). Employees who started before 2024 keep 30%. The Loyens & Loeff piece was written when the plan was a bill; Business.gov.nl now states the 27% as fact.

- Treatment: partial_exemption, exempt share 30%
- Minimum income: 48,013 EUR; maximum: 262,000 EUR
- Duration: 5 years. Maximum 5 years from the first working day. Employer must file within 4 months of starting. 150 km rule: lived more than 150 km from the Dutch border for over 16 of the prior 24 months. Reduced norm EUR 36,497 for under-30s with a master's degree.
- Foreign income required: no; local employment required: yes; social security still due: yes

Open points: Whether social security applies to the tax-free allowance was not stated on an opened page (the ruling is a tax matter; assumed unchanged). 27% from 2027: Loyens & Loeff described a bill; Business.gov.nl states it as law. The WNT cap above EUR 262,000 is the 2026 figure only. Self-employed people cannot use the ruling (employed status required).

## Residence routes

EU/EEA and Swiss citizens have free movement. There is no digital nomad visa. Skilled non-EU employees use the highly skilled migrant or EU Blue Card route with an employer that is a recognised sponsor: 2026 thresholds are EUR 5,942 gross a month for HSM aged 30+ (EUR 4,357 under 30) and EUR 5,942 for the Blue Card. Self-employed non-EU nationals have separate routes (e.g. the DAFT treaty for US citizens) that were not verified here.
