# Germany — earned income, nomad and expat dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026

## Summary

Germany taxes worldwide income of residents on a progressive scale: 0% up to the EUR 12,348 basic allowance (2026), rising from 14% to 42% by EUR 69,878, 42% from EUR 69,879 and 45% from EUR 277,826. A solidarity surcharge of 5.5% of the tax applies in full from roughly EUR 105,500 taxable income single, so the top marginal rates are about 44.3% and 47.5%. Berlin levies no municipal income tax. Employees pay about 21.75% in social contributions (childless, average health fund), mostly capped; freelancers outside the statutory scheme pay no pension contributions but must carry health and long-term care insurance. Freelancers (Freiberufler) are also exempt from trade tax.

Brackets: 0% band up to the EUR 12,348 allowance (EUR 24,696 joint); the two progression zones follow the statutory formula in section 32a(1) EStG for 2026 (zone 2, EUR 12,349-17,799: (914.51 y + 1,400) y with y = (income - 12,348)/10,000; zone 3, EUR 17,800-69,878: (173.10 z + 2,397) z + 1,034.87 with z = (income - 17,799)/10,000; then 0.42 x - 11,135.63). The marginal rate is linear within each zone (14% rising to 23.97%, then 23.97% rising to 42%); it is shown as 11 steps, each the exact average marginal rate over the step, so the tax at every step boundary equals the statutory tax to the euro. Solidarity surcharge: nil if tax is up to EUR 19,950 (about EUR 73,463 taxable); sliding scale up to about EUR 105,500; full 5.5% above (folded into the last two brackets: 42% x 1.055 = 44.31%, 45% x 1.055 = 47.475%). No municipal income tax in Berlin. Employee 21.75% = pension 9.3 + unemployment 1.3 (ceiling EUR 101,400) + health 7.3 + average additional contribution 1.45 + long-term care 1.8 + childless supplement 0.6 (ceiling EUR 69,750 for health/care).

## Digital nomad visa

### Residence permit for freelance work (Freiberufler)

No dedicated digital nomad visa exists. Non-EU freelancers apply to the local Ausländerbehörde for a residence permit for freelance employment; approval depends on a viable freelance plan, proof of client demand (contracts or letters of intent), financing of living costs, accepted health insurance and any professional licence. Tax treatment is ordinary resident taxation.

- Treatment: standard
- Minimum income: none stated; maximum: none stated
- Duration: not stated years. Granted for a limited period and extendable while the activity stays viable; no maximum stated on the opened page.
- Foreign income required: no; local employment required: no; social security still due: no

Open points: Official make-it-in-germany.com page could not be opened (bot check); requirements come from a secondary guide. No minimum income figure found; over-45 pension-provision rule seen only on a third-party site.

## Residence routes

EU citizens (and EEA/Swiss) have free movement and need only to register their address. US and other non-EU remote workers have no nomad visa: self-employed professionals can apply for the freelance residence permit (Freiberufler) at the local Ausländerbehörde, which requires proof of client demand, financing and accepted health insurance. Skilled employees with a German employer typically use a skilled-worker permit or EU Blue Card (thresholds not verified in this file).
