# Belgium — earned income, nomad and expat dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026

## Summary

Belgian federal income tax for 2026 is 25% to EUR 16,720, 40% to EUR 29,510, 45% to EUR 51,070 and 50% above; adding an average municipal surcharge of about 7% of the tax (Brussels) gives 26.75% to 53.5%. Employees pay 13.07% social security uncapped; the self-employed pay 20.5% of net income up to about EUR 75,025 and 14.16% up to about EUR 110,562, capped at EUR 5,103 a quarter.

The tax-free allowance (about EUR 11,180 per one secondary source) is a credit worked into the lower bracket and is not modelled. A separate special social security contribution of EUR 9.30-60.94 a month (max EUR 731.28 per family) is not included.

- Employee social security: 13.07% (no cap).
- Self-employed social security: 20.50% (cap 110,562.42 EUR/yr).
- Self-employed notes: Main-occupation self-employed 2026: 20.5% on net taxable income up to EUR 75,024.54, 14.16% from there to EUR 110,562.42, nothing above; minimum EUR 890.42 a quarter for income under EUR 17,374.08, maximum EUR 5,103.05 a quarter (about EUR 20,412 a year); a fund administration fee of about 3-4% is added. The 20.5% headline is shown as the rate and the cap is where contributions stop.

## Expat regimes

### Special tax regime for inbound taxpayers (impatriates), as amended by the Law of 18 December 2025

Employees recruited abroad or seconded to a Belgian group company may have their employer reimburse expenses as a tax-free and social-security-free lump sum. As amended with retroactive effect from 1 January 2025 the lump sum is 35% of gross remuneration (was 30%) with the EUR 90,000 cap on the base abolished, and the minimum gross salary is EUR 70,000 (was EUR 75,000) for regular impatriates; researchers have no salary minimum but need a diploma. Conditions: no Belgian tax filing in the previous 60 months and previous residence more than 150 km from the Belgian border; the employer and employee must apply together within three months of starting.

- Treatment: partial_exemption, exempt share 35%
- Minimum income: 70,000 EUR; maximum: none stated
- Duration: 8 years. Five years, extendable by three more. Regime applies to those starting employment in Belgium from 1 January 2022.
- Foreign income required: no; local employment required: yes; social security still due: yes

Open points: Whether the national social security office aligns the exemption with the 35% (Andersen: not yet clarified). Social security still applies to the non-exempt remainder. PwC page still shows the old 30% / EUR 75,000 figures. Self-employed people are not eligible (employees only).

## Residence routes

EU/EEA citizens register with the commune. Belgium has no digital nomad visa (not confirmed on an official page). Skilled non-EU employees need a single permit via a Belgian employer (regional work authorisation) or an EU Blue Card; self-employed non-EU nationals need a professional card. None of the regional thresholds were verified here.
