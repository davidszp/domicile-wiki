# New Zealand — earned income, nomad and expat dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026

## Summary

Residents pay 10.5% to NZD 15,600, 17.5% to NZD 53,500, 30% to NZD 78,100, 33% to NZD 180,000 and 39% above; there is no general social security tax. Employees pay the ACC earners' levy of 1.75% up to NZD 156,641; KiwiSaver (3.5% default) is not compulsory. Visitors may work remotely for offshore clients, and new migrants get a four-year foreign income exemption that excludes employment and services income.

## Digital nomad visa

### Visitor visa remote work and non-resident visitor tax exemption

Visitor visas applied for from 27 January 2025 allow remote work for an employer or clients outside New Zealand. Inland Revenue's non-resident visitor category (from 1 April 2026) covers a visitor present up to 275 days in any 18-month period who is not a NZ tax resident or transitional resident, is not working for or paid by a NZ resident or NZ branch, does not sell to NZ customers, is not required to be in New Zealand for the work, is lawfully here and pays tax in a country where they are resident. Moore Markhams lists the income covered as employment income, services to non-resident persons and non-resident business income, so a remote worker's pay for work done while in New Zealand is exempt; work that requires physical presence in New Zealand, and work for NZ employers, is not. Counted as covering local work, but only for stays within the 275-day window.

- Treatment: exempt
- Minimum income: none recorded
- Duration: 0.75 years. Up to 275 days within any 18-month period (Inland Revenue). Immigration NZ's visitor visa page separately cites 92 days (income taxed elsewhere) or 183 days (treaty country) as day thresholds where New Zealand tax may arise; the two are not reconciled. Visitor visa length itself not stated.
- Foreign income required: True; local employment required: False; social security applies: False
- Covers pay for work done in the country: True
- Confidence: medium (partially_verified)

## Expat regimes

### Transitional resident (new migrant foreign income exemption)

A natural person who becomes NZ tax resident after at least 10 years as a non-resident is exempt from NZ tax on most foreign-sourced income for 48 months from the end of the month they become resident (once only; ends if you or your partner claim Working for Families; you can opt out). Inland Revenue's flowchart IR1249 states that foreign employment income and income from the supply of services are not exempt, so it does not cover a nomad's or expat's pay and mainly shelters foreign investment income.

- Treatment: exempt
- Minimum income: none recorded
- Duration: 4 years. 48 months after the end of the month in which the person becomes resident, ignoring the 183-day backdating rule (IR1249).
- Foreign income required: True; local employment required: False; social security applies: True
- Covers pay for work done in the country: False
- Confidence: high (verified)

## Residence routes

EU, US and other citizens: visitors can work remotely for offshore employers or clients on a visitor visa (applications from 27 January 2025), with no dedicated digital nomad visa; remote work on a visitor visa cannot be for NZ clients and stays are limited by visitor visa length and tax day counts. Skilled employees need an employer-sponsored work visa; categories were not researched.
