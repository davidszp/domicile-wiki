# Slovenia — earned income, nomad and expat dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026

## Summary

Slovenian income tax is progressive from 16% to 50% after a EUR 5,551.93 allowance. Employees pay 23.1% social security; the self-employed about 40.2% (derived). The nomad permit lasts one year and is non-renewable. A secondment relief excludes 20% of salary. Ljubljana median asking rents: EUR 1,000 for one bedroom, EUR 1,600 for two (thin sample).

## Income tax and social security

Marginal rates on taxable income (EUR):

- from 0.00: 16.00%
- from 9,721.43: 26.00%
- from 28,592.44: 33.00%
- from 57,184.88: 39.00%
- from 82,346.23: 50.00%

Personal allowance deducted before the brackets: 5,551.93 EUR.

Employee social security: 23.10% (cap: none stated). Self-employed: 40.20% (cap: none stated).

Progressive income tax of 16% to EUR 9,721.43, 26% to 28,592.44, 33% to 57,184.88, 39% to 82,346.23 and 50% above, after a general allowance of EUR 5,551.93 (higher at low incomes). Employees pay 22.1% social security plus 1% long-term care; the self-employed bear the employee and employer shares.

Self-employed notes: PwC (other taxes) lists employee rates 22.10% (pension 15.50, health 6.36, unemployment 0.14, parental 0.10) and employer rates 16.10% (pension 8.85, health 6.56, unemployment 0.06, injury 0.53, parental 0.10), plus long-term care of 1% for employees, 1% for employers and, expressly, 2% of the relevant base for the self-employed. A compulsory supplementary health contribution of EUR 39.36 a month applies March 2026 to February 2027. The recorded self-employed rate 40.2% = 22.1% + 16.1% + 2% is DERIVED: it assumes sole proprietors bear both the employee and employer shares (Remote Work Europe says they 'bear both shares'); PwC does not list self-employed rates, and the injury share may not apply. The contribution base (minimum/maximum) and any ceiling were not found, so no cap is recorded. Flat-rate (normirani) expense rules for sole proprietors were not found.

Local tax: No municipal income tax. The general allowance is EUR 5,551.93 for income at or above EUR 17,766.18 and is larger below that; the recorded value is the flat amount.

## Digital nomad visa

Non-EU applicants, roughly twice the average net wage, up to one year. No special tax rate.

## Expat regimes

No general expat regime. A special tax base for seconded employees excludes 20% of salary up to EUR 1,000 a month for up to 60 months in 10 years.

Regimes recorded in the JSON file:

- **Digital nomad permit** (digital_nomad_visa, treatment: standard): Temporary residence permit for non-EU nationals employed by, or under a civil-law contract with, a business based outside Slovenia, or self-employed abroad, working entirely remotely with ICT (gov.si, 21 Nov 2025). Requires monthly funds of at least twice the average monthly net salary in Slovenia (figure from the Official Gazette; any lawful income counts). Valid up to one year and 'cannot be extended'; a new application is possible six months after expiry, or a different permit type (e.g. employment) during validity. Family may join immediately. No flat tax for foreign remote workers: a resident pays the normal scale. The euro threshold (about EUR 3,200 net a month on Remote Work Europe, EUR 38,400 a year; a teaser there says about EUR 3,098) could not be tied to the Official Gazette figure. Minimum income: 38,400 EUR. Maximum income: none. Duration (years): 1. Up to one year, cannot be extended; re-application possible six months after expiry (gov.si). Foreign income required: True. Local employment required: False. Covers work done locally: None. Social security still applies: True.
- **Special tax base for seconded employees** (expat_regime, treatment: partial_exemption 0.2): For employees seconded to or from abroad for more than 30 uninterrupted days, with a workplace over 200 km away, a salary of at least 1.5 times the last known Slovenian average wage and no tax residence in the secondment country in the previous five years, 20% of salary (capped at EUR 1,000 a month) is excluded from the tax base. Covers income paid within at most 60 months in a 10-year period. Minimum income: none stated. Maximum income: none. Duration (years): 5. Maximum 60 months within 10 years from the first secondment. Foreign income required: False. Local employment required: True. Covers work done locally: None. Social security still applies: True.

## Living costs

Ljubljana: 1-bedroom EUR 1,000 a month, 2-bedroom EUR 1,600 (Global Property Guide Q3 2026, median asking rents, thin sample).

## Residence routes

EU citizens register their stay. US and other non-EU remote workers can use the digital nomad permit (about EUR 3,200 net a month, one year, described as non-renewable). Skilled employees from outside the EU need a single permit sponsored by a Slovenian employer or an EU Blue Card, which were not researched.

## Open questions

- Single-permit and Blue Card routes not researched.
- Official gov.si pages for the nomad permit not opened.
- Rents rest on one aggregator with a thin Slovenian sample; no second source obtained.
- Self-employed total of 40.2% is derived from PwC's employee and employer rates plus the stated 2% long-term care; PwC and the opened gov.si pages do not list self-employed rates or the base (confidence low for this figure).
- Contribution ceiling for employees and the minimum/maximum bases for the self-employed were not found.
- Digital nomad permit: The monthly amount (2x average net wage, Official Gazette) was not found on an official page; EUR 3,200 net is from Remote Work Europe, which also quotes EUR 3,098. The test is on net funds, so the gross equivalent is higher (not derived here).
- Digital nomad permit: Permit is one year and non-renewable (gov.si), so a two-year stay requires a six-month gap or another permit type.
- Digital nomad permit: Whether permit holders become tax residents (183 days) was not covered by the opened pages.
- Special tax base for seconded employees: The page does not say whether it applies to employees who relocate under a local contract rather than a secondment.
- Special tax base for seconded employees: The EUR 1,000 a month cap limits the benefit to EUR 12,000 of excluded salary a year; recorded as a 20% share.
