# Philippines — earned income, nomad and expat dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026

## Summary

Residents pay graduated tax of 0% to 35% (2023 onward) on compensation income; non-resident aliens engaged in trade or business use the same rates. Self-employed individuals and professionals under PHP 3 million of receipts can elect an 8% tax on receipts above PHP 250,000 instead. Foreign employees' SSS and PhilHealth contributions are capped at about PHP 51,000 a year.

## Income tax and social security

Rates apply to taxable income in PHP; allowance 0.

| From | Marginal rate |
|---|---|
| 0 | 0% |
| 250,000 | 15% |
| 400,000 | 20% |
| 800,000 | 25% |
| 2,000,000 | 30% |
| 8,000,000 | 35% |

- Employee social security: 4.25%, annual income cap: 1,200,000.
- Self-employed social security: 4.25%, cap: 1,200,000.
- Notes: Rate and cap are a blended equivalent: PwC states the maximum annual social tax for a foreign employee (SSS + PhilHealth) is PHP 51,000 (2025); PHP 51,000 / PHP 1,200,000 = 4.25%. Consistent with SSS employee 5% of a monthly salary credit capped at PHP 35,000 (PHP 21,000 a year) plus PhilHealth at about 2.5% of income up to PHP 100,000 a month (PhilHealth rate not verified). Pag-IBIG no longer required of foreigners. Self-employed SSS/PhilHealth rates are higher and not verified; the same blended figure is used. 8% option: 8% of gross receipts above PHP 250,000 in lieu of graduated rates and percentage tax, if not VAT-registered and receipts within PHP 3 million. Check pass 2026-10-15: PwC confirms only the PHP 51,000 maximum and the end of Pag-IBIG for foreigners; Forvis Mazars confirms SSS at 15% (employee 5%, employer 10%) on a monthly salary credit of PHP 5,000-35,000 from 2025, but did not state the self-employed share; PhilHealth 5% premium with a PHP 100,000 income ceiling was seen only in a search summary of PhilHealth Advisory 2025-0002, so the self-employed rate remains a placeholder equal to the employee blend.
- Local tax: No local income tax. Business owners pay local business taxes (not modelled).

## Digital nomad visa

### Digital Nomad Visa (Executive Order 86)

Remote-worker visa created by Executive Order No. 86 (24 April 2025). The order's text (Lawphil excerpt) requires proof of remote work and of sufficient income earned outside the Philippines, health insurance, no criminal record and no employment in the Philippines, with a stay of a maximum of one year and renewal possible. It sets no minimum income and says nothing about taxation: the BIR only helps study the programme and issue implementing guidelines. The claim that holders are not tax resident and exempt on foreign-employer pay appears only on a secondary site (thedigitalnomad.asia) with no citation, so no exemption is recorded. The order required DFA guidelines within 30 days and a pilot within 60 days of effectivity; that site's page (updated 30 July 2026) still says the visa is not launched, and the Bureau of Immigration's visa list shows no digital nomad visa.

- Treatment: standard
- Covers work performed locally: not established
- Minimum income: not stated; duration: 1 years. EO 86 s.3: stay up to a maximum of one year, renewable; renewal terms await the implementing guidelines (the secondary site claims 12 + 12 months).
- Foreign income required: True; local employment required: False; social security applies: False
- Confidence: medium (partially_verified)

## Residence routes

EU, US and other citizens: Executive Order 86 (2025) created a Digital Nomad Visa (stay up to one year, renewable, proof of foreign income, no work for Philippine employers), but implementing guidelines and launch could not be confirmed and the Bureau of Immigration's visa list does not show it; until it is open, remote workers use visa-free entry with extensions. Skilled employees need a 9(g) pre-arranged employment visa and Alien Employment Permit through a Philippine employer (not researched in detail here).
