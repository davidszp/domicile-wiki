# South Korea — earned income, nomad and expat dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026

## Summary

Progressive income tax of 6% to 45% plus local income tax of 10% of the tax gives a top combined marginal rate of 49.5%. Foreign employees who start work in Korea by 31 December 2026 can elect a flat 19% (20.9% with local tax) for up to 20 years in place of progressive rates and deductions; a 21% rate and 2029 deadline are only proposed. Employees pay about 9.7% in pension, health, long-term care and employment insurance.

Self-employed people are 'local subscribers': they pay the full National Pension (9.5% total rate in 2026, no employer share; PwC) and regional health insurance. The NHIS page gives a 2026 health insurance rate of 7.19%, and long-term care is 13.14% of the health premium (braindetox.kr), which matches PwC's roughly 8.135% for health plus long-term care. The 17.63% used (9.5% + 7.19% x 1.1314) is therefore built from sourced rates but is an upper-bound ESTIMATE: regional premiums are also assessed on property points, and the pension is capped at the same KRW 6,590,000 monthly income ceiling (about KRW 79m a year), neither of which is modelled.

Marginal rates shown already include local income tax of 10% of the tax (PwC, reviewed 2 July 2026). personal_allowance is the basic personal deduction of KRW 1.5 million for the taxpayer (PwC deductions page); the employment income deduction (70% of the first KRW 5m, 40% to KRW 15m, 15% to KRW 45m, 5% to KRW 100m, 2% above; capped at KRW 20m; employees only) and the insurance deductions are not included. Employee contributions: National Pension 4.75% on monthly pay up to KRW 6,590,000 (from July 2026; maximum KRW 313,025 a month), health insurance including long-term care about 4.0674% (monthly cap KRW 10,390,220 in total), employment insurance 0.90%. The pension cap means the 9.7174% total overstates contributions at high pay; no single cap is recorded.

## Digital nomad visa

### Workation visa (F-1-D)

One-year visa for people who work remotely for a foreign company, with annual income of twice Korea's prior-year GNI per capita (about KRW 85m per the 2024 consulate page), one year of experience in the same industry, age 18+ and KRW 100m medical and repatriation insurance. Local employment is restricted.

- Treatment: standard
- Minimum income: 85,000,000 KRW a year
- Duration: 2 years. Valid one year, one extension of one year possible in Korea.
- Foreign income required: True; local employment required: False; social security applies: True
- Confidence: low (partially_verified)

## Expat regimes

### 19% flat tax for foreign employees

Foreign employees who start work in Korea no later than 31 December 2026 may elect a flat 19% rate (excluding 10% local tax, so 20.9%) on employment income instead of progressive 6-45%. Electing forfeits other deductions, exemptions and credits; not available when working for a related-party company. Lasts 20 years from first day of work.

- Treatment: flat_rate (flat rate 19%)
- Minimum income: none recorded
- Duration: 20 years. 20 years from the first day of work in Korea through the end of the tax year before the 20-year anniversary. A reform proposal of 3 August 2026 would raise the rate to 21% (23.1% with local tax) and extend the sunset to 31 December 2029 from 1 January 2027; not enacted.
- Foreign income required: False; local employment required: True; social security applies: True
- Confidence: medium (verified)

## Living costs

City: Seoul (Yongsan).

- 1-bedroom: 1,500,000 KRW a month. Typical Yongsan 1-bedroom monthly rent (wolse), Seoulstart neighbourhood data Q2 2026, with a KRW 35m deposit; Numbeo city-centre average KRW 1,187,649 (14 Oct 2026).
- 2-bedroom: 2,300,000 KRW a month. Typical Yongsan 2-bedroom monthly rent, Seoulstart Q2 2026, with a KRW 180m deposit; Numbeo 3-bedroom city-centre average KRW 3,316,866.

Seoulstart figures are 'typical ranges' with no sample size. Monthly rent is paid on top of a large refundable deposit; jeonse (lump-sum deposit) is an alternative.

## Residence routes

EU, US and other citizens: remote workers can use the F-1-D workation visa (foreign employer or own foreign company, income about KRW 85m, 1+1 years). Skilled employees need an employer-sponsored work visa (E-series); specific categories were not verified on an opened official page. The 19% flat tax is open to foreign employees of any nationality who start work by 31 December 2026.

## Open questions

- Official Ministry of Justice / HiKorea pages not opened.
- Current workation income threshold.
- Self-employed rate is a composite of sourced component rates; the exact regional (local subscriber) income-point formula and the property component were not found.
- Employment income deduction and tax credits are not modelled (only the KRW 1.5m basic deduction).
- Pension ceiling KRW 6,590,000 (PwC, July 2026) vs KRW 6,370,000 (secondary guide): PwC used.
- Proposal to move to 21% from 2027 and extend to 2029 was not enacted as of the source.
- Local income tax (10% of the tax) is on top of the 19%.
- Threshold is the 2023 figure (KRW 85m, twice the prior year's GNI per capita) on the consulate page; the current-year GNI-based figure was not found.
- Foreign-source income of a resident visitor: PwC says residents with five years or less in the last ten are taxed on Korea-source income, and foreign-source income only if paid by a Korean entity or transferred to Korea; whether a nomad's pay for work done in Korea counts as Korea-source was not found, so treatment stays standard.
- Earned income deduction (employees; PwC deductions page): KRW 70% of pay to KRW 5m; 3.5m + 40% of the excess over 5m to 15m; 7.5m + 15% over 15m to 45m; 12m + 5% over 45m to 100m; 14.75m + 2% above 100m; capped at KRW 20m. Not captured in the file.
