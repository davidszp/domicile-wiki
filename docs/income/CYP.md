# Cyprus — earned income, nomad and expat dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026

## Summary

Cyprus has a 0% band to EUR 22,000 and a top rate of 35% above EUR 72,000. Employees pay 11.45% (8.8% social insurance to EUR 68,904, 2.65% GHS); the self-employed 20.6%. New employees earning over EUR 55,000 can exempt 50% of pay for 17 years. The nomad visa needs EUR 3,500 net a month. Limassol median asking rents: EUR 1,450 for one bedroom, EUR 2,100 for two.

## Income tax and social security

Marginal rates on taxable income (EUR):

- from 0.00: 0.00%
- from 22,000.00: 20.00%
- from 32,000.00: 25.00%
- from 42,000.00: 30.00%
- from 72,000.00: 35.00%

Personal allowance deducted before the brackets: 0.00 EUR.

Employee social security: 11.45% (cap: 68,904.00). Self-employed: 20.60% (cap: 68,904.00).

From 1 January 2026 resident income tax is 0% to EUR 22,000, 20% to 32,000, 25% to 42,000, 30% to 72,000 and 35% above. Employees pay 8.8% social insurance up to EUR 68,904 and 2.65% GHS (health) up to EUR 180,000; the self-employed pay 16.6% and 4.0%. The recorded combined rates are 11.45% and 20.6% with the social-insurance cap.

Self-employed notes: Social insurance 16.6% (since 2024) plus GHS 4.00% of income (PwC other taxes, opened 2026-10-15). PwC says self-employed contributions are subject to a minimum and a maximum that depend on profession or trade, set annually, but lists no figures; the cap recorded (EUR 68,904) is the 2026 EMPLOYEE ceiling applied by assumption and could not be confirmed (Ministry of Labour pages returned 404). GHS applies to income up to EUR 180,000, so above EUR 68,904 the recorded rates overstate by the GHS part (2.65% employees, 4% self-employed). Business profits are taxed on the normal scale after deductible expenses.

Local tax: No local income tax. The 0% bracket to EUR 22,000 stands in for the tax-free amount. Dividends and interest face Special Defence Contribution only for domiciled residents (domicile after 17 of the last 20 years).

## Digital nomad visa

Non-EU applicants, EUR 3,500 net a month, one year renewable to three, capped at 500 permits. Taxed as resident at normal rates, with the non-dom exemption for dividends and interest.

## Expat regimes

50% employment exemption (17 years, over EUR 55,000, first employment since 2022, 15 years of non-residence); 20% exemption (EUR 8,550 cap, seven years); a 25% exemption (cap EUR 25,000, 2025 to 2030 starters) also exists.

Regimes recorded in the JSON file:

- **Digital nomad visa** (digital_nomad_visa, treatment: standard): Visa for non-EU remote workers with stable income of at least EUR 3,500 a month NET (after taxes and contributions) per Remote Work Europe, valid one year and renewable for up to two further years (three in total), capped at 500 permits. min_income is a gross equivalent: EUR 42,000 net needs about EUR 61,000 gross for a self-employed person (16.6% + 4% contributions) and about EUR 54,700 for an employee, using the 2026 Cyprus scale (our arithmetic). The visa does not change tax: a resident (over 183 days) pays the normal scale on worldwide income, and the non-dom regime exempts dividends and certain interest from Special Defence Contribution. The 100% exemption for remuneration for work exercised OUTSIDE Cyprus for over 90 days a year for a non-resident employer (PwC) does not cover work done in Cyprus. Minimum income: 61,000 EUR. Maximum income: none. Duration (years): 3. One year plus two one-year renewals. Foreign income required: True. Local employment required: False. Covers work done locally: None. Social security still applies: True.
- **50% exemption on first employment in Cyprus** (expat_regime, treatment: partial_exemption 0.5): Half of employment income is exempt for 17 years for people whose first employment in Cyprus starts after 1 January 2022, who were not Cyprus tax resident for at least 15 consecutive years before, and whose remuneration exceeds EUR 55,000 a year. Usable once per person; not combinable with the 20% or 25% exemptions. Minimum income: 55,000 EUR. Maximum income: none. Duration (years): 17.  Foreign income required: False. Local employment required: True. Covers work done locally: None. Social security still applies: True.
- **20% exemption on first employment in Cyprus** (expat_regime, treatment: partial_exemption 0.2): 20% of employment income is exempt, capped at EUR 8,550 a year, for seven years starting the tax year after employment begins. Requires employment starting after 26 July 2022, at least three consecutive non-resident years and prior employment abroad by a non-resident employer. Not combinable with the 50% or 25% exemptions. ## Living costs

Limassol: 1-bedroom EUR 1,450 a month, 2-bedroom EUR 2,100; Nicosia EUR 650 and EUR 900 (Global Property Guide, median asking rents).

## Residence routes

EU citizens register for residence. US and other non-EU remote workers can use the digital nomad visa (EUR 3,500 net a month, up to three years, 500 permits). Skilled employees from outside the EU need an employer-sponsored work permit under the Cyprus Immigration scheme, which was not researched. Cyprus's 50% and 20% employment exemptions apply to any new employee who meets the non-residence tests.

## Open questions

- Employer-sponsored work permit rules and quotas not researched.
- Official Civil Registry and Migration Department page for the nomad visa not opened.
- Rents rest on one aggregator (Global Property Guide); no second source obtained.
- Self-employed maximum insurable earnings for 2026 not found on any official page; the employee ceiling of EUR 68,904 (confirmed for employees on PwC) is assumed. Confidence low for the self-employed cap.
- Digital nomad visa: Official page (gov.cy / Migration Department) returned 403; threshold, net basis, duration and the 500-permit cap rest on Remote Work Europe only.
- Digital nomad visa: The EUR 3,500 is net: the gross equivalent of about EUR 61,000 (self-employed) is our derivation and an applicant is judged on documented net income.
- Digital nomad visa: Sources disagree on renewal (one or two further years); three years in total is used.
- 50% exemption on first employment in Cyprus: Employment starting 1 January 2022 to 29 June 2023 follows a 10-year non-residence test.
- 20% exemption on first employment in Cyprus: Cap on the exempt amount (EUR 8,550 a year) is not representable in the model, so exempt_share overstates the relief above EUR 42,750 of income.
- 20% exemption on first employment in Cyprus: The 25% exemption (cyp_25) is now modelled separately.
- 25% exemption on first employment or business in Cyprus (2025-2030 starters): The EUR 25,000 cap on the exempt amount is not modelled (binds above EUR 100,000 of income); the relief is overstated for the expat persona, though the 50% regime is larger where it applies.
- 25% exemption on first employment or business in Cyprus (2025-2030 starters): Only PwC opened; the Cyprus Tax Department wording was not reached. Whether self-employed digital nomads working for foreign clients count as 'carrying on a business in Cyprus' was not checked.
