# Costa Rica — earned income, nomad and expat dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026

## Summary

Costa Rica taxes Costa Rican-source income only (services rendered in Costa Rica count as local-source, PwC). Employees pay 0% up to CRC 11.0m a year, then 10-25% (top bracket above CRC 56.7m), plus about 10.84% CCSS and other deductions. The Law 10008 visa (USD 3,000 a month, one year renewable once) is described by a consultancy as exempting foreign income; whether that covers work done while living in Costa Rica could not be established, so it is not scored. Rents: 1-bed in San Jose averages CRC 429,540 (Numbeo, 38 contributors); 2-bed median USD 1,500 (Encuentra24, 21 listings).

## Income tax and social security

2026 monthly employment scale (Decree 45333-H via Alegra and Teletica): exempt to CRC 918,000, 10% to 1,347,000, 15% to 2,364,000, 20% to 4,727,000, 25% above. Self-employed annual scale per Deloitte: exempt to CRC 6,244,000, 10% to 8,329,000, 15% to 10,414,000, 20% to 20,872,000, 25% above. Social security: employee 10.84% (Deloitte), self-employed 10.5% to 18.5%, employer 26.33%.

## Digital nomad visa

Law 10008 (Decree 43619): USD 3,000 a month averaged over a year (USD 4,000 with family), up to one year renewable once with 180 days in-country in year one, health insurance with USD 50,000 cover. A consultancy states that income from abroad is exempt from Costa Rican income tax and that the holder is not treated as tax resident. Because PwC defines Costa Rican-source income to include services rendered within the country, whether the exemption reaches pay for work done in Costa Rica depends on the statute's wording, which could not be read; covers_local_work is left unset. Source: consultancy page only.

## Expat regimes

None. Territorial taxation already leaves foreign income untaxed for residents.

## Living costs

1-bed: Numbeo San Jose, city centre CRC 429,540 (range 300,000 to 600,000; 546 entries from 38 contributors). 2-bed: Encuentra24 apartments for rent (64 listings read on 6 results pages, asking rents in USD, furnishing mixed): median USD 1,500 from 21 two-bedroom units in Escazu/Santa Ana/Lindora and central San Jose (range 900 to 4,500). For comparison its 17 one-bed units have a median of USD 1,100.

## Residence routes

Remote workers of any citizenship can use the Law 10008 visa (USD 3,000 a month, up to one year renewable once, USD 50,000 health cover). Employees of a Costa Rican employer need a work permit or residence (not researched here). The earlier repo research (data/jurisdictions/CRI.json) lists income- and investment-based residence categories (rentista, inversionista, pensionado) without verified thresholds. EU, US and other citizens use the same routes. US citizens remain taxable in the US on worldwide income; Costa Rican tax on Costa Rican-source income can be credited only where the US foreign tax credit rules allow.

## Open questions

- Decree 45333-H and Hacienda's own tables were not opened; the 2026 employment scales come from two media/blog pages that agree with each other and with Deloitte's self-employed table. PwC still shows the 2025 self-employed table (exempt to CRC 4,094,000).
- CCSS contribution ceilings and the self-employed tier for a USD 60,000 earner not found (the CCSS site could not be reached); 18.5% is the top of Deloitte's 10.5% to 18.5% range and is assumed to apply at that income.
- covers_local_work not established: PwC defines Costa Rican-source income as including services rendered within Costa Rican territory, so a remote worker's pay for work done in Costa Rica is local-source unless Law 10008 itself exempts it. The consultancy page says Law 10008 gives 'a full exemption from income tax', but the statute text (SINALEVI/Gaceta) could not be opened (script-only site) and Migracion's site returned 403. Left unset, so the regime is shown but not scored.
- The source is an immigration consultancy page, not Migracion or the Gazette. Fees were not listed.
- Whether CCSS contributions are due on a holder's foreign income is not stated.
- A separate source in a search result reported a 180-day stay; this page describes up to one year renewable once.
- Work-permit and rentista thresholds not verified.
- 2-bed rent is a median of asking prices from the first 6 pages (relevance order) of one portal.
- Costa Rica: social_security_deductible = 0 for employees is read from Ley 7092 arts. 32-34 in a consolidated text that carries notes up to 2011-2020 (no Hacienda guidance found); payroll sites disagree (Alegra: gross salary; several calculators: net of CCSS). Self-employed (lucrative activities, Title I) may deduct expenses, but whether their own CCSS payments are deductible was not read.
