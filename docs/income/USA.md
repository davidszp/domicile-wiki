# United States — earned income, nomad and expat dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026

## Summary

US federal income tax runs 10% to 37% (2026 single brackets; standard deduction USD 16,100). New York State (3.9% to 10.9% in 2026) and New York City (3.078% to 3.876%) are stacked on top, giving roughly 41-47% at the 150,000 level. Employees pay 6.2% social security up to USD 184,500 plus 1.45% Medicare; the self-employed pay 15.3% (12.4% + 2.9%). Other states vary widely: nine have no wage income tax (for example Texas and Florida).

- Self-employed notes: Self-employment tax is 15.3% (12.4% social security + 2.9% Medicare) applied to 92.35% of net self-employment earnings (IRS Schedule SE line 4a/5b, opened 2026-10-15), so the rate on net earnings is 15.3% x 0.9235 = 14.13%. The 12.4% part stops at the wage base (USD 184,500 for 2026, i.e. net earnings of about USD 199,783 after the 92.35% factor); the cap field uses that point, but the 2.9% Medicare part is uncapped and a 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax applies above USD 200,000 (single; IRS), neither modelled above the cap. Half of the SE tax is deductible in computing income tax (IRS), not modelled. Self-employed people may deduct the employer-equivalent half of self-employment tax (IRS, in adjusted gross income), so about 0.5 of their contributions reduce taxable income; the employee figure (0) governs the field.
- Local tax note: Brackets are combined federal + New York State + New York City stacked on the FEDERAL taxable income (single, standard deduction USD 16,100). NYS uses a USD 8,000 standard deduction, so NY thresholds were shifted by USD 8,100 (NY taxable = federal taxable + 8,100). NYS 2026 rates for the five lowest brackets are the 2025 rates minus 0.1 point (3.9%, 4.4%, 5.15%, 5.4%, 5.9%). The NYS benefit recapture above USD 107,650 AGI, the NYC school tax credit, state and local tax itemising, FICA non-deductibility and the federal QBI/pass-through rules are not modelled. State variation: for example Texas, Florida, Nevada and Washington have no tax on wage income.

## Residence routes

There is no digital nomad visa. EU and many other nationals can visit on ESTA or B-1/B-2 status but may not work for US clients while there, so a remote worker has no direct immigration route; long stays need a work visa (H-1B, L-1, O-1, TN for Canada/Mexico), an investor visa or family sponsorship, all employer- or relationship-based. US citizens need no immigration route but owe US tax on worldwide income wherever they live. A skilled employee at a US employer typically uses H-1B (annual lottery), L-1 intra-company transfer or O-1.
