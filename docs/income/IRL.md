# Ireland — earned income, nomad and expat dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026

## Summary

Ireland taxes a single person at 20% up to EUR 44,000 and 40% above in 2026; Universal Social Charge (USC) adds 0.5%, 2%, 3% and 8% in bands (to EUR 12,012, 28,700, 70,044 and above), so the combined top marginal rate is 48% (51% on self-employed income over EUR 100,000 with the 3% USC surcharge). Tax credits of EUR 2,000 personal plus EUR 2,000 employee or earned-income credit cut the bill by EUR 4,000. PRSI is 4.2%, rising to 4.35% from 1 October 2026.

No local income tax. USC is folded into the brackets (income tax 20/40% plus USC 0.5/2/3/8% on separate bands: income tax band limit EUR 44,000; USC limits EUR 12,012, 28,700, 70,044). Tax credits (EUR 4,000 for a single employee) are not modelled and lower the tax by EUR 4,000 (about 20,000 of income taxed at 20%). Employee PRSI is the 4.2%/4.35% blended rate; no earnings ceiling was found on the opened page. USC does not apply below EUR 13,000 of income.

- Employee social security: 4.24% (no cap).
- Self-employed social security: 4.24% (no cap).
- Self-employed notes: Class S PRSI 4.2% to September then 4.35% from October 2026 (full-year blended about 4.24%), with a minimum of EUR 650 per income.ie (PwC says EUR 500; sources conflict) when income is EUR 5,000 or more. USC carries an extra 3% on self-employed income above EUR 100,000. The earned income credit of EUR 2,000 replaces the PAYE credit.

Open points: USC reduced rates and the PAYE employee credit amount for employees not opened. PRSI ceiling and minimum contribution conflict between PwC and income.ie.

## Digital nomad visa

### Stamp 0 residence (independent means) - closest to a nomad route

Ireland has no digital nomad visa and none planned. Non-EU remote workers' closest route is Stamp 0 permission as a person of independent means (about EUR 50,000 a year of non-Irish income and about EUR 100,000 in savings, private health insurance, no public funds). It does not explicitly authorise work, including remote work for a foreign employer. Taxation is ordinary residence-based.

- Treatment: standard
- Minimum income: 50,000 EUR; maximum: none stated
- Duration: not stated years. Renewed annually; does not lead to permanent residence.
- Foreign income required: yes; local employment required: no; social security still due: yes

Open points: Figures from a third-party guide; the Irish Immigration Service page was not opened. Social security applies as for any resident self-employed person (assumed).

## Expat regimes

### Special Assignee Relief Programme (SARP)

For employees relocated to Ireland by an employer, 30% of income between the threshold and EUR 1,000,000 is disregarded for income tax. For arrivals from 1 January 2026 the minimum salary is EUR 125,000 (EUR 100,000 for earlier arrivals) and the 30% applies to income above that threshold. The programme was extended to 31 December 2030. The relief lasts five consecutive years; employer certification within 90 days (late filing within 180 days gives four years starting the next year).

- Treatment: partial_exemption, exempt share 30%
- Minimum income: 125,000 EUR; maximum: 1,000,000 EUR
- Duration: 5 years. Five consecutive tax years (four if certified late).
- Foreign income required: no; local employment required: yes; social security still due: yes

Open points: Exempt share applies only to income above EUR 125,000, not to the whole salary. BDO page does not address USC and PRSI; the relief is generally an income tax measure so they still apply (assumed). Revenue SARP page returned 404; conditions such as prior non-residence period not verified. Self-employed people are excluded (employees only).

## Residence routes

EU/EEA citizens and UK citizens (Common Travel Area) can live and work freely. Non-EU skilled employees need an employment permit: Critical Skills (EUR 38,000+ for eligible occupations, EUR 64,000+ for others, per a secondary guide) or General Employment Permit (EUR 34,000+ with a labour-market test). There is no digital nomad visa; visa-exempt visitors (e.g. US) get up to 90 days (visitor, no work authorisation), and Stamp 0 is the long-stay workaround.
