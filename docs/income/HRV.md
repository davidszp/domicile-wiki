# Croatia — earned income, nomad and expat dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026

## Summary

Zagreb taxes income at 23% to EUR 60,000 and 33% above, after a EUR 7,200 personal allowance. Employees pay 20% pension contributions up to EUR 143,496; the self-employed rate (36.5%) is unverified. The digital nomad stay (EUR 3,622.50 a month, up to 18 months) exempts foreign-sourced income from Croatian income tax. Zagreb median asking rents: EUR 600 for one bedroom, EUR 770 for two.

## Income tax and social security

Marginal rates on taxable income (EUR):

- from 0.00: 23.00%
- from 60,000.00: 33.00%

Personal allowance deducted before the brackets: 7,200.00 EUR.

Employee social security: 20.00% (cap: 143,496.00). Self-employed: 36.50% (cap: none stated).

Cities set their own income tax rates. Zagreb applies 23% up to EUR 60,000 of annual taxable income and 33% above (decision NN 28/2025, in force from 1 March 2025; no 2026 change found). The basic personal allowance is EUR 600 a month (EUR 7,200 a year). Employees pay 20% pension contributions (15% + 5%) up to EUR 143,496 a year; employer pays health insurance.

Self-employed notes: UNVERIFIED: 36.5% is the pension (15% first pillar + 5% second pillar) plus health insurance (16.5%) rates as generally stated for the self-employed; no opened page gives the self-employed rates or contribution base. The Tax Administration craftsman page (porezna-uprava.gov.hr) only confirms that craftspeople owe mandatory contributions and shows no rates; PwC's Croatian other-taxes page covers employees only (employee 20%: 15% + 5%, cap EUR 143,496 a year for first-pillar payments; the 16.5% health rate there is an employer contribution). The contribution base for the self-employed (fixed minimum vs actual income) is not established, so 36.5% on all income without a cap may overstate. Sole traders with receipts up to EUR 60,000 and no VAT registration can pay a lump-sum tax assessed by the tax administration (amounts not given).

Local tax: Zagreb has no surtax; each city sets lower and higher rates itself (permitted ranges 15% to 23% and 25% to 33%; Zagreb uses the maximum 23% and 33%). Smaller towns and municipalities can charge less.

## Digital nomad visa

Non-EU applicants, 2.5 times the average net wage, 6 to 18 months, not renewable. Foreign-sourced income is exempt from income tax according to the one source opened.

## Expat regimes

No expat or impatriate regime found.

Regimes recorded in the JSON file:

- **Digital nomad temporary stay** (digital_nomad_visa, treatment: exempt): Temporary stay for digital nomads: a non-EU national employed by, or working through communication technology for, a company (or own company) not registered in Croatia and not serving employers in Croatia (Zakon o strancima art. 3 definition, art. 57 point 11), valid up to 18 months and extendable once within that period (art. 61), with a six-month wait before a new application (art. 57(4)). Remote Work Europe gives the income test as 2.5 times the average net monthly wage (EUR 3,622.50 a month in 2026, EUR 43,470 a year, or savings of the same amount); the Ordinance (Pravilnik) with the amount was not opened. Tax: Income Tax Act art. 9(1) point 26 says income tax is not paid on 'receipts of natural persons earned from dependent work or activity for an employer not registered in the Republic of Croatia on the basis of acquired digital nomad status under a special regulation'. This is a statutory exemption of the holder's remote-work pay regardless of where it is performed, so it covers work done while living in Croatia (covers_local_work true). The statute speaks of an 'employer'; whether freelance fees from foreign clients count equally is assumed from the definition of a digital nomad (own company or services for non-Croatian firms), not tested. Remote Work Europe adds that holders are treated as non-residents for tax beyond 183 days; not verified from the statute. Minimum income: 43,470 EUR. Maximum income: none. Duration (years): 1.5. Up to 18 months, no renewal; six-month cooling-off period. Foreign income required: True. Local employment required: False. Covers work done locally: True. Social security still applies: False.

## Living costs

Zagreb: 1-bedroom EUR 600 a month, 2-bedroom EUR 770 (Global Property Guide Q2 2026, median asking rents, all locations).

## Residence routes

EU citizens register their stay with no permit. US and other non-EU remote workers can use the digital nomad stay (about EUR 3,622.50 a month, up to 18 months, no route to permanent residence). Skilled employees from outside the EU need an employer-sponsored residence and work permit or an EU Blue Card, which were not researched. Croatia has no expat tax regime.

## Open questions

- No expat or impatriate tax regime was found; the PwC pages opened do not list one.
- Rents rest on one aggregator (Global Property Guide); no second source obtained.
- Self-employed contribution rates and base for 2026 not found on any opened page; 36.5% is carried from general statements and is unverified (confidence low for the self-employed figure).
- Whether Zagreb changed its rates for 2026 was not checked beyond the 2025 decision (zakon.hr database, updated July 2026, still shows 23% and 33%).
- The EUR 60,000 threshold between the two rates is from the PwC page; the Zagreb decision itself does not state it.
- Digital nomad temporary stay: Income threshold (2.5x average net wage) rests on Remote Work Europe; the Pravilnik on the stay of third-country nationals and mup.gov.hr pages were not reachable.
- Digital nomad temporary stay: Art. 9(1)(26) names income 'for an employer'; coverage of self-employed fees from foreign clients is inferred from the definition in the Foreigners Act, not stated.
- Digital nomad temporary stay: Social security: not addressed by the sources; recorded as not applying, unverified. Zakon.hr is a consolidated-text database, not the official Narodne novine.
- Digital nomad temporary stay: Other sources quote older thresholds (EUR 3,295 a month).
