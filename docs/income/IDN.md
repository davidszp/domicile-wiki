# Indonesia — earned income, nomad and expat dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026

## Summary

Residents pay 5% to 35% on taxable income after IDR 54 million personal relief and a 5% occupational expense deduction (max IDR 6 million a year). Residents are taxed on worldwide income; only certified foreign experts get a four-year territorial treatment. Employees pay about 4% to BPJS.

## Income tax and social security

Rates apply to taxable income in IDR; allowance 54,000,000.

| From | Marginal rate |
|---|---|
| 0 | 5% |
| 60,000,000 | 15% |
| 250,000,000 | 25% |
| 500,000,000 | 30% |
| 5,000,000,000 | 35% |

- Employee social security: 4%, annual income cap: none.
- Self-employed social security: 0%, cap: none.
- Notes: Employee shares (PwC Indonesia other-taxes page: old-age savings 2%, pension 1%, BPJS Kesehatan 1%; employer-only: work accident, death, unemployment): BPJS Kesehatan 1% (calculation base capped at IDR 12 million a month), old-age savings 2%, pension 1% (capped, figure not on page). Because the health cap is monthly while the other bases are not capped on the page, the blended 4% overstates burden at high incomes. Self-employed contributions were not covered on the PwC page; whether foreigners without an Indonesian employer must enrol was not verified.
- Local tax: No national local income tax. Personal relief for a single taxpayer is IDR 54 million, plus IDR 4.5 million for a spouse and each dependant (up to three). Bali has no extra income tax.

## Digital nomad visa

### Remote Worker Visa (E33G)

One-year ITAS for remote workers with a contract with a company established outside Indonesia, income of about USD 60,000 a year and USD 2,000 in liquid funds. Renewable once (two years maximum). It gives no tax exemption: holders become tax residents early (permit over 183 days counts as intent to reside under PER-23/PJ/2025) and are taxed on worldwide income. The Bali immigration office (Kantor Imigrasi Ngurah Rai) press release of 28 September 2026 on the immigration site shows an 'ITAS Remote Worker' exists in practice.

- Treatment: standard
- Covers work performed locally: not established
- Minimum income: USD 60,000; duration: 2 years. Initial 1-year ITAS, one renewal. Earning from Indonesian entities or customers is prohibited. Fee reported around IDR 7,000,000 but inconsistent.
- Foreign income required: True; local employment required: False; social security applies: False
- Confidence: low (partially_verified)

## Expat regimes

### Four-year territorial treatment for certified foreign experts (PP 55/2022)

Under the Omnibus Law a foreigner who becomes tax resident and meets skill requirements is taxed only on Indonesian-source income (including income paid offshore) for the first four years of residence. A secondary source says it is limited to certified specified expertise in science, technology or mathematics with at least five years of experience and a knowledge-transfer obligation, so ordinary employees and remote workers usually do not qualify. Not available where a tax treaty is used. Indonesian-source income, including pay for work done in Indonesia, stays taxable; only income from outside Indonesia is relieved.

- Treatment: exempt
- Minimum income: not stated; duration: 4 years. First four years of tax residence.
- Foreign income required: False; local employment required: True; social security applies: True
- Confidence: low (partially_verified)

## Living costs

City: Bali (Canggu).

- 1-bedroom rent: IDR 17,125,000 a month (2026). Midpoint of IDR 16.25-18 million summary range for 1-bedroom villas in Canggu on Bali Home Immo; listing cards on the same page ranged IDR 16.9-49 million, so this is a lower-end figure (198 monthly villa listings on page, 30 shown).
- 2-bedroom rent: IDR 21,500,000 a month (2026). Midpoint of IDR 18-25 million summary range for 2-bedroom villas in Canggu on Bali Home Immo; the 9 visible 2-bedroom listing cards ranged IDR 25-62.5 million, so true asking prices are higher.

Denpasar Numbeo (1-bedroom city centre IDR 6.39 million, 17 contributors) is much cheaper but not where nomads live. Jakarta not researched.

## Residence routes

EU, US and other citizens: remote workers can apply for the E33G Remote Worker Visa online (about USD 60,000 a year from non-Indonesian sources, two years maximum); the older B211A visit visa does not allow work. Skilled employees need an Indonesian sponsor for a work KITAS (RPTKA and work permit; not researched here).

## Open questions

- E33G rules need confirmation from the official immigration portal.
- Rents are agency listing ranges, not a market report.
- Self-employed BPJS obligations for foreigners unverified (PwC page silent).
- Pension calculation-base cap not on the PwC page; old-age base shown as uncapped, so the blended 4% overstates burden at high incomes.
- Whether foreigners are covered by BPJS is not stated on the PwC page.
- Remote Worker Visa (E33G): Income threshold (USD 60,000), USD 2,000 funds, duration and fee remain from a secondary source (countrytaxcalc); evisa.imigrasi.go.id and the immigration regulation text could not be read.
- Remote Worker Visa (E33G): PER-23/PJ/2025 treatment (ITAS over 183 days = intent to reside) comes from the same secondary source.
- Four-year territorial treatment for certified foreign experts (PP 55/2022): Eligibility criteria (skill certification) not confirmed against PP 55/2022 itself; 'exempt' means foreign-source income is outside Indonesian tax, Indonesian-source income is taxed normally.
