# Mexico — earned income, nomad and expat dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026

## Summary

Mexican tax residents pay ISR on worldwide income at progressive rates from 1.92% to 35% (35% above MXN 5,107,703.93 in 2026). Employees owe IMSS social security (employee share capped at MXN 29,103 a year). Self-employed professionals may opt into RESICO (1.00-2.50% on cash receipts up to MXN 3.5 million) instead of the ordinary scale. Mexico City levies no local personal income tax.

- Employee social security: 2.72%, annual cap 1,070,155.00 MXN.
- Self-employed social security: 0%, annual cap none.
- Self-employed notes: RESICO (art. 113-E LISR): tax on cash receipts invoiced by CFDI with no deductions: 1.00% up to MXN 25,000/month, 1.10% to 50,000, 1.50% to 83,333.33, 2.00% to 208,333.33, 2.50% up to the MXN 3.5 million annual limit (a third-party source cites an inflation-updated limit of MXN 3,652,000; the 2027 budget proposes 5 million). Above the limit or if excluded, ordinary rates apply and the income is business/professional fees. Independent professionals (honorarios) have no mandatory IMSS contribution: since the IMSS Technical Council rules published in the DOF on 28 May 2024 they may enrol voluntarily in the 'Seguro para Trabajadores Independientes' (open to Mexicans and foreigners resident in Mexico), with no single fee: contributions are computed from the declared occupation and insured days and paid monthly to annually (an annual figure of MXN 20,538 from 1 Feb 2026 is quoted for some modalities, e.g. modalidad 44). The 0% self-employed rate recorded here therefore means 'no mandatory contribution'; voluntary enrolment is an optional extra cost not modelled. PwC's Mexico 'Other taxes' page does not mention independent workers.
- Local tax note: No municipal or state personal income tax in Mexico City; state payroll taxes fall on employers.
- Confidence: medium; verification: partially_verified.

## Digital nomad visa

**Temporary Resident visa by economic solvency (de facto remote-worker route)**. Mexico has no dedicated digital nomad visa. Remote workers use the Temporary Resident visa on economic solvency, which does not require an employer and does not change tax rules. Consulates test income or savings against the UMA (117.31 MXN in 2026); amounts vary by consulate. Tax residency is decided by permanent home / centre of vital interests, not a day count.

- Minimum income: 957,250 MXN a year.
- Duration: 4 years. Single-entry visa valid 180 days, exchanged for a card valid one year, renewable annually to four years in total, then permanent residence.
- Tax treatment: standard; social security applies: True.

## Expat regimes

**RESICO (Regimen Simplificado de Confianza) for self-employed individuals** (other). Simplified regime for resident individuals with business or professional income up to MXN 3.5 million a year: tax of 1.00% to 2.50% on cash receipts invoiced by CFDI, with no deductions. Requires Mexican tax residency and SAT registration.

- Treatment: flat_rate, flat rate 2.5%; duration: open-ended years. Open-ended while the income limit and conditions are met.
- Maximum income: 3,500,000 MXN.

## Residence routes

EU and other passport holders (including US) may enter visa-free as visitors but cannot work for Mexican entities; remote workers with foreign income typically apply at a consulate for a Temporary Resident visa on economic solvency (income or savings test), exchanged for a card within 30 days of entry, valid one year and renewable to four, then permanent residence. A skilled employee of a Mexican employer needs a work permit (employer-sponsored) via the INM; US, EU and other nationalities follow the same visa categories, with differences only in whether a consular visa is needed to enter. There is no EU free-movement route and no special regime for US citizens other than ordinary rules.

- IMSS applicability to a temporary resident without Mexican employer is unclear.
- flat_rate records the top RESICO rate (2.50%); lower receipts are taxed at 1.00%-2.00%.
- Not confirmed from SAT or legislation: relies on a third-party guide.
