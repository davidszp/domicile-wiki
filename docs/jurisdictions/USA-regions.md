# United States (states and DC) — family-office tax dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026
Reviewer: research agent (Claude), unreviewed by a tax adviser

## Summary

States and DC. Overrides give COMBINED federal + state figures because each overriding metric replaces the national one: capital gains and dividends = federal 20% + 3.8% NIIT + state top rate (local city/county income taxes excluded and described in text); inheritance = federal estate tax (40% above USD 15 million, state death tax deductible federally) combined with the state estate or inheritance tax as one best-faith marginal bracket list for an estate passing to children. States with no income tax and no death tax have no overrides. No state levies a wealth tax. Federal figures follow data/jurisdictions/USA.json (tax year 2026).

No-income-tax states (AK, FL, NV, NH, SD, TN, TX, WY) without death taxes carry no overrides; Washington taxes only long-term capital gains on intangibles (7% / 9.9%). The highest combined rates on gains are California (37.1%), New York State (34.7%, 38.576% in New York City), New Jersey and DC (34.55%), Minnesota (34.65%), Oregon (33.7%). Fifteen jurisdictions add a death tax that reaches children: estate taxes in CT, HI, IL, ME, MD, MA, MN, NY, OR, RI, VT, WA and DC; inheritance taxes that reach children in PA (4.5%) and NE (1% above USD 100,000 per child). Kentucky, New Jersey and Maryland inheritance taxes exempt children; Iowa repealed its inheritance tax from 2025.

## Residence and eligibility

State residence follows domicile (permanent home, intent to remain) and statutory residence tests (commonly a permanent place of abode plus more than 183 days, e.g. New York). High-tax states audit departures; keep evidence of a completed move. Immigration is federal (see USA.md).

## Inheritance and gifts to children

Federal estate tax: 40% above USD 15,000,000 per person (2026, OBBBA, indexed from 2027; IRS). State death taxes are deductible for federal estate tax (IRC 2058), so above the federal threshold the combined marginal rate is 40% + 60% x state marginal rate. The JSON bracket lists combine both layers for an estate passing to children; `exemption` is the state exemption and `from_amount` is measured above it. They assume a single decedent, no lifetime gifts and no other deductions.

### Connecticut

Connecticut estate tax: 12% flat on the Connecticut taxable estate above the exemption, which equals the federal basic exclusion (USD 15,000,000 for 2026); total Connecticut tax capped at USD 15 million. Combined with the federal estate tax (40% above USD 15,000,000; state death taxes are deductible federally under IRC 2058), the brackets are the combined marginal rates for an estate passing entirely to children: federal tax starts once the estate net of state tax exceeds USD 15 million (about USD 15.00 million gross). Illustrative combined tax for an unmarried decedent: 11.8% of a USD 20 million estate, 33.0% of USD 50 million.

Combined marginal brackets (amount above the exemption -> rate): USD 0 -> 47.2%

Gifts: Connecticut is the only state with a gift tax: lifetime taxable gifts above the annual exclusion are taxed at 12% once cumulative gifts exceed the USD 15 million exemption (unified with the estate tax). Federal gift tax also applies.

### Washington

### Kentucky

Kentucky inheritance tax exempts Class A beneficiaries (spouse, children, grandchildren, parents, siblings), so it does not reach a transfer to children; no Kentucky estate tax.

### New Jersey

New Jersey's estate tax was repealed for deaths from 1 January 2018; its inheritance tax exempts Class A beneficiaries (spouse, children, grandchildren, parents), so transfers to children bear only the federal estate tax.

### Iowa

Iowa's inheritance tax was fully repealed for deaths on or after 1 January 2025 (it already exempted lineal heirs); no estate tax.

## Wealth tax

No state or DC levies an annual net wealth tax in 2026; no wealth_tax overrides. Proposals (e.g. Washington, California) are not in force. Local property taxes are ordinary municipal taxes.

## Special regimes

None at state level. Puerto Rico Act 60 is in PRI.json.

## Company taxation (kept separate from personal tax)

State corporate income taxes (0% to about 11.5%) apply on top of federal 21% CIT; Nevada, South Dakota, Wyoming and Texas (franchise/margin tax instead) have no corporate income tax; Washington has B&O gross receipts tax. Not researched in detail.
