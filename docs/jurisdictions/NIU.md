# Niue — family-office tax dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026
Reviewer: research agent (Claude), unreviewed by a tax adviser

## Summary

Residents pay income tax on worldwide income at 10%, 20% and 30% (above NZD 20,000). There is no capital gains tax outside dealing. Dividends are exempt income under the Income Tax Act 1961, though they count when setting the rate on other income. The Tax Office's return form lists dividends as income, so this is low confidence. No wealth, estate or gift tax was found.

## Residence and eligibility

Resident if domiciled in Niue at any time in the year, or present 183 days in any 12-month period beginning or ending in the year (Income Tax Act 1961 s.2). Part-year rules for arrival and departure years.

Routes:

- Niue immigration permit (EU, US, OTHER): Niue controls its own immigration (Ministry of Finance immigration office). Long-term residence requires a permit (details not researched).

**US citizens:** No US treaty. US citizens pay US federal tax on worldwide income; US estate tax applies.

## Capital gains

No capital gains tax. Gains are income only if the taxpayer deals in the property, acquired it for resale, or made them in a profit-making scheme (Income Tax Act 1961, assessable income provisions).

**Rate basis:** Income Tax Act 1961: profits from the sale of property are assessable only when the business is dealing, the property was acquired for resale, or under a profit-making scheme. An investor's gains are therefore scored at 0.

**Professional trader:** Dealing in securities makes gains ordinary income at up to 30%.

**Foreign assets:** Same rule for foreign assets.

Confidence: medium; verification: partially_verified.

## Dividends

Income Tax Act 1961 s.49(1)(d) exempts 'dividends and other profits derived from shares… in companies other than companies which are exempt from income tax'. Under s.40 they still count when setting the rate on other assessable income. The current TF1 return nevertheless asks for dividends as income.

**Rate basis:** Statutory exemption, s.49(1)(d); s.40(2)(c) refers to dividends 'derived by an absentee from sources out of Niue', which implies foreign dividends are within the exemption too. Scored at 0. Low confidence because the Tax Office's TF1 form lists 'DIVIDENDS' among income and the exemption's application to foreign companies is not explicit.

**Withholding outbound:** Not researched.

**Treaty network:** No comprehensive tax treaties (s.85 allows agreements with other countries).

Confidence: low; verification: partially_verified.

## Wealth tax

No net wealth tax. The Niue Tax Office administers income tax, consumption tax (NCT), business licensing and company registration.

**Property tax note:** No property tax found; most land is customary land.

Confidence: medium; verification: partially_verified.

JSON: type 'none'.

## Company taxation (kept separate from personal tax)
