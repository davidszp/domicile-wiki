## Tax on investments, inheritance and wealth

Brunei has no personal income tax, so there is no tax on capital gains, dividends or interest for individuals. There is also no inheritance, gift or wealth tax and no VAT. Companies pay 18.5%. Brunei has no residence-by-investment programme, foreigners generally cannot own land, and Sharia criminal law applies alongside common law.

- **Brunei resident**: No personal income tax, capital gains, dividend, inheritance, gift or wealth tax.

## Inheritance law and trusts

For Muslims, who are most residents, Islamic law fixes the heirs' shares and a will can dispose of at most one third. Non-Muslims are believed to have freedom of testation. Brunei has trust law, including an international trusts order, but I found no foundation statute and the Hague Trusts Convention is not in force.

#### Summary

Brunei has no personal income tax, so there is no tax on capital gains, dividends or interest for individuals. There is also no inheritance, gift or wealth tax and no VAT. Companies pay 18.5%. Brunei has no residence-by-investment programme, foreigners generally cannot own land, and Sharia criminal law applies alongside common law.

US citizens: US citizens remain fully US-taxed; Brunei levies nothing on individuals, so the US layer is the full cost.

#### Residence and eligibility

Not relevant to individuals (no personal income tax). The Income Tax Act defines a resident individual as one residing in Brunei, including a person present and employed 183 days or more in the preceding year.

- **Employment pass, dependant pass, permanent residence** (EU, US, OTHER): No residence-by-investment programme was found; residence is tied to employment or family, and permanent residence is discretionary (not researched in detail).
