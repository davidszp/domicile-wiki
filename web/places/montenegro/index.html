Tax on investments, inheritance and wealth

Montenegro (euro, non-EU) taxes residents' worldwide capital gains at a flat 15% (no municipal surtax on gains) and dividends at 15% plus a municipal surtax of up to 15% of the tax (17.25% in Podgorica). No wealth tax, and no inheritance or gift tax for children (only real estate transfer tax for other heirs). No special tax regime for wealthy arrivals.

  • Ordinary tax residence: Ordinary residence; worldwide income taxed.
  • Family holding company (d.o.o.): The family holds the portfolio through a Montenegrin d.o.o. owned by the parents. The company pays progressive profit tax (9% to EUR 100,000, 12% to EUR 1.5m, 15% above) on foreign dividends and on realised gains alike: the dividend exclusion in Art. 9 covers only payers that are themselves Montenegrin profit-tax payers, and there is no relief for gains on shares. Foreign tax is credited up to the Montenegrin tax on that profit (Art. 33). Money taken out is taxed again as a dividend at 15% plus the municipal surtax. Wealth tax (none) and inheritance (none on movables) are the same as for direct holding, so those metrics are inherited from the standard regime. The company is rarely better than personal holding; it is shown because it is the ordinary vehicle for holding assets in a company.

Income tax for employees and remote workers

Salaries are taxed at 0% up to EUR 700 a month, 9% from EUR 700 to 1,000 and 15% above (EUR 8,400 and 12,000 a year); entrepreneurial income uses the same annual thresholds (EUR 8,400 and 12,000) from 1 January 2026. A municipal surtax of 15% of the tax applies in Podgorica (13% in most other towns), giving effective marginal rates of 10.35% and 17.25%. Employees pay 10% pension contributions (capped, EUR 75,453 in 2025) and 0.5% unemployment insurance.

How to get residence

Montenegro is not in the EU: EU citizens, like everyone else, need a temporary residence permit for stays beyond the visa-free period. Common routes are owning property (a minimum assessed value of EUR 150,000 for non-EU citizens from 2026; EU citizens are exempt from the value test), a remote-work permit, running a company, or employment. The citizenship-by-investment programme ended on 31 December 2022 and has not reopened.

Inheritance law and trusts

Descendants and the spouse are guaranteed half of their intestate share, with a smaller share for other close relatives. Montenegro has no trust law and is outside the Hague Trusts Convention. Foundations are limited to non-profit use.

Montenegro — family-office tax dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-16 Tax year: 2026 Reviewer: research agent (Claude), unreviewed by a tax adviser

Summary

Montenegro (euro, non-EU) taxes residents' worldwide capital gains at a flat 15% (no municipal surtax on gains) and dividends at 15% plus a municipal surtax of up to 15% of the tax (17.25% in Podgorica). No wealth tax, and no inheritance or gift tax for children (only real estate transfer tax for other heirs). No special tax regime for wealthy arrivals.

Residence and eligibility

Resident if domicile or centre of personal and economic interests in Montenegro, or present at least 183 days in a tax year; treaty tie-breakers prevail.

  • Temporary residence permit (e.g. real estate ownership, business, family): Montenegro is not in the EU; all foreigners including EU citizens need a residence permit. Grounds and conditions (including the property-ownership ground) not verified for 2026. The citizenship-by-investment programme closed at the end of 2022 (not re-verified).

Capital gains

Resident individuals pay a flat 15% personal income tax on capital gains from shares, securities and real estate, worldwide. Gain = sale price minus acquisition price. No holding-period exemption for securities. The municipal surtax (prirez) is not charged on capital-gains tax: the Law on Financing Local Self-Government (Art. 8(3)) levies it only on the tax on personal earnings, self-employment income, income from property and property rights, and capital income (dividends, interest).

Exemptions: Exempt: sale of the taxpayer's only and main residence; transfers between spouses linked to marriage, divorce or inheritance; gifts to first-order relatives (spouse, children). Capital losses can be set against gains of the same year.

Exceptions: Since Sl. list CG 152/22 (in force 30 Dec 2022) capital, capital-gains and property income is taxed at 15% (previously 9%). The tax authority can substitute market value when shares in a company are sold below market value.

Holding period: No holding-period exemption for shares or real estate (other than the main-residence exemption).

Foreign assets: Worldwide taxation for residents with a foreign tax credit capped at Montenegrin tax; treaties prevail.

Professional trader: Frequent trading could be treated as self-employment (entrepreneurial) income, taxed at progressive 0/9/15% plus social contributions; no specific guidance found.

JSON numbers: headline 0.15; all asset-class rates 0.15. Flat 15% PIT rate on net capital gains (law amended by Sl. list CG 152/22). No municipal surtax: Art. 8(3) of the local-finance law does not list tax on capital gains in the surtax base.

Regional: PIT is national. The municipal surtax does not apply to capital gains (Law on Financing Local Self-Government, Sl. list CG 3/2019, Art. 8(3) lists the taxes it is levied on, and capital gains are not among them).

Dividends

Dividends received by resident individuals are capital income taxed at a flat 15% on the gross amount, withheld by a Montenegrin payer; foreign dividends are self-assessed at 15% with a credit for foreign tax up to the Montenegrin tax. The municipal surtax applies on top, because Art. 8(3) of the Law on Financing Local Self-Government includes the tax on capital income in its base: up to 15% of the tax in Podgorica and Cetinje (15% x 1.15 = 17.25%), up to 13% elsewhere (16.95%).

Exemptions: Interest on bonds of the State, the Central Bank or local governments is not taxable capital income (from 19 Nov 2025, Sl. list CG 133/25).

Exceptions: Pre-2023 rate was 9%; some secondary sources still quote 9%.

JSON numbers: domestic 0.1725, foreign gross 0.1725, relief by credit. 15% PIT on gross dividends (PwC, reviewed 7 Aug 2026) plus the Podgorica municipal surtax of 15% of the tax (local-finance law Art. 8): 0.15 x 1.15 = 0.1725.

Regional: Municipal surtax on the tax on capital income, set by each municipality: up to 15% in Podgorica (Glavni grad) and Cetinje (Prijestonica), up to 13% elsewhere (Art. 8(1)–(2)). Effective 17.25% in Podgorica; the Tivat and Budva rates were not checked (at most 16.95%).

Outbound withholding: 15% WHT on dividends paid to residents and non-residents (30% to entities in listed tax havens), reducible by treaty.

Treaties: 47 treaties per PwC, including most EU states, UK, Switzerland, UAE, China; no treaty with the United States.

Inheritance and gifts to children

There is no inheritance or gift tax on money, securities or other movables. Inherited or gifted real estate is subject to real estate transfer tax (3%/5%/6% progressive), but first-order heirs (children, spouse) and the deceased's parents, and first-order donees and the donor's spouse, are exempt.

Exemptions: Art. 14(1)(1) Law on Real Estate Transfer Tax: no tax for the first-order heir, spouse and parent of the deceased, or a first-order donee and the donor's spouse.

Exceptions: Other heirs pay real estate transfer tax: 3% up to EUR 150,000, 5% EUR 150,000-500,000, 6% above (since 1 Jan 2024). Gifts to first-order relatives are also exempt from capital gains tax.

Gifts: No gift tax on movables; real estate gifts follow real estate transfer tax with the same first-order exemption.

JSON: basis beneficiary, children fully_exempt, exemption 0, brackets none (children untaxed), other transfer cost 0.

  • Surviving spouse (spouse_treatment fully_exempt): Spouse is exempt (Art. 14(1)(1)), and movables such as securities are outside the tax.

Wealth tax

Montenegro has no net wealth tax and no recurring levy on financial assets.

Property tax (not a wealth tax): Annual real estate tax at municipal rates of 0.25%-1% of market value (PwC). A 2025 amendment to the real estate tax law was enacted; its detail was not reviewed.

Special regimes

None. No special tax regime for newly arriving wealthy individuals was found; only the standard regime is recorded.

Structure regime holding_doo (family d.o.o.), added 2026-10-16 for the 60-year projection: gains and foreign dividends at the 15% top rate (progressive bands below EUR 1.5m), domestic dividends excluded, payout to the family 17.25% (15% + Podgorica surtax assumed), wealth tax and inheritance inherited from the standard regime. Foundation and trust were not added: no family-wealth foundation or trust vehicle in common use was found (not researched against a statute).

Company taxation (kept separate from personal tax)

CIT rate in JSON: 0.15 (top rate). Progressive (Art. 28 of the profit tax law, consolidated text to Sl. list CG 28/23): 9% up to EUR 100,000, EUR 9,000 + 12% to EUR 1.5m, EUR 177,000 + 15% above. No municipal CIT. Dividends are excluded from the base only when the payer is a taxpayer under the same law (Art. 9; PwC), so foreign dividends are taxable. Gains on shares are taxable profit (Arts 21-22); the older 2-year exemption and reinvestment relief for securities are not in the current text. Foreign profit tax is credited up to the Montenegrin tax on that profit (Art. 33). 15% WHT on dividends paid out (30% to tax havens).

Open questions

  • Current consolidated text of the PIT law after Sl. list CG 152/22 and later amendments could not be opened; the 15% rate relies on PwC (reviewed Aug 2026) and Unija/ePorezi summaries.
  • No exit tax for individuals was found, but this was not confirmed against legislation.
  • The surtax conclusion rests on the 2019 text of the local-finance law (Paragraf, last updated 2021); later amendments were not checked.
  • Surtax rates actually set by Tivat and Budva not checked; the JSON uses the Podgorica maximum.
  • Whether the surtax is collected on self-assessed foreign dividends in practice (the law applies it to the tax on capital income generally).
  • Mechanics of self-assessment for foreign dividends received through a foreign broker.
  • Exemption article taken from the 2013 consolidated text; a 2026 secondary source confirms it is unchanged, but the current official consolidated text was not opened.
  • Montenegrin real estate held by a foreign estate and probate/court fees were not quantified.
  • Residence permit routes for 2026 not verified.
  • Surtax rates in Tivat/Budva not checked.
  • Structure holding_doo: the profit tax text read is the consolidated version to Sl. list CG 28/23 (via a law-catalogue PDF); later amendments not checked. Whether the municipal surtax applies to a passive shareholder (Art. 8(1) local finance law refers to persons carrying on an activity) and whether treaty withholding is creditable under Art. 33 are open.
