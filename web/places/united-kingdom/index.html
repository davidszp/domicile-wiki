Tax on investments, inheritance and wealth

High-tax for a long-term resident: 24% CGT, 39.35% top dividend rate (from April 2026), 40% inheritance tax on worldwide assets after 10 years of residence, no wealth tax. New arrivals get a 4-year exemption for foreign income and gains (FIG regime) and stay outside IHT on foreign assets for about 10 years. No investor visa route. A family investment company pays 25% on gains but nothing on most dividends, with dividend tax (up to 39.35%) or tax-free loan repayments on extraction, and lets future growth pass to the children outside IHT.

  • Ordinary UK tax residence: Arising-basis taxation of worldwide income and gains for UK residents under the Statutory Residence Test. The remittance basis and domicile concept were abolished from 6 April 2025.
  • 4-year foreign income and gains (FIG) regime: Replaced the remittance basis from 6 April 2025. New arrivals who were non-UK resident for at least 10 consecutive tax years can claim, in each of their first 4 tax years of UK residence, that foreign income and gains are not taxed in the UK, whether or not brought into the UK. Claims cost the personal allowance and CGT annual exempt amount.
  • Family investment company (FIC, private limited company): The parents fund a UK private company, usually with a loan plus a small subscription, and the company holds the portfolio. It pays 25% corporation tax on gains (one point more than personal CGT at 24%) and on interest, but most dividends it receives are exempt, against 39.35% personally. Money comes out as dividends at 10.75% / 35.75% / 39.35%, or tax-free as repayment of the parents' loan. The main reason families use a FIC is inheritance tax: children hold growth or non-voting 'alphabet' shares, so future growth accrues outside the parents' estates while the parents keep control. IHT itself (40%) is unchanged on whatever the parents still own; the inheritance metric is inherited from the standard regime. For a newly arrived family the 4-year FIG regime and the 10-year IHT exclusion of foreign assets are usually better in the early years: FIC shares are UK-situs assets and are within IHT from day one.

Income tax for employees and remote workers

For 2026/27 (England, Wales and Northern Ireland) UK income tax is 0% up to a GBP 12,570 personal allowance, 20% to GBP 50,270, 40% to GBP 125,140 and 45% above, with an effective 60% rate between GBP 100,000 and GBP 125,140 as the allowance is withdrawn. Employee National Insurance is 8% between GBP 12,570 and GBP 50,270 and 2% above; self-employed Class 4 is 6% and 2%. Scotland has its own rates (not modelled).

How to get residence

EU free movement ended on 31 December 2020: EU citizens now need a visa like everyone else, except those who joined the EU Settlement Scheme (deadline 30 June 2021) and Irish citizens, who live in the UK freely under the Common Travel Area. There is no investor visa (Tier 1 Investor closed on 17 February 2022) and no digital nomad visa. The routes open to most people are Innovator Founder (an endorsed business, settlement after 3 years), Global Talent (endorsed leaders in research, arts and digital technology) and Skilled Worker (a sponsored job paying at least GBP 41,700).

Citizenship: British citizenship usually after 5 years of residence, with indefinite leave to remain (ILR) held for 12 months first. A government plan to move the settlement baseline to 10 years ('earned settlement') was consulted on in late 2025 and was not in the Immigration Rules as of mid-2026; check before relying on 5 years. A higher English standard (B2) for several settlement routes is announced for 26 March 2027.

Inheritance law and trusts

England and Wales and Northern Ireland have no forced heirship, but a court can order provision for close family. Scotland protects spouse and children with legal rights over movable estate. Trusts are fully recognised; there is no private foundation.

Research notes

United Kingdom — family-office tax dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15 Tax year: 2026 Reviewer: research agent (Claude), unreviewed by a tax adviser

Summary

Tax year 2026/27 (6 April 2026 - 5 April 2027). For a long-term resident wealthy family the UK is high-tax: CGT 24%, dividends up to 39.35%, inheritance tax 40% on the worldwide estate above £325,000, no annual wealth tax. Two features soften this for new arrivals: the 4-year foreign income and gains (FIG) regime (foreign income and gains untaxed in years 1-4) and the residence-based IHT test (non-UK assets outside IHT until UK resident in 10 of the previous 20 tax years). There is no passive investor visa.

Residence and eligibility

  • Statutory Residence Test, Finance Act 2013 Schedule 45. Automatic overseas tests: fewer than 16 UK days (46 if non-resident in each of the previous 3 years), or full-time work abroad with fewer than 91 days. Automatic UK tests: 183+ days, or only home in the UK (91+ consecutive days, used 30+ days in the year). Otherwise the sufficient-ties test (family, accommodation, work, 90-day and country ties against day counts).
  • Immigration: Tier 1 Investor visa closed in 2022, no replacement. Innovator Founder visa needs an endorsed business plan (£1,000 endorsement, check-points at 12 and 24 months), dependants allowed, settlement after 3 years. Since Brexit EU citizens need a visa (Irish citizens excepted). An invitation-only investor visa was reported in the press in 2026 but is not announced and is not recorded.

Capital gains

  • Rates from 6 April 2025, unchanged for 2026/27: 18% within the basic-rate band, 24% above, same for shares and residential property. JSON: all asset classes 0.24, headline 0.24.
  • Annual exempt amount £3,000. Business Asset Disposal Relief and Investors' Relief rise from 14% to 18% on 6 April 2026 (£1m lifetime limit each). Carried interest moves into income tax from 6 April 2026.
  • Arising basis on worldwide gains; remittance basis abolished from 6 April 2025.
  • Gifts are disposals at market value (no CGT-free lifetime gifting of appreciated shares); death gives a tax-free uplift. 5-year temporary non-residence rule.
  • Trading risk: badges of trade; frequent dealing can be taxed as income (45% + NIC).

Dividends

  • From 6 April 2026 (Budget 2025, Finance Bill 2025-26): 10.75% ordinary, 35.75% upper, 39.35% additional (unchanged). Dividend allowance £500. JSON: domestic and foreign 0.3935, foreign withholding credited.
  • Savings (interest) rates 20/40/45% in 2026/27, rising to 22/42/47% from 6 April 2027 (announced, legislated per HMRC technical note) - relevant for bonds.
  • No UK withholding on dividends paid by UK companies (REIT/PAIF PIDs 20%, 22% from April 2027). Treaty network about 120 territories (PwC).

Inheritance and gifts to children

  • Inheritance Tax Act 1984. Rate 40% above the £325,000 nil-rate band (frozen to 5 April 2031). Residence nil-rate band £175,000 tapers away above a £2m estate. JSON: basis estate, exemption 325,000, bracket 0.40 from 0, children taxed.
  • Scope from 6 April 2025 (Finance Act 2025 s.44, new IHTA ss.6A-6C): long-term UK resident = UK resident in at least 10 of the previous 20 tax years; worldwide assets in scope. Tail after leaving: 3 years (10-13 years' residence) rising to 10 years (20 years). Ten consecutive non-resident years reset the count. Non-long-term residents: UK-situs assets only.
  • Gifts: potentially exempt transfers, tax-free if the donor survives 7 years; taper 32/24/16/8% for years 3-7. Annual exemption £3,000; gifts out of surplus income unlimited. Most trust gifts 20% immediately.
  • Business/Agricultural Property Relief from 6 April 2026: 100% relief up to a combined £2.5m per person (first announced as £1m in October 2024, raised 23 December 2025), transferable to a spouse (£5m per couple), 50% above. AIM and other non-'listed' market shares 50% relief. Listed portfolio shares get no relief (JSON: business_relief_covers_public_shares false).
  • Pensions: unused pension funds and most death benefits enter the estate from 6 April 2027 (Finance Act 2026).
  • Probate fee £526 for estates over £5,000 (JSON other_transfer_cost_rate 0).

Surviving spouse: Transfers to a spouse or civil partner are exempt without limit (Inheritance Tax Act 1984 s. 18(1)), but where the transferor is a long-term UK resident and the spouse is not, the exemption is capped at the exemption limit, £325,000 (s. 18(2)); the gift must not be conditional beyond 12 months or take effect after an interest ends (s. 18(3)). It defers rather than removes the tax: the assets form part of the survivor's estate.

Wealth tax

None (JSON type none). Council tax is a modest per-dwelling charge. Announced: High Value Council Tax Surcharge in England from April 2028 for homes valued at £2m+ in 2026: £2,500 / £3,500 / £5,000 / £7,500 by band (£2-2.5m, £2.5-3.5m, £3.5-5m, £5m+), CPI-indexed from 2029-30; not in force, final legislation not seen. Transaction taxes: SDLT up to 12% (17% second homes, +2% non-resident), 0.5% stamp duty/SDRT on UK share purchases.

Special regimes

4-year FIG regime (JSON id fig_4_year, duration 4): for individuals in their first 4 tax years of UK residence after at least 10 consecutive years of non-residence, from 2025/26. Claimed foreign income and gains (foreign dividends, interest, foreign capital gains, overseas trade/property profits) are not taxed and can be brought into the UK. Claim per year and per source in the return; no carry-forward; claimants lose the personal allowance and CGT annual exempt amount. UK-source income and gains are taxed normally. JSON: foreign listed securities and foreign real estate 0.0; foreign dividends 0.0; domestic items unchanged. Inheritance override explains that new arrivals are outside IHT on non-UK assets until long-term resident (about 10 years). Temporary Repatriation Facility (12% for 2025/26 and 2026/27, 15% for 2027/28) only for pre-April-2025 foreign income/gains of former remittance-basis users.

Holding structures

Structure modelled: family investment company (FIC), regime id `family_investment_company`.

  • Corporation tax: a FIC is normally a close investment-holding company, so it pays the 25% main rate on all taxable profits; it cannot use the 19% small profits rate or marginal relief (GOV.UK, Marginal Relief). Gains on portfolio shares are taxable at 25%, one point above personal CGT (24%); no indexation for assets acquired from
  • The substantial shareholding exemption covers 10%+ stakes in trading companies (JSON private business 0). Interest is taxed at 25% (against 45%, 47% from April 2027, for individuals).
  • Dividends received: exempt under CTA 2009 Part 9A: distributions on non-redeemable ordinary shares (s.931F) and portfolio holdings under 10% (s.931G). Foreign withholding tax on exempt dividends is a final cost. JSON: dividends 0, exemption.
  • Payout: dividends to individuals in 2026/27 at 10.75% / 35.75% / 39.35% after a GBP 500 allowance (GOV.UK). payout_tax_rate 0.3935 assumes additional-rate parents. In practice FICs are funded with an interest-free loan repayable on demand, and repaying it is tax-free, so withdrawals can be untaxed for many years (ICAEW). Dividends can be steered to adult children through alphabet shares, subject to the settlements legislation.
  • Inheritance (not representable in the metric, which stays at the standard 40%): parents keep voting or freezer shares fixed at today's value while children hold growth shares that take value above a hurdle, so growth accrues outside the parents' estates (Geldards). Gifts of shares are potentially exempt transfers (free after 7 years); funds put into the FIC are not PETs in themselves, and a loan repayable on demand avoids a transfer of value. No business property relief: an investment company's shares are not relevant business property (IHTA 1984 s.105(3)). Watch IHTA s.98 (altering share rights), s.3(3) (not exercising dividend rights) and gifts with reservation. FIC shares are UK-situs, so they are in the IHT net even before the family becomes long-term resident, unlike a foreign custody portfolio of a new arrival.
  • Getting assets in: transferring shares in is a disposal at market value between connected persons (24% CGT); FICs are funded with cash. New arrivals can realise foreign gains tax-free in their FIG years, then fund the FIC.
  • Exit and liquidation: liquidation distributions are capital (24% CGT, no BADR; TAAR in ITTOIA s.396B). The UK has no exit tax on individuals; dividends to non-residents bear no UK withholding, but the 5-year temporary non-residence rule catches close-company distributions. Moving the company's management abroad triggers a corporate exit charge.
  • Comparison for this family (illustrative, app profile): dividend tax falls from about EUR 155k (39.35% less 15% foreign withholding credited) to nil, but gains cost EUR 12k more and the foreign withholding is lost; with a EUR 400k payout taxed as dividends (EUR 157k) the FIC is slightly worse on annual tax than direct holding (about EUR 457k against EUR 443k). Its advantages are those the annual score does not show: tax-free extraction by loan repayment, 25% instead of 45-47% on bond interest, and IHT savings from growth shares.
  • Running costs: not sourced (omitted). Annual accounts and CT600 required; small companies are usually audit-exempt.
  • US citizens: excluded (US CFC/PFIC rules).

Company taxation (kept separate from personal tax)

Corporation tax 25% main rate (profits above £250,000), 19% small profits rate (below £50,000), marginal relief between. Distribution exemption for most dividends received; substantial shareholding exemption for trading subsidiaries. No dividend withholding tax.
