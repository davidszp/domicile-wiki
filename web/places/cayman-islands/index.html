Tax on investments, inheritance and wealth

The Cayman Islands has no income, capital gains, dividend, inheritance, estate, gift, wealth or property tax. The real costs are stamp duty on buying Cayman land (7.5%, and 10% from 1 January 2026 for property worth CI$2 million or more) and nominal probate fees for Cayman-situs assets. No tax treaties. Wealthy foreigners usually reside under the 25-year Residency Certificate for persons of independent means (CI$1 million investment, at least CI$500,000 in developed real estate, CI$120,000 annual income).

  • Ordinary residence: No direct taxes on individuals.

Income tax for employees and remote workers

No income, withholding, payroll or social security taxes on individuals. The only mandatory contribution is the national pension: 5% from the employee (matched by the employer) up to CI$87,000 of earnings, or 10% for the self-employed.

How to get residence

The Cayman Islands offer a 25-year renewable residency certificate (CI$1 million invested plus CI$120,000 income, CI$50,000 fee, no right to work) and a certificate of permanent residence for CI$2 million of property (CI$200,000 fee, work allowed), both revised on 1 May 2026. The remote-worker programme (Global Citizen Concierge) is closed. No direct taxes. EU, US and other passports use the same routes.

Inheritance law and trusts

The Cayman Islands have no forced heirship. Cayman trusts are protected by statute against foreign forced-heirship claims and a foundation company (2017) is available. The Hague Trusts Convention is not listed as extended to Cayman.

Research notes

Cayman Islands — family-office tax dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15 Tax year: 2026 Reviewer: research agent (Claude), unreviewed by a tax adviser

Summary

The Cayman Islands has no income, capital gains, dividend, inheritance, estate, gift, wealth or property tax. The real costs are stamp duty on buying Cayman land (7.5%, and 10% from 1 January 2026 for property worth CI$2 million or more) and nominal probate fees for Cayman-situs assets. No tax treaties. Wealthy foreigners usually reside under the 25-year Residency Certificate for persons of independent means (CI$1 million investment, at least CI$500,000 in developed real estate, CI$120,000 annual income).

Residence and eligibility

No tax residence test needed. 25-year Residency Certificate: CI$1m investment (≥ CI$500k developed real estate), CI$120k income, CI$50k grant fee, 30 days' presence a year. Permanent residence for persons of independent means: CI$2m+ real estate (Bedell Cristin 2023). Thresholds expected to rise.

Capital gains

None. JSON 0.

Stamp duty on transfers of Cayman land: 7.5% for non-Caymanian buyers, on the higher of price and market value; from 1 January 2026 10% where the value is CI$2 million or more (Stamp Duty (Rates of Duty) (No. 2) Regulations 2025). Share transfers in land-holding companies follow the same 7.5%/10%. Mortgages: 1% up to CI$300,000, 1.5% above. First-time Caymanian buyer concessions (exempt up to CI$550,000 from February 2025) do not apply to non-Caymanians.

Dividends

None; no WHT; no treaties. JSON 0.

Inheritance and gifts to children

No inheritance tax, estate tax, death duty or gift tax (PwC 2026; Mourant 2026). Probate fees nominal (CI$200 application, CI$10 stamp on grant). Natural-love-and-affection land transfers between certain relatives exempt from stamp duty. JSON basis none, fully_exempt, other cost 0.

Second pass (2026-10-15): PwC's Cayman 'Other taxes' page (reviewed 29 May 2026) gives stamp duty of generally 7.5% on transfers of Cayman immovable property (also on shares of land-holding companies) but does not address transmission on death or family transfers. other_transfer_cost_rate stays 0 pending the Stamp Duty Act schedule; see Open questions.

Wealth tax

None; no property tax.

Special regimes

None needed.

Company taxation (kept separate from personal tax)

No corporate income, capital gains, payroll or other direct taxes on companies. No Pillar Two domestic minimum tax enacted (PwC reports no significant developments in the year to May 2026). Economic substance and CRS/FATCA reporting apply.

Open questions

  • An October 2025 Immigration reform bill (Cayman Compass) may change investor-residency thresholds; article could not be opened and current WORC figures were not verified on an official page.
  • No indicator overrides: the Cayman Islands is in the World Bank/WGI data (homicide rate 2020, internet users 2017, life expectancy 2024), so under the research guide no override is added; RCIPS annual crime statistics were not retrieved to check for a newer homicide figure. UNDP publishes no HDI and TI no CPI for the Cayman Islands; there is no same-definition substitute, so the hdi and corruption composites stay unscored.
  • inheritance (standard): Whether stamp duty applies when Cayman land vests in heirs on death (transmission). PwC (reviewed 29 May 2026) gives stamp duty of generally 7.5% on transfers of Cayman immovable property and on shares in land-holding companies, but does not address transfers on death or to family; the Stamp Duty Act schedule was not read. other_transfer_cost_rate is kept at 0 pending a primary source.
