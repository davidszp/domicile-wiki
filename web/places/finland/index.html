Tax on investments, inheritance and wealth

Capital gains taxed at 34% at the top, listed dividends at an effective 28.9%. Inheritance tax per child: tax-free under EUR 30,000 (2026), then 7%-19%, about 18-19% on a large share. No wealth tax. EU member, no special regime for wealthy arrivals. A family investment Oy pays 20% on gains and dividends, and pays out lightly taxed unlisted-company dividends (7.5% on the first EUR 150,000 per shareholder within an 8% return).

  • Ordinary tax residence: Residents are taxed on worldwide income. Capital income (gains, dividends, interest, rents) is taxed separately from earned income at 30%/34%.
  • Private investment company (osakeyhtiö, Oy): The family holds its portfolio through a Finnish private limited company (Oy). The company pays the 20% corporate rate on portfolio gains and on listed-share dividends (portfolio dividends are fully taxable in an unlisted company), instead of 34% / 28.9% personally. Withdrawals are dividends from an unlisted company, which are taxed lightly: within an 8% return on the shares' net asset value, 25% of the first EUR 150,000 per shareholder a year is taxable capital income (7.5% effective). No wealth tax; inheritance tax applies to the Oy shares as to other assets (inherited from the standard regime).

Income tax for employees and remote workers

State progressive scale (12.64% to 37.5% as PwC lists it) plus municipal tax of 4.70-10.90% (7.5% used). Employee contributions about 10.17%; YEL for self-employed 24.4%.

Inheritance law and trusts

Children are guaranteed half of their intestate share. A foreign resident can choose the law of their nationality (EU Succession Regulation art. 22). Finland has no trust law; foundations exist but family-benefit foundations get no tax exemption and their precise limits were not confirmed.

Research notes

Finland — family-office tax dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-16 Tax year: 2026 Reviewer: research agent (Claude), unreviewed by a tax adviser

Summary

Capital gains are taxed at 34% at the top. Listed dividends are taxed at an effective 28.9%. There is no wealth tax. Inheritance tax per child: nothing below EUR 30,000 (from 2026), then 7% to 19% on the whole share, so about 18.6% on a EUR 10m share. Finland is an EU member with no special regime for wealthy arrivals.

Residence and eligibility

A person is resident with a permanent home or habitual abode in Finland, or after more than 6 months' continuous stay. Finnish nationals stay resident for 3 calendar years after leaving unless they prove they have no essential ties (PwC). EU citizens use free movement (registration after 3 months). Others need a residence permit on another basis (work, entrepreneur, start-up, family). There is no investor visa. Migri could not be fetched automatically, so these permit details are unverified.

Capital gains

Capital income tax is 30% up to EUR 30,000 and 34% above (Vero, "Selling shares"; PwC). There is no holding-period relief on the rate. A deemed acquisition cost of 20% of the price (40% after 10 years) can replace the actual cost. Sales totalling EUR 1,000 or less a year are exempt. A main home used for 2 years is exempt. Losses carry forward 5 years. JSON: 0.34 for all categories (the statutory marginal rate on the gain). There is no exit tax for individuals. 2026 change: earn-out gains are now taxed in the year the amount is confirmed. A business-scale trader can be taxed partly as earned income (top marginal rate about 52%).

Dividends

For listed companies, 85% of the dividend is taxable at 30%/34%, so JSON 0.289 = 0.85 x 0.34. For unlisted companies, 25% is taxable on the part within an 8% return on net asset value up to EUR 150,000. Above that, within the 8% return, 85% is taxable. Above the 8% return, 75% is taxable as earned income. Foreign dividends get the same treatment if paid from the EU/EEA or a treaty country, which includes the US; foreign WHT is credited up to the treaty rate (Vero, "Foreign dividends"). Dividends from non-treaty, non-EEA countries are taxed in full as earned income. Outbound WHT is 30%/35% to non-resident individuals and 20% to non-resident companies. PwC lists about 76 treaty partners.

Inheritance and gifts to children

Inheritance tax is charged per heir, and the 2026 tables apply to deaths from 1 January 2026 (Vero inheritance tax calculator). Children (bracket 1) pay nothing below EUR 30,000. Above it, tax starts at EUR 100 + 7% of the excess over 30,000. The base amount is EUR 800 at 40,000 (+10%), EUR 2,800 at 60,000 (+13%), EUR 21,000 at 200,000 (+16%) and EUR 149,000 at 1,000,000 (+19%). JSON: marginal brackets from 30,000, bracket_mode marginal (checked: 100 + 0.07 x 10,000 = 800, and so on), with the flat EUR 100 omitted.

Gifts from one donor are added up over 3 years. They are exempt below EUR 7,500 (EUR 5,000 before 2026), and rates run from 8% to 17%. Gifts made within 3 years before death are added to the inheritance. The tax reaches worldwide assets if the deceased or the heir was resident; Finnish real estate is always taxable. Generational business relief exists but does not cover listed portfolios.

  • Surviving spouse: tax class I with the same 7%-19% scale as children plus a EUR 90,000 spouse deduction (PerVL 12 §); about 18.6% on a EUR 10 million share, so taxed.

Wealth tax

None (abolished 2006; PwC). Municipal real estate tax is 0.41%–2.00%. Transfer tax is 3% on real property and 1.5% on unlisted securities; listed securities are generally exempt.

Special regimes

None relevant. The 25% tax-at-source regime for key employees applies to earned income only.

Holding structures

Structure modelled: family investment company (osakeyhtiö, Oy), regime id `holding_oy`.

  • Company level: an unlisted Oy pays the 20% corporate rate (2026) on gains on portfolio shares; the participation exemption needs a 10% fixed-asset holding kept for one year. Dividends from listed companies in which an unlisted company holds less than 10% are 100% taxable, and dividends from outside the EU/EEA are fully taxable too, so portfolio dividends also bear 20%, with credit for foreign withholding (PwC, Corporate income determination, reviewed September 2026). Compare 34% on gains and 28.9% on listed dividends personally.
  • JSON (structure-level): gains 0.2 on all classes (private business conservatively 0.2; exempt only if the participation-exemption conditions are met); dividends 0.2 domestic and foreign with credit.
  • Payout: dividends from an unlisted company (Vero, "Dividend from an unlisted company", updated 1 January 2026): within an 8% return on the shares' mathematical value (net assets per the previous year's balance sheet), 25% is taxable capital income up to EUR 150,000 per shareholder a year (7.5% effective) and 85% above; above the 8% return, 75% is taxable earned income. The limit is per shareholder across all unlisted companies. payout_tax_rate 0.1546 = tax as a share of the gross dividend when two parents each net EUR 200,000 (EUR 400,000 in total, which is what the projection needs after tax). Per shareholder with gross dividend G above EUR 150,000: taxable capital income = 25% x 150,000 + 85% x (G - 150,000); tax = 30% x 30,000 + 34% x (taxable - 30,000). Net 200,000 needs G = EUR 236,568 and tax EUR 36,568 (15.46% of G); EUR 473,136 gross in total, tax EUR 73,136. The earlier 0.13 was the tax on a EUR 400,000 gross payout (EUR 52,000), which leaves only EUR 348,000 net. Bands are the 2026 figures; no statutory indexation was found, so in real terms the rate would rise over time, but the model keeps it fixed. Adult children as shareholders put more into the 25% band. The 8% limit needs book net assets of at least EUR 1.9m per EUR 150,000 of dividend.
  • Wealth tax: none in Finland. Inheritance: the Oy shares are inherited at fair value under the ordinary rates (inherited from standard); generational business relief does not cover investment companies.
  • Anti-avoidance and running: general anti-avoidance rule (Act on Assessment Procedure § 28); hidden profit distributions are taxed as dividends or earned income; repayment from the invested unrestricted equity fund of an unlisted company is taxed as a dividend. Running and set-up costs not sourced (omitted).
  • Getting assets in: contributing an existing portfolio is a taxable sale for the individual at 30/34% and Finland gives no step-up on immigration.
  • Exit and liquidation: no exit tax for individuals (Finnish nationals keep a 3-year residence tail). Liquidation proceeds are a capital gain for the shareholder at 30/34%. Dividends paid after emigration bear withholding.
  • Pending: HE 151/2026 would cut the corporate rate to 18% from 2027 (not confirmed enacted). A VATT/Etla study proposes reworking the unlisted-dividend relief; no bill found.
  • US citizens: excluded (US CFC/PFIC rules).
  • Unverified: participation-exemption treatment of a private business stake in a passive holding's income source; audit thresholds; inheritance valuation of the Oy shares (deferred tax on latent gains).

Company taxation (kept separate from personal tax)

CIT is 20%. Government proposal HE 151/2026 would cut it to 18% from 2027 (and corporate WHT to 18%), with loss carry-forward extended to 25 years. Enactment was not confirmed as of 2026-10-15.
