Tax on investments, inheritance and wealth

High-tax for investment wealth: dividends and share gains effectively 37.84%, plus a broad worldwide net wealth tax of 1.0-1.1% (about 0.8-0.88% on listed shares after the 20% valuation discount). No inheritance or gift tax, but heirs inherit the cost basis. A 37.84% exit tax on latent share gains above NOK 3m applies on leaving. No special regime for wealthy arrivals. A personal holding AS defers tax: gains on Norwegian/EEA shares and funds are exempt in the company and dividends taxed at 0.66%, with 37.84% due on payout.

  • Ordinary tax residence: Norwegian tax resident taxed on worldwide income and wealth.
  • Personal holding company (holding AS) under the participation exemption: The family holds its portfolio through a Norwegian private limited company (aksjeselskap) owned by the parents. Under the participation exemption (fritaksmetoden, skatteloven § 2-38) the company pays no tax on gains on qualifying shares and only 0.66% on qualifying dividends; personal tax of 37.84% is deferred until money is paid out to the family. Qualifying shares are shares in Norwegian and EEA companies and EEA funds (UCITS); direct holdings below 10% in companies outside the EEA (e.g. US shares) do NOT qualify and are taxed at 22% in the company, so the foreign portfolio is normally held via EEA-domiciled funds or EEA shares. Wealth tax and the absence of inheritance tax are as for personal holding (inherited from the standard regime): the AS shares are valued at the company's net tax value with the same 20% share discount.

Income tax for employees and remote workers

22% on general income plus bracket tax (trinnskatt) of 1.7% to 17.8% on personal income, plus national insurance 7.6% for employees (10.8% self-employed). Top marginal rate 47.4% for employees (22 + 17.8 + 7.6).

How to get residence

EEA free movement applies: EU/EEA citizens register with the police within three months and may stay as workers, students or self-sufficient persons with funds and insurance. US and other citizens (UK citizens included since Brexit) have no investor or digital nomad route; they need a permit based on work, study or family, such as the skilled worker permit with a minimum pay.

Citizenship: Eight years of residence in the last eleven (six of the last ten with sufficient income and a fixed tax settlement), plus Norwegian language proficiency and the citizenship test.

Inheritance law and trusts

Children are guaranteed two thirds of the estate, up to a cap per child, and a will cannot reduce it. Norway has no trust law and is outside the Hague Trusts Convention. Foundations exist but family foundations are restricted.

Research notes

Norway — family-office tax dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-16 Tax year: 2026 Reviewer: research agent (Claude), unreviewed by a tax adviser

Summary

Norway is a high-tax home for investment wealth. Dividends and share gains are taxed at an effective 37.84%. A broad worldwide net wealth tax of 1.0% applies above NOK 1.9 million, and 1.1% above NOK 21.5 million. Listed shares count at 80% of their value, so the effective wealth tax on a share portfolio is about 0.8–0.88%. Norway has had no inheritance or gift tax since 2014, but heirs take over the cost basis. An exit tax of 37.84% applies to latent share gains above NOK 3 million when a resident leaves. There is no special regime for wealthy arrivals.

Residence and eligibility

  • Tax residence: more than 183 days in Norway in any 12-month period, or more than 270 days in any 36-month period.
  • Ending residence: residence ends from the first income year in which the person spends no more than 61 days in Norway and has no dwelling available to them or close relatives. After 10 or more years of residence, those conditions must hold for three consecutive years, and residence ends on 1 January of the fourth year. (PwC, Residence.)
  • EU/EEA citizens: register under the EEA rules. A person who does not work must show sufficient means and health insurance.
  • US and other citizens: there is no investor visa. A permit needs a basis such as work, self-employment, family or study.
  • These immigration routes were not verified on UDI, whose pages returned HTTP 403.

Capital gains

  • Rule: share gains are multiplied by 1.72 and taxed at the 22% ordinary income rate, an effective 37.84% (Skatteetaten, 2026 factor table). This applies to Norwegian and foreign shares, listed or not, and to the sale of a private company by a personal shareholder.
  • JSON: listed domestic and foreign shares and private business sale are all 0.3784 (22% × 1.72).
  • Shielding deduction: cost basis × the risk-free rate is deducted. The rate was 3.6% for 2025; the 2026 rate is published in January 2027. It is ignored in the JSON because it is small against a large gain.
  • Real estate: gains are ordinary income at 22% (JSON 0.22). The main home is exempt under the ownership and use conditions, which were not re-verified. Foreign real estate is set at 0.22 with a credit; this was not re-verified either.
  • Share savings account (aksjesparekonto): defers tax on listed shares and equity funds held inside it until more than the amount deposited is withdrawn.
  • Exit tax (skatteloven § 10-70), rewritten by Lovvedtak 17 (2024–2025) for emigrations from 20 March 2024:
  • On leaving, latent share gains are taxed as if realised the day before departure, at 37.84%, on the part of total net latent gains above NOK 3,000,000.
  • Payment can be deferred for up to 12 years with security: either interest-free instalments of 1/12 a year, or one payment at the end with interest.
  • The deferral ends on sale, death and certain transfers.
  • The tax lapses for shares still held if the person becomes resident again within 12 years.
  • Skatteetaten's guideline of 31 March 2025 covers the security rules. When the person moves to an EEA state, security is required only where there is a real risk of non-collection.

Dividends

  • Rule: Norwegian and foreign dividends are taxed at 22% × 1.72 = 37.84% above the shielding deduction. JSON: domestic and foreign rates are both 0.3784.
  • Foreign withholding tax: credited against Norwegian tax.
  • Outbound withholding: 25% on dividends to non-residents, reduced by treaty. EEA corporate shareholders with real substance can be exempt.
  • Treaties: about 82 bilateral treaties plus the Nordic multilateral treaty (PwC table).
  • Pending: the 2027 budget proposal of 7 October 2026 keeps the 1.72 factor. It has not been adopted yet.

Inheritance and gifts to children

  • Rule: inheritance and gift tax were abolished for deaths and gifts from 1 January 2014 (Skatteetaten, "Arveavgift er fjernet").
  • Cost basis carries over: heirs and recipients take over the original cost basis, so the 37.84% gain tax is deferred, not forgiven. Homes, cabins and some farm and forest property are exceptions.
  • JSON: basis none, fully_exempt, no brackets.

Wealth tax

  • Rates for 2026 (Skatteetaten):
  • Municipal: 0.35% above NOK 1,900,000.
  • State: 0.65% between NOK 1,900,000 and NOK 21,500,000, and 0.75% above.
  • Combined: 1.0% / 1.1%.
  • The thresholds are per single taxpayer and doubled for spouses taxed jointly.
  • Valuation discounts for 2026:
  • Shares, including business property: 20%.
  • Operating assets: 30%.
  • Primary residence: 75%, but 30% on value above NOK 14 million. The revised 2026 budget raised this threshold from NOK 10 million.
  • Secondary residences: 0%.
  • Debt is reduced in proportion to the discounted assets.
  • JSON: net_wealth_worldwide, exempt_amount 3,800,000 (the couple's allowance: Skatteetaten doubles the thresholds for spouses taxed jointly), brackets of 1.0% from 0 and 1.1% from 39,200,000. The second figure is NOK 43m (2 x 21.5m) minus the 3.8m allowance. Not indexed by statute. The JSON sets securities_valued_at 0.8, so listed shares and funds count at 80%; the primary-home discount is not modelled, so the home counts in full and wealth tax is somewhat overstated.
  • 2027 proposal (Prop. 1 S 2026–2027), not adopted: the allowance rises to NOK 1,990,000, and the rates and the share discount stay the same (Smarte Penger summary).
  • NOU 2026:9: the tax commission proposed removing the valuation discounts and lowering the rates. It is a study only.
  • Property tax: municipalities may levy an optional eiendomsskatt. It is not a wealth tax, and its rates were not researched.

Special regimes

None. Norway has no lump-sum or non-dom regime.

Holding structures

Structure modelled: personal holding company (holding AS), regime id `holding_as`.

  • Participation exemption (fritaksmetoden, skatteloven § 2-38): a Norwegian AS pays no tax on gains on qualifying shares, whatever the size of the stake; losses on them are not deductible. Qualifying dividends are 97% exempt: 3% is taxed at 22%, an effective 0.66% (PwC, Corporate income determination).
  • What qualifies: shares in Norwegian and EEA companies, and EEA securities funds (UCITS). From 2026 the fund definition sits in FSFIN § 2-38-1.
  • Portfolio exception for non-EEA holdings (§ 2-38 third paragraph letter b): shares in companies outside the EEA qualify only if the AS has held at least 10% of capital and votes for two years. A family's US shares are therefore taxed in the AS at 22% on gains and dividends (with credit for withholding). Binding ruling BFU 5/2026 (3 July 2026) applied the exception to units in a US equity fund. Low-tax countries outside the EEA never qualify. In practice the foreign portfolio is held through EEA-domiciled funds (e.g. Irish or Luxembourg UCITS ETFs) or EEA shares.
  • JSON (structure-level): gains 0 on domestic and foreign listed shares (assuming EEA shares/funds) and on a private business held through the AS; real estate 0.22; dividends 0.0066 domestic and foreign, no credit (the 0.66% leaves little to credit foreign withholding against; immaterial).
  • Payout: dividends to the parents are taxed at 22% × 1.72 = 37.84% above the shielding deduction (payout_tax_rate 0.3784). Loans from the AS to a personal shareholder or close relatives are taxed as dividends (skatteloven § 10-11 fourth paragraph, since 7 October 2015; Skatteetaten Q&A). Repayment of paid-in capital is tax-free.
  • Wealth tax: the AS itself is not liable. The parents pay wealth tax on the AS shares, valued at the company's tax value of net assets on 1 January of the income year (Skatte-ABC A-4-4.2), less the 20% share discount (Skatteetaten 2026 rates). For a listed portfolio this is the same 80% value as direct holding, so the wealth_tax metric is inherited from the standard regime. Valuation lags by one year.
  • Inheritance: none (abolished 2014); heirs take over the cost basis of the AS shares. Inherited from standard.
  • Anti-avoidance and running: general anti-avoidance rule (skatteloven § 13-2). Accounts and a company return are needed; with a balance sheet this size an auditor is likely required (audit thresholds not re-verified). Running and set-up costs are not sourced and are omitted from the JSON. Minimum share capital is NOK 30,000.
  • Getting assets in: transferring an existing personal portfolio to the AS is a realisation at 37.84%, and Norway gives no step-up on immigration; the structure suits cash or assets with small latent gains on arrival.
  • Exit and liquidation: liquidation proceeds are a realisation of the AS shares at 37.84%. On emigration the exit tax (§ 10-70) taxes latent gains on the AS shares above NOK 3m at 37.84%, deferrable up to 12 years.
  • US citizens: excluded from the regime (US CFC/PFIC rules tax the company's passive income currently).
  • Unverified: whether foreign withholding can be credited against the tax on the 3% portion; audit thresholds.

Company taxation (kept separate from personal tax)

  • Rate: 22% corporate income tax, and 25% for some financial companies.
  • Participation exemption (fritaksmetoden): applies to corporate shareholders. Its partial clawback was not re-verified.
  • Holding company: a personal holding company (AS) defers personal dividend tax until it distributes.
  • Svalbard: has its own low-tax regime, which was not researched.

Open questions

  • The 2026 shielding rate is not yet published.
  • The 2027 budget proposals are not yet adopted.
  • The UDI immigration routes were not verified.
  • The shielding deduction (cost basis x risk-free rate) is ignored in the JSON rates; on a cost basis of NOK 30 million it could exceed the whole annual dividend. The cost basis of the family's shares is unknown.
  • Moving an existing personal portfolio into an AS or an ASK is a realisation at 37.84% (no step-up on arrival); the share savings account (ASK) holds only EEA-domiciled shares and funds (Skatteetaten) and is not modelled.
  • The 2027 budget proposal (7 October 2026) keeps the 1.72 factor; not yet adopted by the Storting.
  • The main-home exemption and the taxation of foreign real estate were not re-verified.
