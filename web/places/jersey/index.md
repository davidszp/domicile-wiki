## Tax on investments, inheritance and wealth

Crown Dependency outside the UK and EU. No capital gains tax, no inheritance or gift tax, no wealth tax. Income including dividends is taxed at a flat 20%. Probate stamp duty up to 0.75%, capped at £100,000. Wealthy newcomers normally come as High Value Residents: minimum £250,000 tax a year, 1% on income above £1.25m.

- **Ordinary Jersey residence**: Residents pay a flat 20% income tax on worldwide income. No capital gains, inheritance, gift or wealth tax; probate stamp duty of up to 0.75% (capped at £100,000).
- **High Value Residency (2(1)(e) housing consent)**: Discretionary residency for wealthy newcomers. Jersey property income is taxed at 20%; other income at 20% on the first £1.25m and 1% above. New HVRs (applications from 14 July 2023) must pay at least £250,000 a year: with income under £1.25m they are deemed to have enough income to pay that minimum.
- **Jersey investment holding company (0% company)**: The family funds a Jersey company that holds the listed portfolio. Jersey companies pay 0% income tax on investment income, and there is no capital gains tax, so the company pays nothing on dividends or gains. The shareholders are taxed at 20% only when value is taken out of the company (dividend, buy-back, liquidation); profits it keeps are not taxed on them, because the deemed distribution rules were repealed for profits accruing from 1 January 2012. Foreign withholding tax on the company's dividends is a final cost.

## How to get residence

Jersey is not in the EU or EEA and, since Brexit, EU citizens have no free movement there: they are treated like other foreign nationals. British and Irish citizens can live in Jersey under the Common Travel Area, but still need a housing and work status. For everyone else the main routes are an employer-sponsored work permit (with a visa where required) and, for the wealthy, discretionary Entitled status as a High Net Worth Individual, indicated by taxable income above GBP 1.25 million a year. The old GBP 2 million investor route is closed.

- **High Value Residency: Entitled status for High Net Worth Individuals (formerly 2(1)(e))** (Investor residence; EU citizens, US citizens, other passports; open): Wealthy, economically active people can be granted Entitled status, which gives full access to Jersey's housing and job markets. It is a discretionary grant for people who bring economic or social benefit, not a purchase of residence, and UK-style immigration clearance is also needed.
- **Investor route (GBP 2 million) under the Jersey Immigration Rules** (Investor residence; EU citizens, US citizens, other passports; closed to new applicants): A route for people investing GBP 2 million in Jersey on terms set by the Minister. It no longer accepts new applicants.
- **Person establishing himself in business (entry clearance route)** (Entrepreneur; EU citizens, US citizens, other passports; open): The Jersey Immigration Rules still contain a route for someone to take over, join or set up a business in Jersey, with 2 years' initial leave and settlement after 5 years. Ministerial approval is needed and no capital figure is published in the rules.
- **Long-term work permit (employer-sponsored)** (Work; EU citizens, US citizens, other passports; open): An employer applies for a work permit for a named person in an eligible job. Up to 3 years at a time; time counts toward settlement and dependants are allowed.
- **Common Travel Area: British and Irish citizens** (Other; other passports; open): British and Irish citizens need no visa or work permit to enter and work in Jersey. They still need a Control of Housing and Work status, which starts as Standard and gives full rights after 10 years (Entitled) or 5 years (Entitled for Work Only).

Citizenship: British citizenship through the Jersey Customs and Immigration Service: generally 5 years' residence in the UK or the Islands with indefinite leave or settled status, with at least 12 months after indefinite leave; English language and Life in the UK test (including a Jersey supplement). Fee GBP 1,709 for naturalisation.

## Inheritance law and trusts

Jersey protects heirs on immovable property (land) with fixed shares, but movables are free. Trusts are well established and foundations are available. The Hague Trusts Convention applies to Jersey.

### Jersey — family-office tax dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-16
Tax year: 2026
Reviewer: research agent (Claude), unreviewed by a tax adviser

#### Summary

Crown Dependency outside the UK and EU. No capital gains tax, no inheritance or gift tax, no wealth tax. Income including dividends is taxed at a flat 20%. Probate stamp duty up to 0.75%, capped at £100,000. Wealthy newcomers normally come as High Value Residents: minimum £250,000 tax a year, 1% on income above £1.25m.

#### Residence and eligibility

Resident if present six months or more in a tax year, or if a place of abode is available and at least one night is spent there, or by habitual visits averaging three months a year (PwC). Residence rules are under review.

- **High Value Residency (2(1)(e))** (EU, US, OTHER): Discretionary consent; minimum £250,000 annual tax; expected net worth £10m+; purchase of a high-value property (stated in 2023 as £3.5m houses / £1.75m apartments). Non-British/Irish nationals also need immigration permission (Jersey follows UK-style rules).
- **British/Irish citizens and settled persons** (OTHER): May live in Jersey without immigration permission but face Control of Housing and Work restrictions on buying or renting property until they qualify for housing status (qualifying periods not verified here).

Standard regime eligibility: Tax residence in Jersey. Buying or renting property needs housing status (Control of Housing and Work); newcomers without it have very limited housing options, which is why wealthy arrivals use the HVR route.

#### Capital gains

Jersey has no capital gains tax. Gains by a resident private investor on securities, a private business or real estate are not taxed, unless the activity is a trade (taxed as income at 20%). Stamp duty or land transaction tax applies on buying Jersey property.

- **rate basis**: No capital gains tax in Jersey; income tax (20%) applies to income, and to trading profits, not to capital gains.
- **professional trader**: Share or property dealing that amounts to a trade is taxed as income at 20%; Jersey property development profits are taxed at 20%.
- **holding period rules**: None.
- **foreign assets**: Foreign gains are equally untaxed.

- **Confidence / verification**: high / partially_verified (tax year 2026)
- **Open question**: PwC 'other taxes' page does not expressly state 'no CGT'; widely documented but not quoted from an official Revenue Jersey page in this research.

#### Dividends

Dividends are taxed as income at a flat 20% (the alternative marginal calculation at 26% only helps low incomes). For 2026 the income tax exemption threshold is £21,250; all married couples are taxed independently from 2026. High Value Residents pay 1% on non-Jersey-property income above £1.25m (see special regime).

- **rate basis**: Flat 20% standard rate on net income after allowances (PwC). Foreign dividend withholding is relieved by a double tax credit only where Jersey has a double taxation agreement covering that income; otherwise the taxpayer may ask for the income to be taxed net of the foreign tax (Revenue Jersey). Jersey has no treaty with the United States and few others, so the foreign tax is modelled as a deduction: 20% x (dividend - 15% withholding). Most Jersey companies pay 0% corporate tax, so no imputation credit arises on typical domestic dividends.
- **exemptions thresholds**: Exemption threshold £21,250 (2026); relevant only at low income.
- **withholding outbound**: No withholding tax on dividends, interest or royalties paid by Jersey companies to non-residents (PwC).
- **treaty network**: Small set of double tax agreements (UK, Guernsey, Isle of Man and a few others); no US income tax treaty, so US dividends suffer 30% US withholding. Where there is no agreement covering the income, foreign tax is relieved only by taxing the income net of that tax (Revenue Jersey); where there is one, by credit (Income Tax (Jersey) Law 1961, Art. 111-112).
- **JSON values**: `{"headline_rate": 0.2, "domestic_rate": 0.2, "foreign_gross_rate": 0.2, "foreign_tax_credit": "deduction"}`
- **Confidence / verification**: high / verified (tax year 2026)
- **Open question**: Exact treaty count not verified from an official list. Partner countries with a Jersey treaty covering dividends would give a credit instead of a deduction; the model treats all foreign dividends as from non-treaty countries.

#### Inheritance and gifts to children

No inheritance tax and no gift tax. A probate stamp duty applies to the personal (movable) estate: 0.5% up to £100,000, then £500 plus £75 per £10,000 (0.75%), capped at £100,000 for estates above £13.36m. For a Jersey-domiciled person it applies to worldwide movable estate.

- **exemptions thresholds**: Probate duty (Stamp Duties and Fees (Jersey) Law 1998, Schedule 3 item 9): no fee up to £10,000 net; £50 per £10,000 to £100,000; £500 + £75 per £10,000 up to £13,360,000; flat £100,000 above. Item 9(2) adds a fixed GBP 107 (professional application) or GBP 268 (other). other_transfer_cost_rate 0.0075 is the marginal rate up to the cap; for a £30m movable estate the effective cost is about 0.33%.
- **important exceptions**: Lifetime gifts of shares and portfolios attract no duty, so the probate duty can be avoided by lifetime transfers or holding structures. Jersey succession law gives spouse and children rights over part of movable estate (not a tax; not verified here).
- **foreign assets**: Probate duty covers worldwide personal estate of a Jersey-domiciled deceased; only Jersey-situs estate for others (Art. 5).
- **gift tax**: No gift tax. Stamp duty applies to gifts of Jersey immovable property (land) as for transfers.
- **domicile rules**: Duty base depends on Jersey domicile at death. No tax tail. Families arriving from the UK remain within UK IHT under the residence-based test from 6 April 2025 for up to 10 years after leaving, depending on years of UK residence.
- **JSON values**: `{"basis": "none", "children_treatment": "fully_exempt", "exemption": 0, "other_transfer_cost_rate": 0.0075, "gifts_same_as_inheritance": false}`
- **Confidence / verification**: high / verified (tax year 2026)
- **Open question**: Whether Jersey still has légitime (forced heirship) rights over movables was not verified.

#### Wealth tax

No wealth tax or net worth tax.

- **property tax note**: Parish rates are levied on property by each parish (modest). Stamp duty applies to purchases of Jersey real estate; non-main-residence purchases carry an extra 4 percentage points since 2023.
- **JSON values**: `{"type": "none"}`
- **Confidence / verification**: high / verified (tax year 2026)

#### Special regimes

##### High Value Residency (2(1)(e) housing consent) (`high_value_residency`)

Discretionary residency for wealthy newcomers. Jersey property income is taxed at 20%; other income at 20% on the first £1.25m and 1% above. New HVRs (applications from 14 July 2023) must pay at least £250,000 a year: with income under £1.25m they are deemed to have enough income to pay that minimum.

- **Eligibility**: Application to the Government of Jersey (via Locate Jersey); granted at discretion considering economic and social benefit and financial history. Government stated in 2023 an expectation of net worth of at least £10m excluding the main home and a minimum property purchase of £3.5m (houses) / £1.75m (apartments), confirmation of final figures pending. Consent gives the right to buy or rent property.
- **Duration (years)**: indefinite
- **Why it does not expire**: No time limit: the HVR tax treatment applies for as long as the individual holds 2(1)(e) housing consent, stays Jersey resident and meets the conditions of approval (including the minimum tax); if a condition is breached, the treatment reverts to ordinary rules from that year onwards (Revenue Jersey HVR guidance).
- **Lump sum / cap in JSON**: 250,000 GBP a year, covers foreign_dividends, foreign_capital_gains, foreign_income_all, domestic_income. Minimum annual tax of £250,000 per applicant (gov.je). Covers income up to £1.25m; income above £1.25m (except Jersey property income at 20%) is taxed at 1%. Spouses are taxed independently. HVRs approved before 14 July 2023 stay on older terms (£170,000 minimum under the 2018 rules).

**Dividends under this regime:** For an HVR, dividends above the first £1.25m of income are taxed at 1%; the first £1.25m is taxed at 20%, subject to a minimum total tax of £250,000.

- **rate basis**: Marginal rate 1% on non-Jersey-property income above £1.25m; the £250,000 minimum is modelled as the lump sum.
- **withholding outbound**: No withholding tax on dividends paid by Jersey companies to non-residents.
- **treaty network**: As standard regime.
- **JSON values**: `{"headline_rate": 0.01, "domestic_rate": 0.01, "foreign_gross_rate": 0.01, "foreign_tax_credit": "deduction"}`
- **Confidence / verification**: high / verified (tax year 2026)

#### Holding structures

**Structure modelled: Jersey investment holding company, regime id `holding_company`.**

- **Company tax:** the general corporate rate is 0% (PwC); Jersey has no capital gains tax. Dividends and gains in the company are untaxed (JSON dividends 0, `exemption`; gains 0). Foreign withholding tax on the company's dividends is a final cost. Financial services (10%), utilities, Jersey property and large retail (20%) differ; a passive portfolio company is none of these.
- **Payout:** a distribution to a Jersey-resident individual (cash dividend, buy-back, liquidation payment, repayment of a member's loan, benefit in kind; Income Tax (Jersey) Law 1961, Art. 3AE) is taxed under Schedule DIX at 20% up to the individual's allocated share of 'specified profits', in full under the 'simplified basis'; the excess falls under Schedule DIII and can be exempt (capital profits, return of share capital, loan principal; Art. 78(1A)). `payout_tax_rate` 0.20 is therefore the ceiling (Comptroller's Distribution Rules guide, draft of 11 February 2013).
- **Retained profits:** not taxed on the shareholder; the deemed distribution and attribution rules were repealed for profits accruing from 1 January 2012 (Revenue Jersey). Trusts: the Taxes Office watches their use (Art. 134A).
- **Inheritance:** the shares are movable estate, so probate stamp duty applies as for personal holding; the metric is the standard regime's.
- **Residence:** a Jersey-incorporated company is resident in Jersey (Art. 123(1)(a)).
- **Running costs:** not sourced (omitted). US citizens are excluded (CFC/PFIC rules).
- **Considered, not added:** Jersey private foundations and family trusts (a resident trustee is strictly taxable on all trust income, and the usual concession follows the beneficiaries; Chambers Private Wealth 2024), because they save no income tax for a resident family and were not sourced further.

#### Company taxation (kept separate from personal tax)

Headline CIT rate in JSON: 0.0.

General corporate rate 0%; 10% for regulated financial services companies; 20% for utilities, Jersey real estate income and large retailers. Multinational Corporate Income Tax (15%) for groups with revenue of €750m+ from 2025. No withholding tax on dividends to non-residents.
