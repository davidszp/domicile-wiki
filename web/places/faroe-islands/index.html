Tax on investments, inheritance and wealth

The Faroe Islands tax residents' capital income (interest, dividends and gains on shares and other securities) under a separate capital gains tax law at a flat 35% for individuals; ordinary income pays progressive national tax plus 16%-23% municipal tax. Emigrants are deemed to sell securities worth over DKK 100,000. Inheritance is free of income tax, but an inheritance duty (arvaavgjald) of 1%-12% is paid to the probate court; children pay at most 3%. Gifts from parents to children are tax-free. No wealth tax was found.

  • Full tax liability: Flat 35% tax on capital income and gains; progressive tax on other income; inheritance duty up to 3% for children.

Inheritance law and trusts

Faroese succession law follows the Danish model, which protects children with a forced share, but the exact rules were not confirmed. The Faroes have no trust law and are outside the Hague Trusts Convention. Foundations exist under Faroese law.

Faroe Islands — family-office tax dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15 Tax year: 2026 Reviewer: research agent (Claude), unreviewed by a tax adviser

Summary

The Faroe Islands tax residents' capital income (interest, dividends and gains on shares and other securities) under a separate capital gains tax law at a flat 35% for individuals; ordinary income pays progressive national tax plus 16%-23% municipal tax. Emigrants are deemed to sell securities worth over DKK 100,000. Inheritance is free of income tax, but an inheritance duty (arvaavgjald) of 1%-12% is paid to the probate court; children pay at most 3%. Gifts from parents to children are tax-free. No wealth tax was found.

US citizens: No US-Faroes treaty; US citizens remain subject to US federal tax.

Residence and eligibility

More than 180 days in a 12-month period, or a registered permanent address (TAKS).

  • Danish and Nordic citizens (EU, OTHER): Danish and Nordic citizens can settle freely; the Faroes are outside the EU, so other EU citizens need permits.
  • Others (EU, US, OTHER): Work/residence permits under Faroese immigration rules; no investor route found.

Capital gains

Standard regime

Løgtingslóg nr. 164/2001 on capital gains covers sales of shares, investment certificates and similar securities, debt claims, and also dividends and interest (§ 1). Individuals and estates pay 35% on the net gain (§ 14(2)). On emigration, securities worth over DKK 100,000 (or below acquisition value) are deemed sold; payment can be deferred against a bank guarantee.

  • Important exceptions, statute references and announced changes: Exit tax: deemed disposal of securities over DKK 100,000 on moving away (TAKS).
  • Tax year / confidence / verification: 2026 / medium / verified (checked 2026-10-15)
  • JSON numbers: headline 35% (0.35); listed domestic 35% (0.35); listed foreign 35% (0.35); private business sale 35% (0.35); local real estate n/a (null); foreign real estate n/a (null).
  • How derived: § 14(2) Løgtingslóg um skatting av kapitalvinningi: individuals 35% on the computed gain.

Open points for this tax: Gains on real estate are outside this law; treatment of private real estate gains not researched (left null).

Dividends

Standard regime

Dividends are capital income taxed at 35% (Løgtingslóg nr. 164/2001 § 1(5) and § 14); Faroese companies withhold 35%. Foreign tax paid on foreign income can be deducted from the Faroese tax up to the tax on that income (TAKS Uttanlandsinntøka).

  • Tax year / confidence / verification: 2026 / medium / partially_verified (checked 2026-10-15)
  • JSON numbers: headline 35% (0.35); domestic 35% (0.35); foreign gross 35% (0.35); foreign tax relief: credit.
  • How derived: Flat 35% capital-income rate for individuals; 35% withholding on Faroese dividends.
  • Outbound withholding: 35% on dividends (refund to treaty rate: UK 5%/15%, Nordic 0%/15%, Switzerland 0%/15%, India 15%/25%).
  • Treaty network: Nordic convention, UK, Switzerland, India and a few others.

Inheritance and gifts to children

Standard regime

Inheritances are free of income tax, but arvaavgjald (inheritance duty) of 1%-12% is paid to the probate court depending on the heir and the size of the share (TAKS). The 1908 Danish inheritance duty act, in force in the Faroes by Anordning 468/1921, sets for spouse and children: 1% on shares up to DKK 1,000, rising in steps to 3% on shares over DKK 100,000 (§ 2 A), the band rate applying to the whole share. Advance inheritance (forskotsarvur) is subject to the duty; ordinary gifts from parents to children are tax-free.

  • Important exceptions, statute references and announced changes: No duty while the surviving spouse keeps the estate undivided (óskift búgv). Spouse and children under 18 have an exemption under conditions (§ 3).
  • Tax year / confidence / verification: 2026 / medium / partially_verified (checked 2026-10-15)
  • JSON numbers: basis beneficiary, children taxed_low, exemption 0 DKK, brackets (whole_amount): from 0 DKK: 1% (0.01); from 1,000 DKK: 1.25% (0.0125); from 2,000 DKK: 1.5% (0.015); from 3,000 DKK: 1.75% (0.0175); from 5,000 DKK: 2% (0.02); from 10,000 DKK: 2.25% (0.0225); from 20,000 DKK: 2.5% (0.025); from 50,000 DKK: 2.75% (0.0275); from 100,000 DKK: 3% (0.03); other transfer costs 0% (0.0).
  • Gifts: Gifts from parents to children are tax-free (TAKS, Skattalógin § 29); advance inheritance pays arvaavgjald. (same as inheritance: False; tax-free after years: None)
  • Residence/domicile rules: Estates administered by the Faroese probate court (skiftirættur).
  • Business relief covers listed shares: False

Open points for this tax: Later amendments to Anordning 468/1921 not checked; the TAKS summary (1%-12%) matches the original table. | Scope for foreign assets of a Faroese-domiciled deceased and whether a spouse in undivided estate (óskift búgv) defers the duty for children not analysed.

  • Surviving spouse: pays the same 1%-3% duty as children (1908 act § 2 A); no tax while the estate is kept undivided, but tax falls due when it is divided.

Wealth tax

Standard regime

No wealth tax found: TAKS lists taxes on income, interest, gifts and inheritance, pension tax and capital gains tax, with no tax on wealth.

  • Tax year / confidence / verification: 2026 / medium / partially_verified (checked 2026-10-15)
  • JSON type: none
  • Property tax (not a wealth tax): Municipal property tax not researched.

Special regimes

None modelled; see Residence and eligibility.

Company taxation (kept separate from personal tax)

Headline CIT rate: 18% (0.18). Companies and funds pay 18% on capital gains under Løgtingslóg 164/2001 § 14(2); the general corporate rate is believed to be 18% as well (not verified at source).

Cost of living notes

City: Tórshavn (incl. Hoyvík and Argir).

  • Prime rent (monthly): DKK 14,750 (2026). Median of the 4 listings of at least 140 m² in Tórshavn municipality on Kitt.fo (the Faroese rental portal) on 2026-10-15: 204 m² 5-room house in Hoyvík DKK 14,500 (6-month let); 192 m² 3-bedroom-plus-office flat in Argir DKK 16,900 (over 3 years); 150 m² house DKK 15,000 and 145 m² house DKK 13,000 in central Tórshavn (under 1 year). 48 listings in all; no other had 4+ bedrooms. Some rents include utilities. Very thin sample: many Faroese lets are arranged privately or via Facebook groups. Source: Kitt — Leiguportalurin
  • School (annual, per child): DKK 0 (2026). No international school exists in the Faroe Islands and no private upper-secondary school was found. The leading option is the public Glasir – Tórshavn College (about 1,500 students); education is free at all levels. Teaching is in Faroese, so a newly arrived child would need language support. Sources: faroeislands.fo — Education, Trap Faroe Islands — Education and research
