Tax on investments, inheritance and wealth

Hungary taxes capital gains and dividends at a flat 15% (plus a capped 13% social contribution tax on some income), has no inheritance or gift tax between parents and children, and has no wealth tax in force in 2026. However, a 1% wealth tax on net wealth above HUF 1 billion (about EUR 2.6m) is in draft and would apply to wealth held on 31 December 2026 if passed. The US-Hungary tax treaty ended in 2024, which makes US dividends expensive (about 35%).

  • Ordinary tax residence: Flat 15% PIT on capital income; no special regime for new arrivals.
  • Asset management foundation (vagyonkezelő alapítvány): The family contributes the portfolio to a Hungarian asset management foundation (Act XIII of 2021) with the parents and children as beneficiaries. The foundation is a 9% corporate taxpayer, but where founder and beneficiaries are individuals and its income comes only from holding and disposing of financial instruments, securities, receivables and cash, that income is not taxed, so gains and dividends compound tax-free. Since 31 August 2026 (tax package adopted 28 July 2026) distributions are taxed as dividends (15%), profits first, and the tax-free step-up of contributed assets after five years is gone for new contributions. No inheritance event arises (and Hungary does not tax children anyway). The draft wealth tax (scored pro forma, as in the standard regime) treats the foundation as a separate taxpayer with its own HUF 1bn allowance.

Income tax for employees and remote workers

Flat 15% personal income tax on nearly all income; independent-activity income counts at 90% of gross or gross minus deductible costs; family and other allowances exist. Employee social security contribution 18.5% of gross, no cap stated (minimum wage HUF 322,800 a month from 1 Jan 2026).

How to get residence

EU citizens register after 90 days. For non-EU nationals the Guest Investor Programme (EUR 250,000 in a real estate fund, or a EUR 1 million donation to a university foundation; the property option was dropped) still exists in law as of the latest sources, but the Tisza government elected in April 2026 pledged to abolish it and no repeal or transition rules were found. The White Card gives remote workers earning about EUR 3,000 net a month up to two years. The EU Blue Card needs about HUF 1 million gross a month.

Inheritance law and trusts

Descendants, spouse and parents are guaranteed a third of their intestate share, as a money claim. A foreign resident can choose the law of their nationality (EU Succession Regulation art. 22). Hungary has a statutory fiduciary management contract in place of a trust, and private foundations are allowed.

Research notes

Hungary — family-office tax dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15 Tax year: 2026 Reviewer: research agent (Claude), unreviewed by a tax adviser

Summary

Flat 15% personal income tax (PIT, Act CXVII of 1995, "Szja tv.") on capital gains and dividends; a 13% social contribution tax ("szocho", Act LII of 2018) applies to some capital income but is capped. No inheritance or gift duty between parents and children (Act XCIII of 1990 on duties, "Itv."). No wealth tax in force in 2026, but a government draft published 6 October 2026 would tax net wealth above HUF 1 billion at 1% from the 31 December 2026 valuation date. The US-Hungary tax treaty ended on 1 January 2024.

Residence and eligibility

  • Resident: Hungarian citizens; EEA nationals with a Hungarian registration card present 183+ days in the calendar year; settled third-country nationals; otherwise only permanent home / centre of vital interests / 183 days (PwC).
  • EU citizens: free movement, registration certificate. US/other: national residence permits. The Guest Investor residence permit (in force from July 2024) is slated for abolition by the Tisza government (Vialto, April 2026); no official repeal found as of 2026-10-15.

Capital gains

  • Rule: 15% PIT on the gain. A "controlled capital market transaction" (ellenőrzött tőkepiaci ügylet, Szja tv. § 67/A) through an investment service provider in Hungary, the EEA or a treaty state: 15% only, no szocho, losses can be netted (tax equalisation).
  • Other gains (e.g. private company sale, non-treaty broker): 15% PIT + 13% szocho, szocho base capped at 24x monthly minimum wage = 24 x HUF 322,800 = HUF 7,747,200 (2026), reduced by salary and other szocho-bearing income.
  • Since the US treaty ended, a US broker no longer qualifies for controlled-transaction treatment (RSM).
  • TBSZ (long-term investment account): tax-free after 5 years from the collection year; 10% PIT (and reduced szocho, reported as 8%) in years 4-5; full rates within 3 years. Szocho on TBSZ applies since 2025.
  • Real estate: 15%, exempt after 5 years of ownership.
  • JSON: listed_securities_domestic/foreign = 0.15; private_business_sale = 0.15 (capped szocho immaterial for a large gain); real_estate_local/foreign = 0.0 (held > 5 years).

Dividends

  • 15% PIT. Szocho 13% does not apply to dividends from companies listed on an EEA regulated market; for other dividends (e.g. US-listed) it applies up to the HUF 7,747,200 cap (max about HUF 1.0m a year).
  • Foreign WHT credited up to the treaty rate (15%). For non-treaty income Hungary leaves at least 5% PIT: US dividends now bear 30% US WHT + 5% Hungarian = about 35% (RSM; IRS Announcement 2024-5 via KPMG).
  • Outbound: no WHT on dividends to foreign companies; 15% to non-resident individuals (treaty-reduced).
  • JSON: domestic_rate = foreign_gross_rate = 0.15, foreign_tax_credit = credit.

Inheritance and gifts to children

  • Itv.: general 18% (9% residential property) duty, but inheritances and gifts between lineal relatives (ascendants/descendants by blood or adoption) and to the surviving spouse are fully exempt, any amount, any asset. Siblings exempt since 8 July 2020 (NAV). Exempt lineal gifts need no reporting.
  • Stepchildren and foster children are not lineal relatives.
  • JSON: basis = beneficiary, children_treatment = fully_exempt, brackets = [], gifts_same_as_inheritance = true, other_transfer_cost_rate = 0 (notary probate fees small, not quantified).
  • Surviving spouse: exempt from inheritance and gift duty on the same terms as lineal relatives.

Wealth tax

  • Scored PRO FORMA (editorial decision, 2026-10-15): the draft below is scored as if enacted, because enactment by the two-thirds majority is treated as near-certain. JSON type = broad, basis = pro_forma_draft: net worldwide wealth, HUF 1bn allowance, 1% marginal, 1.5% on wealth above HUF 100bn (bracket from HUF 99bn above the allowance). Each spouse's HUF 1bn allowance is applied (HUF 2bn for the reference couple).
  • In force 2026: none (PwC: "no net wealth/worth taxes in Hungary").
  • Draft bill (Ministry of Finance, public consultation 6-14 October 2026, government summary):
  • Taxpayers: resident individuals (worldwide wealth), non-resident individuals (Hungarian real estate and Hungarian company shares only), and "asset-management taxpayers" (trusts, private foundations, similar foreign structures; foreign ones count as domestic if effectively managed from Hungary).
  • Rate: 1% on net wealth above HUF 1 billion up to a base of HUF 100 billion; 1.5% above that.
  • Each spouse has a separate HUF 1bn threshold; minors' non-inherited assets go into the parent's base; related asset-management structures share one threshold.
  • Debts deductible; personal-use movables excluded except art/jewellery > HUF 3m and vehicles > HUF 10m.
  • Valuation: listed securities at market; unlisted companies 1/3 equity + 2/3 capitalised earnings (15%); foreign real estate at cost indexed 3% a year or by official index.
  • On ceasing residence mid-year, wealth is measured the day before.
  • Entry into force 15 December 2026; first valuation 31 December 2026; return and payment by 31 August 2027.
  • Not yet passed by parliament as of 2026-10-15. Tisza holds a two-thirds majority.
  • Local building and land taxes are municipal property taxes, not wealth tax.

Status check 2026-10-15: the draft is still a consultation draft (consultation closed 14 Oct 2026); no bill had been filed in Parliament (Index.hu, 10 Oct 2026). Not scored; JSON type stays 'none'.

Special regimes

None for new arrivals. The vagyonkezelő alapítvány (asset management foundation, Act XIII of 2019) is a domestic succession/holding vehicle; its tax treatment (CIT, distributions to beneficiaries, pending 2026 changes) was not verified from primary sources, so it is not modelled as a regime. Under the wealth tax draft it would be a separate taxpayer sharing one HUF 1bn threshold with related structures.

Holding structures

Asset management foundation (asset_management_foundation, structure)

A vagyonkezelő alapítvány (Act XIII of 2021), with the parents and children as beneficiaries. It is scored because it removes the 15% tax on gains while assets stay inside: about EUR 450,000 a year against EUR 510,000 personally on the app profile (draft wealth tax included).

  • Foundation level: the foundation is a CIT taxpayer (9%). Its income is not taxed if founder and beneficiaries are individuals and the income comes solely from acquiring, holding and disposing of financial instruments, securities, receivables and cash. Peterka (April 2026) describes this rule for trusts; Mondaq (January 2026) says the same applies to asset management foundations. JSON: listed gains 0, dividends 0. A private business sale and real estate are 0.09 (9% CIT; upper bound). Foreign withholding cannot be credited. The statute itself (njt.hu) could not be reached.
  • 2026 tax package: Bill T/387 was adopted on 28 July 2026 (WTS) and most of it applies from 31 August 2026 (Forvis Mazars, KPMG). For assets contributed from that date:
  • the acquisition value and unrealised gain must be recorded;
  • the tax-free distribution of revalued assets after 5 years ends;
  • distributions are taxed as dividends, accumulated yield first, then the value increase, then the remaining original capital tax-free;
  • distributing an original asset in kind stays tax-free, with carry-over cost.

Also new: annual reporting to NAV (first report due 31 March 2027) and NAV audits of every structure from 2028. Assets already in place before 12 September 2023, or already stepped up, keep the old rules.

  • Payout: dividend income bears 15% PIT, plus szocho capped at 24 x the minimum wage, which is negligible on EUR 400,000. payout_tax_rate 0.15, matching the standard regime's 15%.
  • Wealth tax (pro forma, as the standard regime): the 6 October 2026 draft treats private and asset management foundations as separate taxpayers, separate from founders and beneficiaries. Related structures cannot multiply the HUF 1bn allowance (Ministry of Finance draft summary). Modelled as two components, each 1% above HUF 1bn: the foundation on the portfolio (financial_assets_all) and the family on its home (real_estate_all). The draft actually gives each spouse a separate HUF 1bn allowance; the standard regime uses one family allowance, and this regime follows it for consistency.
  • Inheritance: no event while the foundation holds the assets. Children are exempt in Hungary anyway; the standard regime is inherited.
  • Costs: HUF 6m a year and HUF 2m setup (estimates: accounting, CIT declaration and annual report, board, notary/court). Not sourced.
  • Unverified: the CIT Act text of the foundation exemption, the szocho treatment of foundation distributions, and how the foundation's allowance interacts with the founders' own.

Company taxation (kept separate from personal tax)

9% CIT; local business tax up to 2% of adjusted revenue (dividends and interest exempt); dividends received exempt; no WHT on dividends/interest/royalties to foreign companies (PwC).

Open questions

  • Exact TBSZ szocho rates in years 4-5 after the 2025 change (sources give 8%); not material for a 5-year holding.
  • Whether dividends credited inside a TBSZ account are sheltered like gains; not verified.
  • Draft wealth tax (1% above HUF 1bn net wealth, 1.5% above HUF 100bn) published 6 Oct 2026; consultation closed 14 Oct 2026; not yet submitted to Parliament as of 15 Oct 2026. If enacted as drafted it applies to wealth held on 31 Dec 2026. Re-check parlament.hu and switch this metric to 'broad' with the enacted brackets when passed.
  • Whether the final law adds an exit tax or a main-home exemption, and how the announced 5% 'offering' of the tax to public causes works.
  • Notary probate fees for a large estate not quantified; treated as negligible.
  • Final enactment of the wealth tax bill (draft of 6 Oct 2026; not submitted to Parliament as of 15 Oct 2026).
  • Tax treatment of the vagyonkezelő alapítvány (asset management foundation): CIT exemption of portfolio income, taxation of distributions to beneficiaries, and pending 2026 changes were not verified from primary sources; the draft wealth tax treats such foundations as separate taxpayers.
  • Future of the Guest Investor residence programme.
