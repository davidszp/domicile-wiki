Tax on investments, inheritance and wealth

Poland taxes capital gains and dividends at a flat 19%, with a 4% solidarity levy on income above PLN 1m that hits large capital gains (23%) but not dividends. Gifts and inheritances to children are exempt if reported within 6 months. No wealth tax. New arrivals can opt for a PLN 200,000 a year lump sum on all foreign income for 10 years (PLN 100,000 per family member, plus PLN 100,000 a year of required qualifying spending).

  • Ordinary tax residence: Flat 19% on capital income, 4% solidarity levy above PLN 1m (gains, not dividends).
  • Lump-sum tax on foreign income (ryczałt od przychodów zagranicznych, art. 30j-30p): Fixed annual tax of PLN 200,000 replacing PIT on all foreign-source revenue (except CFC income) for up to 10 years; spouse and children can join at PLN 100,000 each.
  • Family foundation (fundacja rodzinna): Since 22 May 2023 a Polish family foundation can hold the family's portfolio. Its income from permitted activity, which includes acquiring and selling securities and holding interests in companies and funds at home or abroad, is exempt from CIT (art. 6(1)(25) and (7) CIT Act; art. 5 Foundation Act), so gains and dividends are untaxed while they stay inside. Benefits paid to beneficiaries bear 15% CIT payable by the foundation (art. 24q(1) CIT Act); the founder's spouse, descendants and other zero-group relatives are exempt from PIT on them (art. 21(1)(157) PIT Act). No inheritance event arises when the parents die: the foundation is a separate legal person. A government bill (draft UD447, August 2026) would from 2027 raise the 15% to 19%, add a holding period for contributed assets, CFC rules and a tax on unrealised gains; it is not law. Sources disagree on the holding period (36 months in the August 2026 alerts, two years in a 1-2 October 2026 report) and on its stage (Pitax says the Council of Ministers adopted it; no Sejm print was found). A similar 2025 bill was vetoed.

Income tax for employees and remote workers

Resident income tax is 12% on income up to PLN 120,000 (tax-free amount PLN 30,000, delivered as a PLN 3,600 tax-reducing amount) and PLN 10,800 plus 32% above that; a 4% solidarity levy applies above PLN 1,000,000. Employee contributions: 13.71% (pension and disability 11.26% up to the PLN 282,600 annual cap, sickness 2.45% uncapped) plus 9% health on income net of those contributions, uncapped. The recorded rate is the combined 22.71%; the cap applies to the 11.26% pension and disability part only.

Inheritance law and trusts

Descendants, spouse and parents have a money claim for half (two thirds for minors) of their intestate share. A foreign resident can choose the law of their nationality (EU Succession Regulation art. 22). Poland has no trusts but since May 2023 allows family foundations.

Research notes

Poland — family-office tax dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-16 Tax year: 2026 Reviewer: research agent (Claude), unreviewed by a tax adviser

Summary

PIT Act (ustawa o podatku dochodowym od osób fizycznych), consolidated text Dz.U. 2026 poz. 592. Capital gains and dividends: 19% flat. 4% solidarity levy on income above PLN 1m includes capital gains but not dividends. Inheritance and gift tax (Dz.U. 2026 poz. 478): children exempt if reported within 6 months. No wealth tax. Optional PLN 200,000 lump sum on foreign income for 10 years for new residents.

Residence and eligibility

Resident if the centre of personal or economic interests is in Poland or presence exceeds 183 days in a tax year (art. 3(1a); PwC). EU: free movement. US/other: purpose-based permits; no investor visa found (unverified).

Capital gains

  • Art. 30b: 19% on gains from securities, derivatives, shares/stakes, fund units. No holding-period exemption.
  • Art. 30h: solidarity levy 4% on the excess over PLN 1,000,000 of income taxed under art. 27, 30b, 30c and 30f. So large gains bear 23% at the margin.
  • Real estate: taxable only if sold within 5 years from the end of the year of acquisition (art. 10(1)(8)); then 19% under art. 30e, not in the levy base.
  • Exit tax on unrealised gains when leaving (art. 30da-30db, read in the statute 2026-10-16): 19% of the gain on shares, securities and fund units when a person resident in Poland for at least 5 of the previous 10 years moves residence abroad, and only when the assets moved exceed PLN 4,000,000 (spouses together). It matters on departure, not on arrival.
  • JSON: securities and private business 0.23; real estate 0.0; headline 0.23.

Dividends

  • Art. 30a(1): 19% flat; art. 30a(9): foreign tax credited up to the Polish 19%. Not in the solidarity levy base.
  • Outbound: 19% WHT, treaty-reduced (PwC).
  • Treaties: PwC lists 92, 5 not yet in force; US treaty in force.
  • JSON: domestic and foreign 0.19, credit.

Inheritance and gifts to children

  • Art. 4a: exemption for spouse, descendants, ascendants, stepchildren, siblings, stepparents ("zero group") if (1) reported to the tax office within 6 months (inheritance: from the court ruling becoming final or registration of the notarial certificate of succession, art. 4a(1a)); (2) cash gifts above the tax-free amount received by bank/payment-account transfer. Wording amended with effect from 20 August 2025 (Dz.U. 2025 poz. 1064). No report needed under the Group I tax-free amount or for notarial-deed transfers (art. 4a(4)).
  • If missed: Group I scale (art. 15): 3% up to PLN 10,278, 5% to PLN 20,556, 7% above, after the tax-free amount (art. 9: PLN 36,120 in the consolidated text); 5-year aggregation per donor. Gift invoked only during audit: 20%.
  • Scope (art. 1-3): Polish-situs assets; foreign assets if the recipient is a Polish citizen or permanent resident.
  • JSON: basis = beneficiary, children_treatment = exempt_with_conditions, brackets = [].
  • Surviving spouse (spouse_treatment exempt_with_conditions): Spouse is exempt (zero group, Art. 4a) if reported within 6 months and the acquirer is a Polish/EU/EEA citizen or resident (Art. 4(4)); missed reports are taxed at Group I rates.

Wealth tax

None. Municipal real estate tax (2026 max PLN 1.25/m2 residential) and 2% civil law transactions tax on real estate purchases are not wealth taxes (PwC).

Special regimes

Lump sum on foreign income (art. 30j-30p):

  • PLN 200,000 a year (pro rata in the arrival year) replaces PIT on all foreign revenue except CFC income (art. 30f); foreign revenue is not combined with other income.
  • Conditions: no Polish residence in at least 5 of the 6 preceding years; election by end of January after the move, with residence certificate; at least PLN 100,000 a year of qualifying spending (economic growth, science, education, culture, sport) from the year after the move (art. 30m).
  • Family members (spouse, children within art. 27f(1)) may join at PLN 100,000 each, without the spending duty (art. 30p(1)). If the taxpayer dies or the person stops being a family member, the amount becomes PLN 200,000 and the spending duty applies from the next year (art. 30p(1) pt 4). JSON: lump_sum.spouse_supplement = PLN 100,000 for the reference couple, who hold the portfolio jointly; the children are assumed not to elect (no foreign income), so the couple pays PLN 300,000 a year. The spending (PLN 100,000 a year, not deductible, art. 30k(3)) is not a tax and is not in the lump sum amount.
  • Art. 30k(1): the lump sum covers revenue obtained outside Poland, except CFC income (art. 30f). Foreign dividends and gains on foreign shares are such revenue in the usual reading; the statute gives no further definition.
  • 10 consecutive tax years (art. 30n). Lost if the lump sum is unpaid, spending missed, or residence moves abroad.
  • JSON: lump_sum.annual = PLN 200,000, foreign gains and dividends 0; domestic as standard.
  • Open: whether foreign-listed shares sold via a Polish broker are foreign-source (art. 30k(1) does not define it).

Holding structures

Family foundation (family_foundation, structure)

A fundacja rodzinna (Act of 26 January 2023, in force since 22 May 2023). It is scored because gains and dividends are untaxed inside and payouts bear 15%: about EUR 70,000 a year against EUR 309,000 personally on the app profile. The lump-sum regime for foreign income is about EUR 130,000.

  • Foundation level: exempt from CIT on income from permitted activity (art. 6(1)(25) CIT Act, Dz.U. 2026 poz. 554), which includes acquiring and selling securities and derivatives and holding interests in companies and funds in Poland or abroad (art. 5(1) pts 3-4 Foundation Act, Dz.U. 2023 poz. 326). Business activity beyond art. 5 is taxed at 25% (art. 24r); rent of a business or assets used by the founder, a beneficiary or a related party (5%+) loses the exemption (art. 6(8)). JSON: all gains 0, dividends 0 (foreign withholding is a final cost).
  • Payout: 15% CIT paid by the foundation on benefits to beneficiaries, on assets distributed at dissolution (base reduced by the founder's contributed value) and on hidden profits (art. 24q(1) CIT Act; the art. 6 exemption does not cover it, art. 6(6)). The founder's spouse, descendants, ascendants, stepchildren, siblings and step-parents are exempt from PIT (art. 21(1)(157) PIT Act). Groups I-II pay 10%, others 15% (Grant Thornton). payout_tax_rate 0.15.
  • Wealth and inheritance: Poland has no wealth tax. There is no inheritance event while the foundation holds the assets, and children inherit tax-free anyway if the transfer is reported. Both inherit the standard regime.
  • Not in force: a Ministry of Finance project (draft UD447; Pitax of 1-2 October 2026 says the Council of Ministers has adopted it, no Sejm print found) published in August 2026 (MDDP, 5 August 2026; Grant Thornton, 7 August 2026) would:
  • raise the 15% to 19%;
  • require contributed assets to be held to keep the preference (36 months in the August alerts, two years in the Pitax October report; unresolved);
  • apply CFC rules to foundations;
  • tax income earned through transparent entities;
  • clarify short-term rental;
  • extend the PIT exemption to the founder's siblings' descendants.

It is planned for adoption by the Council of Ministers in Q3 2026 and would take effect in 2027 at the earliest. A 2025 bill with similar changes was vetoed by the President. Grant Thornton confirms that in 2026 there is no holding period and no CFC rule.

  • Costs: PLN 40,000 a year and PLN 20,000 setup (estimates; Freenance, a commercial site, gives PLN 20,000-45,000 a year for accounting and administration of an investment foundation). The founding capital of at least PLN 100,000 stays in the foundation.
  • Statute read 2026-10-16: art. 6(1)(25), 6(6)-(9), 24q and 24r CIT Act; art. 2 and 5 Foundation Act; art. 21(1)(157) PIT Act. Unverified: whether source states grant treaty rates to an exempt foundation; the founder's side of contributing listed shares.

Company taxation (kept separate from personal tax)

19% CIT, 9% small taxpayers; Estonian CIT option; family foundations (fundacja rodzinna) CIT-exempt on ongoing passive activity (PwC); 15% on benefits (art. 24q).

Open questions

  • The Group I tax-free amount in the consolidated text is PLN 36,120 (art. 9(1)(1)); not relevant once the zero-group exemption is claimed.
  • Source rules: whether gains on foreign-listed shares sold through a Polish broker count as 'foreign revenue' (art. 30k) was not checked.
  • The brief referred to the lump sum as art. 30h; in the consolidated PIT Act (Dz.U. 2026 poz. 592) art. 30h is the solidarity levy and the lump sum is art. 30j-30p.
  • Government bill UD447 (2026) would raise the family foundation's payout tax to 19% and add a holding period and CFC rules from 2027; the law in force is scored.
  • Treaty rates for dividends paid to a CIT-exempt family foundation were not researched.
