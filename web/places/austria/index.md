## Tax on investments, inheritance and wealth

Austria taxes a resident's capital gains on securities and dividends at a flat 27.5% (bank interest 25%) regardless of holding period or size of holding, and real estate gains at 30%. There is no wealth tax and no inheritance or gift tax (abolished 2008), so children inherit a securities portfolio tax-free; only real estate passing within the family bears a stepped real estate transfer tax of 0.5%-3.5% plus a 1.1% registration fee, and larger lifetime gifts must be notified to the tax office. Emigration triggers a deemed sale of financial assets, deferred without interest for moves within the EU/EEA.

- **Ordinary tax residence**: Unlimited liability on worldwide income for residents. Investment income is taxed at special flat rates (§ 27a EStG) that are final when withheld by an Austrian bank.
- **Private foundation (Privatstiftung, PSG)**: The family endows an Austrian Privatstiftung with the portfolio. The foundation is its own legal person (§ 1 PSG) and owns the portfolio. It pays no tax on foreign portfolio dividends (exempt under § 13(2) and § 10(1) KStG) but 27.5% interim tax (Zwischensteuer) on realised gains, interest and derivatives (§ 13(3), § 22(2) KStG, rate from 2026). Distributions to beneficiaries (Zuwendungen) bear 27.5% KESt, and the interim tax is credited when distributions exceed the separately taxed income (§ 24(5) KStG). Endowing it costs 3.5% foundation entry tax. Austria has no inheritance tax, so the foundation offers no estate-tax saving; its use is control, succession planning and deferral of tax on dividends.
- **Asset-management GmbH (vermögensverwaltende GmbH)**: The family holds the portfolio through an Austrian GmbH. The GmbH pays 23% corporate tax on realised share gains (no exemption for stakes below 10%) but portfolio dividends from comparable foreign corporations in states with comprehensive administrative assistance, and domestic dividends, are exempt (§ 10 KStG). Distributions to the family bear 27.5% KESt. Austria has no inheritance tax, so the GmbH offers no estate-tax saving; its use is deferral of the personal 27.5% tax on dividends and gains.

## Income tax

Austria taxes residents on worldwide income with seven brackets for 2026: 0% to EUR 13,539, then 20%, 30%, 40%, 48%, 50% above EUR 104,859 and 55% above EUR 1 million. There is no municipal income tax in Vienna. Employees pay 18.07% social insurance up to a monthly ceiling of EUR 6,930; the self-employed (GSVG) pay 18.5% pension, 6.8% health and 1.53% self-employed provision on income up to EUR 8,085 a month.

## How to get residence

EU citizens register and get an Anmeldebescheinigung after three months. Austria has no golden visa. Non-EU people of independent means can apply for a Niederlassungsbewilligung - ausgenommen Erwerbstaetigkeit (the 'Privatier' permit): it needs a free place in a small annual quota (385 in 2025) and monthly income of twice the pension compensation rate (twice EUR 1,308.39 in 2026). Citizenship for 'extraordinary merit' (Section 10(6)) is discretionary and not sold at a fixed price.

- **EU/EEA/Swiss free movement** (Free movement; EU citizens; open): EU citizens register and receive an Anmeldebescheinigung after three months; no permit.
- **Settlement permit except gainful employment (Privatier)** (Passive income / retirement; US citizens, other passports; open): Residence for people of independent means who will not work in Austria, subject to a small annual quota.
- **Citizenship for extraordinary merit (Section 10(6) StbG)** (Citizenship by investment; US citizens, other passports; open): Discretionary citizenship for exceptional achievements in art, science, economy or sport that serve the Republic's interest; large investment alone is not enough.
- **EU Blue Card and Red-White-Red Card (work)** (Work; US citizens, other passports; open): Employer-sponsored permits for skilled workers, key workers and graduates.

Citizenship: Ordinarily 10 years of legal residence (reported, with at least 5 as a permanent resident), German at B2, secure income, no criminal record, and a citizenship test. A reform was announced for 2026; its content was not found. From secondary sources and background knowledge, not an official page.

## Inheritance law and trusts

Children and the spouse are guaranteed half of their intestate share (Pflichtteil), and a will cannot remove it. A foreign national habitually resident in Austria can choose the law of their nationality under the EU Succession Regulation. Austria has no trust law, but a Privatstiftung (private foundation) can be set up under the PSG.

### Austria — family-office tax dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-16
Tax year: 2026
Reviewer: research agent (Claude), unreviewed by a tax adviser

#### Summary

Austria taxes a resident's capital gains on securities and dividends at a flat 27.5% (bank interest 25%) regardless of holding period or size of holding, and real estate gains at 30%. There is no wealth tax and no inheritance or gift tax (abolished 2008), so children inherit a securities portfolio tax-free; only real estate passing within the family bears a stepped real estate transfer tax of 0.5%-3.5% plus a 1.1% registration fee, and larger lifetime gifts must be notified to the tax office. Emigration triggers a deemed sale of financial assets, deferred without interest for moves within the EU/EEA.

#### Residence and eligibility

Unlimited liability with a home (Wohnsitz) in Austria, or habitual abode after a stay of more than six months. Treaty tie-breakers apply for dual residents.

- **EU/EEA/Swiss free movement** (EU): Registration of residence; registration certificate (Anmeldebescheinigung) after three months, with proof of sufficient funds and health insurance.
- **Settlement permit except gainful employment (Niederlassungsbewilligung - ausgenommen Erwerbstätigkeit)** (US, OTHER): For people of independent means who will not work in Austria: proof of substantial regular income, accommodation and health insurance; subject to an annual quota and German language requirements. Austria has no golden visa; citizenship by investment is discretionary and rare. Details not verified in this session.

Standard regime eligibility: A home (Wohnsitz) in Austria or habitual abode (stay of more than six months). Non-EU citizens need a residence permit.

#### Capital gains

Gains on shares, bonds, funds and derivatives are taxed at a flat 27.5% (§ 27a(1) EStG) whatever the holding period and whatever the size of the shareholding, including substantial private-company stakes. Gains on private real estate are taxed at a flat 30% (§ 30a EStG); the main residence is exempt under conditions. There is no annual allowance.

JSON values: headline rate 30% (`0.3`); listed securities domestic 27.5% (`0.275`), foreign 27.5% (`0.275`); private business sale 27.5% (`0.275`); local real estate 30% (`0.3`); foreign real estate 30% (`0.3`).

- **How the JSON numbers were derived:** Securities and private business shares: the 27.5% special rate of § 27a(1) Z 2 EStG applies to all capital gains of private investors, with no surcharges and no holding-period relief. Real estate: 30% flat under § 30a(1) EStG. Foreign real estate: the 30% Austrian rate before treaty relief; most Austrian treaties leave real estate gains to the situs country (Austria then exempts or credits).
- **Holding periods:** None for securities acquired after 2010 (bonds/derivatives after 30 March 2012) or for real estate.
- **Professional-trader risk:** Low risk for securities: capital income of private investors stays under § 27 even with frequent trading. Commercial real estate dealing can turn real estate gains into business income at progressive rates (up to 55%).
- **Foreign assets:** Worldwide taxation at the same rates; foreign accounts declared in the return. Foreign real estate gains depend on the treaty with the situs country.
- **Exemptions and thresholds:** No allowance for capital income. Main residence exempt if lived in for 2 years since acquisition or 5 of the last 10 years. Shares bought before 2011 (bonds/derivatives before 30 March 2012) are grandfathered and tax-free, unless a holding of at least 1%.
- **Eligibility conditions:** Resident individual holding as private assets. Austrian banks withhold the 27.5% as a final tax; foreign accounts are declared in the return at the same rate. Option to be taxed at the regular progressive rate if lower (Regelbesteuerungsoption).
- **Regional differences:** None.
- **Important exceptions / announced changes:** Losses can be offset only within the same year against other capital income (not against bank interest), and cannot be carried forward. Exit tax (§ 27(6) Z 1 EStG): leaving Austria is a deemed sale of all financial assets at market value; for a move to an EU/EEA state the tax is assessed but not collected until actual sale (annual proof required if relevant income exceeds EUR 100,000); a move outside the EU/EEA makes the tax due. Since 1 January 2025 a 30% surcharge applies to gains from land rezoned to building land.
- **Tax year / confidence / verification:** 2026 / high / verified (checked 2026-10-15)
- **Sources:** [EStG 1988 § 27a (special tax rates)](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/NormDokument.wxe?Abfrage=Bundesnormen&Gesetzesnummer=10004570&Paragraf=27a); [EStG 1988 § 30a (special rate on real estate gains)](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/NormDokument.wxe?Abfrage=Bundesnormen&Gesetzesnummer=10004570&Paragraf=30a); [EStG 1988 § 27 (capital income, exit taxation Abs. 6)](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/NormDokument.wxe?Abfrage=Bundesnormen&Gesetzesnummer=10004570&Paragraf=27); [Austria - Individual - Income determination](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/austria/individual/income-determination)

#### Dividends

Dividends, domestic or foreign, are taxed at a flat 27.5%, withheld as a final tax by Austrian banks or declared in the return for foreign accounts. Foreign withholding tax is credited up to 15% via the Austrian custodian bank, regardless of the treaty.

JSON values: headline 27.5% (`0.275`); domestic 27.5% (`0.275`); foreign gross 27.5% (`0.275`); foreign tax relief: credit.

- **How the JSON numbers were derived:** 27.5% special rate (§ 27a(1) Z 2 EStG); foreign dividends taxed at 27.5% of the gross amount with a credit for foreign withholding tax of at most 15%.
- **Outbound withholding:** 27.5% KESt on dividends paid to individuals; 23% to corporations; reduced by treaties and to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive.
- **Treaty network:** About 90 income tax treaties including the US and all EU states.
- **Holding-company notes:** Dividends from Austrian and qualifying foreign participations held by an Austrian company are generally exempt; portfolio holdings in a GmbH are taxed at 23% and distributions again at 27.5%. Two structures are modelled as their own regimes (see Holding structures). Privatstiftung: from 2026 it pays 27.5% interim tax (Zwischensteuer, § 22(2) KStG) on realised gains, interest, derivatives and private real estate sales (§ 13(3) KStG), but domestic dividends and dividends from comparable foreign companies resident in a state with comprehensive administrative assistance are exempt (§ 13(2) with § 10(1) Z 1, 5-7 KStG); distributions (Zuwendungen) bear 27.5% KESt, and distributions in excess of the year's separately taxed income earn a credit of the interim tax (§ 24(5) KStG); the endowment bears 3.5% foundation entry tax from 1 January 2026. Asset-management GmbH: 23% corporate tax on realised gains (24% above EUR 1,000,000 of income from 2028), portfolio dividends exempt under § 10 KStG, and 27.5% KESt when it distributes. Austria has no inheritance tax to avoid, so neither structure saves tax at death.
- **Exemptions and thresholds:** No allowance.
- **Eligibility conditions:** Private shareholder; option for progressive taxation if lower.
- **Regional differences:** None.
- **Important exceptions / announced changes:** Foreign withholding above 15% must be reclaimed from the source country.
- **Tax year / confidence / verification:** 2026 / high / verified (checked 2026-10-15)
- **Sources:** [Austria - Individual - Income determination](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/austria/individual/income-determination); [EStG 1988 § 27a (special tax rates)](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/NormDokument.wxe?Abfrage=Bundesnormen&Gesetzesnummer=10004570&Paragraf=27a)

#### Inheritance and gifts to children

No inheritance or gift tax since 1 August 2008. A securities portfolio passes to children tax-free. Real estate passing within the family bears real estate transfer tax on the property value at stepped rates (0.5% on the first EUR 250,000, 2% on the next EUR 150,000, 3.5% above), plus a 1.1% land-registry fee. Lifetime gifts of cash, securities and company shares above EUR 50,000 a year between relatives must be notified within three months (fine up to 10% of value if deliberately not reported).

JSON values: basis `none`, bracket mode `marginal`, children `fully_exempt`, exemption EUR 0, marginal brackets above the exemption: none; other transfer costs 0% (`0`); gifts same as inheritance: True; tax-free after years: None.

- **Gifts:** No gift tax. Gifts above EUR 50,000 a year between relatives must be notified to the tax office within three months; gifts of real estate bear real estate transfer tax (0.5%/2%/3.5% stepped) plus 1.1% registration fee.
- **Business relief:** Not needed for inheritance tax. Real estate transfer tax on business transfers has a EUR 900,000 allowance (from general knowledge, not verified). Covers listed portfolios: no.
- **Foreign assets:** No tax on foreign assets either; foreign inheritance/gift tax of the situs country may apply.
- **Domicile / residence rules:** Not relevant; there is no inheritance tax. The gift notification duty applies if donor or recipient lives in Austria.
- **Exemptions and thresholds:** Gift notification duty (§ 121a BAO): gifts between relatives up to EUR 50,000 per donor and recipient in one year are exempt from notification; real estate is notified under the real estate transfer tax act instead.
- **Eligibility conditions:** None; applies to all.
- **Regional differences:** None.
- **Important exceptions / announced changes:** Transfers to a private foundation bear 3.5% foundation entry tax (Stiftungseingangssteuer; 2.5% until 31 December 2025, raised from 1 January 2026 by the Budgetbegleitgesetz 2025). Inherited real estate: real estate transfer tax (stepped, family rates, aggregated over 5 years) plus 1.1% registration fee on the property value (Grundstückswert). Political proposals to reintroduce inheritance tax are not enacted.
- **Tax year / confidence / verification:** 2026 / high / partially_verified (checked 2026-10-15)
- **Sources:** [Austria - Individual - Other taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/austria/individual/other-taxes); [Schenkungsmeldung](https://www.bmf.gv.at/themen/steuern/steuern-von-a-bis-z/schenkungsmeldung.html); [Grunderwerbsteuer Österreich 2026 - der vollständige Überblick](https://brandauer-rechtsanwaelte.at/2026/05/24/grunderwerbsteuer-oesterreich-2026-ueberblick/)

#### Wealth tax

Austria has no wealth tax (net wealth tax not levied since 1994).

JSON type: `none`.

- **Property tax (not a wealth tax):** Municipal Grundsteuer based on the old assessed value (Einheitswert), at most 1% of assessed value; usually small.
- **Important exceptions / announced changes:** Reintroducing wealth and inheritance taxes is a recurring SPÖ demand; the 2025 coalition (ÖVP-SPÖ-NEOS) budget measures did not include one.

#### Holding structures

Two structures are modelled as separate regimes of type `structure`. Austria has no inheritance or gift tax, so neither saves tax at death; their use is deferral of the personal 27.5% tax (and, for the foundation, control and succession planning).

##### Private foundation (Privatstiftung, `privatstiftung`)

The family endows an Austrian Privatstiftung with the portfolio; the foundation is its own legal person (§ 1 PSG) and owns it.

- **Gains:** 27.5% interim tax (Zwischensteuer) on realised gains on shares, bonds and derivatives, on interest and crypto income (§ 13(3) Z 1 KStG) and on private real estate sales (§ 13(3) Z 2), rate § 22(2) KStG from calendar year 2026 (23% in 2024 and 2025). Unrealised gains are not taxed. JSON: 0.275 for listed, private business sale and local real estate; foreign real estate null (not researched). Holdings of at least 1% in the last five years can be rolled over (§ 13(4) KStG), not relevant for a diversified portfolio.
- **Dividends:** exempt at the foundation: domestic dividends under § 10(1) Z 1 KStG and foreign dividends under § 13(2) with § 10(1) Z 5-7 (comparable corporation in the EU/EEA or a state with comprehensive administrative assistance; denied where the payer can deduct the dividend, § 10(4)). Fund distributions are not covered. JSON: 0, foreign tax credit `exemption`, so foreign withholding is a final cost.
- **Payout:** Zuwendungen are capital income of the beneficiary (§ 27(5) Z 7 EStG) taxed at 27.5% KESt (§ 27a(1) Z 2 EStG), withheld by the foundation (`payout_tax_rate` 0.275). The interim tax is credited when distributions exceed the year's separately taxed income (§ 24(5) KStG, from 2026); the engine charges it in full and then charges KESt on the payout.
- **Entry and set-up:** 3.5% foundation entry tax on assets endowed from 1 January 2026 (2.5% before; 25% where the foundation is not comparable to a Privatstiftung or documents are not filed), about EUR 1.05 million on EUR 30 million. Minimum endowment EUR 70,000 (§ 4 PSG); board of at least three members, two habitually resident in the EU/EEA (§ 15 PSG); a court-appointed auditor (§ 20 PSG). Running and set-up costs are null in the JSON: no Austrian source with euro amounts was found.
- **Exit:** on dissolution the remaining assets pass to the final beneficiary in the deed and are taxed as Zuwendungen at 27.5% (§ 34 PSG; treatment of the endowed capital not verified). The founder's death changes nothing.

##### Asset-management GmbH (`vv_gmbh`)

The family holds the portfolio through an Austrian GmbH owned privately.

- **Gains:** 23% corporate tax on realised gains on shares below the 10% international-participation threshold (§ 22(1) KStG); unrealised gains are not taxed. From 2028 the rate is 24% on income above EUR 1,000,000 a year (BGBl. I Nr. 62/2026); the engine takes 23% throughout. JSON: 0.23, foreign real estate null.
- **Dividends:** exempt: domestic (§ 10(1) Z 1 KStG) and from comparable foreign corporations in qualifying states (§ 10(1) Z 5-7). JSON: 0, `exemption`; foreign withholding is a final cost.
- **Payout:** 27.5% KESt withheld by the GmbH (`payout_tax_rate` 0.275). Repayments of contributed capital and shareholder loans are not modelled.
- **Running:** minimum corporate tax of EUR 500 a year is due even in a loss year (EUR 125 per quarter, § 24(4) KStG), creditable against later tax. Running and set-up costs are null in the JSON. Liquidation proceeds were not researched; on a shareholder's death the shares pass without Austrian inheritance tax.

#### Company taxation (kept separate from personal tax)

Headline CIT rate in JSON: 23% (`0.23`).

Corporate income tax 23%. Dividends from domestic and qualifying foreign participations are generally exempt; international participations of at least 10% held for one year are also exempt on gains (unless opted out). Distributions to individuals bear 27.5% withholding.

Special zones: None.
