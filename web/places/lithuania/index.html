Tax on investments, inheritance and wealth

2026 reform: most income progressive 20/25/32%, but dividends and gains on shares held over 5 years stay at a flat 15%; real estate gains exempt after 5 years. Children fully exempt from inheritance tax; no gift tax; no wealth tax, but a new progressive tax on residential real estate above thresholds.

  • Ordinary tax residence: Ordinary Lithuanian tax residence; no special regime for new wealthy arrivals.

Income tax for employees and remote workers

From 1 Jan 2026 most income is aggregated: 20% up to 36 average wages (EUR 83,237.40), 25% to 60 average wages (EUR 138,729), 32% above; some income is taxed at a flat 15%. NPD (non-taxable allowance): maximum EUR 747 a month at pay up to the EUR 1,153 minimum wage, reduced by 0.49 x (income - minimum wage), ending at EUR 2,677.49 a month; not modelled (personal_allowance 0). Employee social security 19.5% including 6.98% health, withheld; above 60 average wages (EUR 138,729) only the 6.98% health applies (recorded cap EUR 138,729 for the capped part).

Inheritance law and trusts

Children, the spouse and dependent parents are guaranteed half of their intestate share. A foreign resident can choose the law of their nationality (EU Succession Regulation art. 22). Lithuania has no common-law trust; whether foundations can hold family wealth was not confirmed.

Research notes

Lithuania — family-office tax dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-16 Tax year: 2026 Reviewer: research agent (Claude), unreviewed by a tax adviser

Summary

2026 reform: most income progressive 20/25/32%, but dividends and gains on shares held over 5 years stay at a flat 15%; real estate gains exempt after 5 years. Children fully exempt from inheritance tax; no gift tax; no wealth tax, but a new progressive tax on residential real estate above thresholds.

Residence and eligibility

  • Tax residence: Resident if: permanent place of residence in Lithuania; centre of personal, social or economic interests in Lithuania; present 183+ days in the tax period; or 280+ days over consecutive tax periods with at least 90 in one; or certain state-paid citizens abroad.
  • EU/EEA free movement (EU): Register residence as EU citizen.
  • Temporary residence permit (non-EU) (US, OTHER): Permit on grounds such as business/investment or employment under the Law on the Legal Status of Aliens; investment thresholds not verified in this research.

Capital gains

  • Rule in force: From 2026, gains on securities held 5 years or less are added to total income and taxed at 20/25/32% (32% above about EUR 138,700). Gains on shares/units held more than 5 years and acquired outside an investment account are taxed separately at a flat 15%. Real estate held 5 years or more is exempt (was 10 years).
  • Exemptions/thresholds: Gains on financial instruments up to EUR 500/yr exempt; other capital gains up to EUR 2,500/yr. Main home in the EEA exempt after 2 years' declared residence (or reinvestment within 1 year).
  • Conditions: 15% flat rate (PIT Law art. 6(6)(3)): income from selling shares, parts or units (akcijos, dalys, pajai) that were acquired outside an investment account more than 5 years before the sale is taxed at 15% and not added to annual income. A reorganisation or restructuring of the issuer does not restart the 5-year period (VMI clarification reported by KPMG, March 2026). Bonds and other financial instruments are not covered and are taxed at the progressive rates. Investment account (since 2025): withdrawals above contributions taxed at 15%; reinvested returns not taxed. Accounts in EEA/OECD/treaty countries; individual (not joint) only.
  • Holding periods: Shares/units/interests: more than 5 years -> 15% flat. Real estate: 5 years -> exempt (10 years before 2026).
  • Foreign assets: Worldwide gains taxed; relief for foreign tax under treaties, capped at Lithuanian tax.
  • Trader risk: Individual-activity (self-employed) income has its own regime (effective 5-20% up to EUR 42,500, then progressive); frequent trading recharacterisation not researched.
  • Exceptions, reforms, announced changes: 2026 reform (adopted 2025, in force 1 Jan 2026): most income is aggregated and taxed at 20% up to 36 average wages (EUR 83,237), 25% up to 60 AW (EUR 138,729) and 32% above; previously 15%/20% for capital gains. Dividends, gains on shares held more than 5 years (outside an investment account) and investment-account withdrawals stay at a separate flat 15%.
  • Confidence: high, partially verified.
  • JSON: headline 32%; listed domestic 15%, listed foreign 15%, private business 15%, local real estate 0%, foreign real estate 0%. Basis: Assumes holding beyond the 5-year period: shares 15% flat; real estate exempt after 5 years. Short-term gains (<=5 years) on a large amount: 32% marginal. Headline 32% = top aggregated rate.

Conditions for the 15% rate re-checked 2026-10-15 (KPMG Lithuania, March 2026, summarising VMI on art. 6(6)(3)): shares/parts/units acquired outside an investment account more than 5 years before sale; 15%, not aggregated; reorganisation does not restart the clock. Bonds excluded. Confidence raised to high.

Dividends

  • Rule in force: Dividends, Lithuanian and foreign, are taxed at a flat 15% PIT, separately from other income (not aggregated). Foreign WHT is credited up to the Lithuanian tax and the treaty rate.
  • Exceptions, reforms, announced changes: 2026 reform (adopted 2025, in force 1 Jan 2026): most income is aggregated and taxed at 20% up to 36 average wages (EUR 83,237), 25% up to 60 AW (EUR 138,729) and 32% above; previously 15%/20% for capital gains. Dividends, gains on shares held more than 5 years (outside an investment account) and investment-account withdrawals stay at a separate flat 15%.
  • Confidence: high, verified.
  • JSON: headline 15%, domestic 15%, foreign gross 15%, relief method 'credit'. Basis: Flat 15% on gross dividend; a 15% US treaty WHT would be fully credited, leaving little or no Lithuanian tax.
  • Outbound withholding: Dividends to non-resident companies: 17% WHT from 2026 (was 15%), 0% under the 10%/12-month participation exemption, reduced by treaty. Dividends to individuals carry 15% PIT withholding.
  • Treaties: Broad network across EU/OECD and the US; treaties with Russia and Belarus cease to apply from 1 Jan 2026 (PwC). Exact count not verified.
  • Holding companies: Lithuania taxes company profits annually (17% from 2026), not on distribution; participation exemption for qualifying dividends and share gains.

Inheritance and gifts to children

  • Rule in force: Inheritance tax exists (5% up to EUR 150,000, 10% above, EUR 3,000 threshold) but children, spouse, parents, grandchildren, grandparents and siblings are fully exempt. There is no gift tax; gifts are a PIT matter and gifts from close family are exempt.
  • Exemptions/thresholds: Children (incl. adopted) fully exempt from inheritance tax.
  • Foreign assets: Residents' worldwide inheritances are in scope for non-exempt heirs; foreigners only on Lithuanian-registered property.
  • Confidence: high, partially verified.
  • Gifts: No gift tax (PwC). Gifts are income for PIT purposes, but gifts from close family members are exempt (general rule; not re-verified).
  • Business relief: Not needed for children.
  • Domicile/scope: Liability depends on heir's residence; irrelevant for children (exempt).
  • JSON: basis 'beneficiary', children 'fully_exempt', exemption 0 EUR, brackets none, other transfer cost 0%.
  • Surviving spouse: exempt from inheritance tax regardless of value.

Wealth tax

  • Rule in force: No net wealth tax and no recurring levy on financial assets. From 2026 real estate tax became progressive on residential property (see property note), which is a property tax, not a wealth tax.
  • Confidence: high, verified.
  • Property taxes (not a wealth tax): From 2026: primary residence taxed only above EUR 450,000 per owner (EUR 900,000 for two co-owners) at 0.1-1% set by the municipality; other residential property taxed progressively 0.2%-1.0% above EUR 50,000 (1.0% above EUR 1,000,000); commercial 0.5-3% plus a 0.2% defence component.
  • JSON: type 'none'.

Special regimes

None. No special tax regime for newly arriving wealthy individuals was found; only ordinary residence (JSON regime 'standard').

Company taxation (kept separate from personal tax)

  • Headline CIT 17%. Standard CIT rose from 16% to 17% from 2026 (reduced rate 7%; 0% for the first two years for small new companies). Annual profit taxation. Participation exemption for dividends (10%/12 months). New loss-utilisation limits on reorganisations.

Open questions

  • Whether ETF units of foreign (e.g. Irish UCITS) funds count as 'units' (pajai) for the 5-year 15% rule is assumed (yes), not confirmed in a VMI text.
  • Average-wage thresholds are preliminary (KPMG gives EUR 82,962/138,270; PwC EUR 83,237/138,729).
  • PIT exemption for gifts from close relatives not confirmed in a source opened during this research.
  • Notary fees for the certificate of inheritance not verified (assumed small).
  • Holding structures (2026-10-16): a Lithuanian UAB holding company was considered and not added as a regime. Its profit is taxed every year at 17%, not on distribution; gains on shares are exempt only for a stake above 10% held at least two years (PwC), so a diversified portfolio's gains and non-EEA dividends would be taxed at 17% inside the company, then dividends again at 15% on payout. It is never better than direct holding, and no source shows advisers recommending it for a listed portfolio. A private foundation or trust was not researched.
  • Health insurance (PSD, 6.98%) on dividends and gains of an individual was searched for and not found in any source read; the file assumes none applies to these items.
  • Needs an engine change (see data/recipes/LTU.json): the investment account (withdrawals above contributions taxed at 15%).
