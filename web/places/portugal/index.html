Tax on investments, inheritance and wealth

Residents pay a flat 28% on capital gains and dividends (35% if from blacklisted jurisdictions); since 2024, 10%/20%/30% of gains on listed securities and fund units held 2/5/8+ years is excluded (effective 19.6% after 8 years). Children, spouse and parents are exempt from the 10% stamp duty on inheritances and gifts; only a 0.8% stamp duty on gifts of Portuguese real estate remains. No general wealth tax, but AIMI (from 0.7%) on Portuguese real estate above EUR 600,000 per person. The IFICI regime ('NHR 2.0') exempts most foreign income but only for people working in qualifying high-value activities: it does not help a purely passive investor, and the old NHR is closed to new arrivals.

  • Ordinary tax residence: Worldwide taxation. Investment income and gains taxed at special flat rates (28%), with an option to aggregate at progressive rates (12.5%-48% plus 2.5%/5% solidarity surcharge above EUR 80,000/250,000).
  • IFICI ('NHR 2.0', scientific research and innovation incentive): 10-year regime: 20% flat rate on Portuguese employment/business income from eligible high-value activities, and exemption of most foreign-source income (including dividends, interest and capital gains) except pensions and income from blacklisted jurisdictions. Requires carrying out an eligible activity in an eligible entity: a purely passive investor or family-office principal without such a role does not qualify. The old NHR is closed to people becoming resident after 2024.

Income tax for employees and remote workers

Resident income tax is progressive from 12.5% to 48% on taxable income, plus a solidarity surcharge of 2.5% above EUR 80,000 and 5% above EUR 250,000, which gives top marginal rates of 50.5% (EUR 86,634 to 250,000) and 53%. Employees pay 11% social security with no stated cap. Self-employed pay 21.4% on a base of 70% of service income (effective 14.98% of income).

How to get residence

EU citizens only register after 3 months. Non-EU citizens (US and others) can use the D7 (passive income of about EUR 11,040 a year), the D8 (remote income of EUR 44,160 a year), or the Golden Visa from EUR 250,000 (donation) or EUR 500,000 (fund). Property no longer qualifies.

Inheritance law and trusts

A spouse, children and ascendants are guaranteed a legitimate share, between a half and two thirds of the estate. A foreign resident can choose the law of their nationality (EU Succession Regulation art. 22). Portugal has no general trust law, and its foundations are for social-interest purposes.

Research notes

Portugal — family-office tax dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15 Tax year: 2026 Reviewer: research agent (Claude), unreviewed by a tax adviser

Summary

  • Capital gains and dividends: flat 28% (35% if from blacklisted jurisdictions). Gains on listed securities held 8+ years are taxed at an effective 19.6%.
  • Inheritances and gifts: children are exempt from the 10% stamp duty. Only a 0.8% stamp duty on gifts of Portuguese real estate remains.
  • Wealth tax: none in general. AIMI applies to Portuguese residential property above EUR 600,000.
  • IFICI ("NHR 2.0") requires a qualifying high-value activity, so it does not help a passive investor. The old NHR is closed.

Residence and eligibility

  • Resident if more than 183 days in any 12-month period, or if holding a habitual residence intended as the primary home. Split-year treatment applies (PwC).
  • EU citizens move freely.
  • Non-EU citizens: D7 visa or Golden Visa. Real estate routes for the Golden Visa were removed in 2023. Amounts are not verified.

Capital gains

  • Shares and other securities: 28% flat.
  • Holding-period exclusions on listed securities and fund units (PwC, "2024 changes"):
  • 10% excluded if held 2-5 years.
  • 20% excluded if held 5-8 years.
  • 30% excluded if held 8 years or more.
  • Holdings of less than 365 days must be aggregated at progressive rates if taxable income is EUR 86,634 or more.
  • Unlisted micro and small company shares: 50% of the gain is taxed.
  • Real estate: 50% of the gain is taxed at progressive rates (top 48% plus a 5% solidarity surcharge).
  • Decree-Law 97/2026: reinvestment relief for residential letting.
  • JSON: listed securities 0.28 x 0.7 = 0.196; private business 0.28; real estate 0.5 x 53% = 0.265.

Dividends

  • 28% flat, or optional aggregation at progressive rates.
  • 35% if from blacklisted jurisdictions.
  • Foreign tax credit capped at the treaty rate.
  • JSON: 0.28 with credit.

Inheritance and gifts to children

Código do Imposto do Selo, read on the Portal das Finanças:

  • Tabela Geral verba 1.2: 10% on free acquisitions (inheritances and gifts).
  • Art. 6(1)(e): the spouse or de facto partner, descendants and ascendants are exempt from verba 1.2.
  • Verba 1.1: 0.8% on acquisitions of real estate for consideration or by gift. It still applies to gifts of Portuguese real estate to children. On its wording it does not cover inheritance.
  • Scope: Portuguese-situs assets only (general knowledge of art. 4, unverified).
  • JSON: beneficiary basis, children_treatment exempt_with_conditions, brackets [], other cost 0.
  • Surviving spouse (spouse_treatment fully_exempt): Spouse is exempt from the 10% stamp duty on free transfers (Código do Imposto do Selo Art. 6(1)(e)); the 0.8% charge on Portuguese real estate is not covered by the verified exemption.

Wealth tax

  • No net wealth tax (PwC).
  • AIMI: 0.7% for individuals (PwC). The EUR 600,000 allowance and the 1% / 1.5% tiers above EUR 1M / EUR 2M come from general knowledge.
  • Modelled as limited, on local real estate, with exempt 600,000 and brackets 0.007 / 0.01 / 0.015.
  • IMI is 0.3-0.45% (municipal property tax).

Special regimes

  • IFICI (art. 58-A EBF, 2024 State Budget):
  • 10 years.
  • 20% on Portuguese income from eligible activities.
  • Foreign-source income is generally exempt, except pensions and blacklisted sources (PwC).
  • Requires an eligible activity in an eligible entity. It is not available to a purely passive investor.
  • Whether foreign securities gains are exempt is unconfirmed; the old NHR taxed them at 28%.
  • Old NHR: only for people resident by the end of 2024.

Holding structures

No holding structure is scored for Portugal. A company that only manages securities or other assets (sociedade de simples administração de bens, art. 6 n.º 4 b) CIRC) is tax-transparent under art. 6 n.º 1 c) CIRC when:

  • a family group (relatives up to the 4th degree) owns the majority of the capital for more than 183 days; or
  • it has five or fewer shareholders.

Its profit is attributed to the shareholders and taxed as their own income every year, so it cannot defer tax (Autoridade Tributária, CIRC art. 6). The participation exemption needs a stake of at least 10% and would not cover the portfolio anyway. How transparent income is taxed in the shareholders' hands was not verified.
