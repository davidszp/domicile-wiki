Tax on investments, inheritance and wealth

For a foreign family Malta works on the remittance basis: residents who are not Malta-domiciled pay no Maltese tax on foreign capital gains even if brought to Malta, and on foreign dividends only if remitted, subject to a EUR 5,000 annual minimum tax when unremitted foreign income is EUR 35,000 or more. The Global Residence Programme (non-EU) taxes remitted foreign income at 15% with a EUR 15,000 minimum; from 1 Jan 2027 it is replaced by the Individual Tax Programme with a EUR 35,000 minimum (applications by 31 Dec 2026 keep the old terms until 2031). No inheritance, gift or wealth tax, but duty on documents of 5% on inherited Maltese real estate and about 2% on Maltese shares; foreign assets carry no duty.

  • Ordinarily resident and Malta-domiciled (worldwide basis): Worldwide taxation at progressive rates up to 35%. Applies to individuals both domiciled and ordinarily resident in Malta (Maltese by domicile of origin, a foreigner who acquires a Maltese domicile of choice, or a person married to a domiciled resident). A newly arriving foreign family is normally taxed under the non_dom regime instead.
  • Resident non-domiciled (remittance basis): Default status for a foreigner who becomes ordinarily resident without acquiring a Maltese domicile: Malta-source income and gains taxed; foreign income taxed only if remitted to Malta; foreign capital gains never taxed, even if remitted. A EUR 5,000 minimum tax applies if (with spouse) unremitted foreign income in the preceding year was EUR 35,000 or more. No time limit.
  • Global Residence Programme / Residence Programme (15% on remitted income): Special tax status: foreign-source income remitted to Malta taxed at a flat 15% (double tax relief available), minimum annual tax EUR 15,000; foreign capital gains not taxed (non-dom basis); other income at 35%. GRP is for non-EU/EEA/Swiss nationals and gives a residence permit; The Residence Programme (TRP) is the EU/EEA/Swiss equivalent. Replaced from 2027 by the Individual Tax Programme Rules (L.N. 195 of 2026, read 2026-10-15): 15% on foreign income received in Malta by the beneficiary, spouse and dependent children; minimum tax EUR 35,000 for global resident and EU/EEA/Swiss resident status (EUR 15,000 for retired pensioner status); property threshold EUR 700,000 purchase or EUR 14,000/year rent; EUR 8,500 non-refundable application fee; status for 5 years, renewable. Statuses granted or applications received by 31 Dec 2026 keep the old rules until 31 Dec 2031.

Income tax for employees and remote workers

Single resident rates for 2026: 0% to EUR 12,000, 15% to 16,000, 25% to 60,000 and 35% above. Employees pay 10% social security, self-employed 15%, both capped at about EUR 29,084 of annual income (maximum weekly contribution EUR 55.93 and EUR 83.89).

How to get residence

EU citizens only register. Non-EU citizens (US and others) can get permanent residence through the MPRP (EUR 99,000 in government payments plus a EUR 375,000 property or EUR 14,000 rent) or a Nomad permit at EUR 42,000 a year. Citizenship by investment ended in 2025. The Global Residence Programme (tax status) is replaced on 1 January 2027 by the Individual Tax Programme with much higher thresholds.

Inheritance law and trusts

Children are guaranteed a third of the estate (a half with five or more children). A foreign resident can choose the law of their nationality (EU Succession Regulation art. 22). Malta has its own trust law, has been a Hague Trusts Convention state since 1996, and allows private foundations.

Research notes

Malta — family-office tax dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-16 Tax year: 2026 Reviewer: research agent (Claude), unreviewed by a tax adviser

Summary

A foreign family resident in Malta is normally ordinarily resident but not domiciled. Malta then taxes it on the remittance basis:

  • Foreign capital gains are never taxed, even if remitted.
  • Foreign dividends are taxed only if remitted.
  • A minimum tax of EUR 5,000 applies where unremitted foreign income is EUR 35,000 or more.

The Global Residence Programme (GRP, for non-EU nationals) and The Residence Programme (TRP, for EU nationals) give 15% on remitted foreign income with a EUR 15,000 minimum. From 2027 they are replaced by the Individual Tax Programme (ITP) with a EUR 35,000 minimum. Applications by 31 Dec 2026 keep the old terms until 2031.

There is no inheritance, gift or wealth tax. Duty on documents applies to Maltese real estate (5%) and Maltese shares (about 2%) on death or gift.

Residence and eligibility

  • Ordinary residence is tested on the facts; 183 or more days in a year generally makes a person resident.
  • Domicile follows common-law principles: a foreigner stays non-domiciled unless they acquire a Maltese domicile of choice.
  • EU citizens: free movement, with TRP available.
  • Non-EU citizens: GRP (property of EUR 275,000 or rent of EUR 9,600 a year until end-2026; EUR 700,000 or EUR 14,000 under the ITP), or the Malta Permanent Residence Programme (MPRP). MPRP amounts after Legal Notice 146/2025 are not verified.

Capital gains

  • Domiciled residents (standard): chargeable gains on shares, property, business, IP and partnership interests are taxed at marginal rates up to 35%.
  • Shares listed on the Malta Stock Exchange are exempt. Shares listed on foreign exchanges recognised by the Commissioner are exempt too, where the gain is capital in nature (PwC income determination; KPMG Malta on the Commissioner's November 2022 list: WFE members, NYSE, ESMA-registered EEA regulated markets). Units in some collective investment schemes may be excluded. Statute (art. 5(1) ITA) not read.
  • Maltese property pays a final tax of 8% of the transfer value (10% if acquired before 2004).
  • JSON: domestic listed 0, foreign listed 0 (was 0.35, corrected 2026-10-16), private business 0.35, local real estate 0.08 (on value), foreign real estate 0.35 (assumed).
  • Non-dom: foreign gains are not taxed, even if remitted (PwC). JSON foreign = 0.

Dividends

  • Full imputation: dividends from Maltese companies carry a credit for the 35% corporate tax, so no further personal tax is due. JSON domestic 0.
  • Foreign dividends:
  • Domiciled residents pay up to 35% with a credit for foreign tax.
  • Non-doms pay only on remitted amounts. JSON 0, which assumes the dividends are not remitted, plus the EUR 5,000 minimum.
  • GRP/TRP pay a flat 15% on remitted amounts, with a minimum of EUR 15,000.
  • Malta levies no withholding tax on outbound dividends (general knowledge, not verified in this research).

Inheritance and gifts to children

  • No inheritance, estate or gift tax (PwC: "There are no inheritance, estate, or gift taxes in Malta").
  • The Duty on Documents and Transfers Act (Cap. 364) charges duty on Maltese assets:
  • 5% on immovable property inherited or donated.
  • 2% on shares in Maltese companies (higher for property companies).
  • Reliefs:
  • No duty on a dwelling passing from a parent to descendants (conditions apply).
  • An inherited dwelling occupied by the heirs pays 3.5%, up to EUR 400,000 from 28 Oct 2025.
  • A donation of residential property from parent to child is free on the first EUR 250,000, then 3.5%.
  • Gifts of marketable securities and family business premises to close relatives pay 1.5% until 31 Dec 2026.
  • Foreign assets pay no duty.
  • JSON: basis none, children_treatment taxed_low (duty only on Maltese situs assets), other_transfer_cost_rate 0 (mostly foreign portfolio).

Wealth tax

None. There is also no annual property tax.

Special regimes

  • non_dom (remittance basis): indefinite (duration_note): the remittance basis applies while the person is resident but not domiciled in Malta (Income Tax Act, Cap. 123, art. 4(1)); it ends only if Maltese domicile is acquired. Minimum tax EUR 5,000, modelled as lump_sum.
  • The 0% on foreign dividends assumes they are not remitted to Malta; remitted foreign income is taxed up to 35% with credit. Foreign capital gains are untaxed even if remitted.
  • grp (GRP/TRP): 15% on remitted foreign income, minimum EUR 15,000.
  • Individual Tax Programme Rules, 2026 (Legal Notice 195 of 2026, gazetted 14 July 2026, in force 1 Jan 2027): the 15% rate stays.
  • New minimum: EUR 35,000 for Global Resident and EU/EEA/Swiss Resident status, EUR 15,000 for retired pensioners, EUR 20,000 for UN pensioners.
  • Property: EUR 700,000 purchase or EUR 14,000 a year rent. Status lasts 5 years and is renewable (fee EUR 8,500).
  • Rule 3(3): status granted or applied for by 31 Dec 2026 continues until 31 Dec 2031; new statuses run 5 years and are renewable for further 5-year periods, so duration_years is null with a duration_note.
  • Read in the L.N. 195/2026 PDF on 2026-10-15: rule 3(1) categories (global resident; EU/EEA/Swiss resident; retired pensioner; UN pensioner), rule 3(2) fee EUR 8,500, rule 5(1) 15% on foreign income received in Malta by the beneficiary, spouse and dependent children, minimum EUR 35,000 for the first two categories and EUR 15,000 for retired pensioners, payable in full in the first and last year; rule 5(3) other income at 35%.
  • Recipe modelling (2026-10-16): the JSON minimum is EUR 35,000, the long-run ITP figure (rule 5(1) proviso, one minimum for the beneficiary, spouse and dependent children); the first five years under the old terms (EUR 15,000) are not modelled. With 15% foreign withholding the Maltese 15% is relieved in full (art. 74), so only the minimum is due.
  • The 15% foreign_gross_rate assumes foreign dividends are remitted; unremitted dividends are untaxed but the minimum tax is due regardless.
  • For a family that does not remit income, the plain non-dom basis (EUR 5,000 minimum) is cheaper. GRP mainly provides the residence permit for non-EU nationals.

Holding structures

No holding structure is scored for Malta.

  • A Maltese company pays 35%. The shareholder refund (6/7 for ordinary income, 5/7 for passive interest and royalties, 2/3 where double tax relief is claimed; Legal 500/PKF) arises only when profits are distributed, so retained gains bear 35%.
  • The FITWI election introduced in 2025 is a 15% final tax with a five-year lock-in (PwC Malta).
  • Portfolio stakes of at least 5%, or an investment of about EUR 1.2m held 183 days, can be participating holdings with exemption, subject to anti-abuse conditions; not modelled.
  • An arriving family is normally non-domiciled. Under the non_dom regime, foreign capital gains are never taxed and foreign dividends kept abroad are untaxed (EUR 5,000 minimum tax), so a company would add tax rather than save it. Only Malta-domiciled residents (the standard regime) could gain, and they are not the target family.

Company taxation (kept separate from personal tax)

  • Corporate tax is 35%, with refunds to shareholders under the imputation system (6/7 for trading income), for an effective rate of about 5%.
  • A participation exemption applies.
