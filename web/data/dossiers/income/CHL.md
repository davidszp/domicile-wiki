# Chile — earned income, nomad and expat dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026

## Summary

Chilean residents pay the progressive Second Category (employment) tax of 0% to 40% on monthly income above CLP 977,940 (annualised from the November 2026 monthly table). Employees contribute 10% pension plus AFP commission, 7% health and unemployment insurance up to 90 UF a month. Honorarios (self-employed) issue invoices with a 15.25% retention and contribute annually on 80% of fees.

- Self-employed notes: Honorarios workers retain 15.25% of each invoice in 2026 (rising 0.75 points a year to 17% in 2028) as an advance of tax and contributions, and settle in the annual Operacion Renta. Contributions are due on 80% of annual fees (partial cover) or 100% (full cover); people issuing under five minimum monthly incomes (CLP 2,767,765) in the previous year, and some older workers, are exempt. The 0.1636 rate is 80% x (10% pension + 7% health + 1.45% AFP commission + 2.00% SIS), using ProVida's published July 2026 commission and SIS for independent affiliates (the highest-commission AFP; cheaper AFPs lower it); occupational-accident cover (ISL) is not added. The cap converts 90 UF a month to gross fees (UF CLP 40,828.47 on 3 July 2026).
- Local tax note: No municipal income tax. Brackets are the November 2026 Second Category monthly table x 12; the monthly thresholds move with the UTM during the year (January exempt limit CLP 941,638.50).
- Confidence: medium; verification: partially_verified.

## Expat regimes

**First three years of residence - only Chilean-source income taxed (art. 3 Ley sobre Impuesto a la Renta)** (expat_regime). A foreigner who takes up domicile or residence in Chile is taxed only on Chilean-source income during the first three years from entry; foreign-source income is outside the tax. The Regional Director may extend the period in qualified cases. Art. 10 of the same law makes income from activities carried out in Chile Chilean-source, so a remote worker's fees or salary for work done while living in Chile are not covered.

- Treatment: exempt; duration: 3 years. Three years from entry into Chile; extension possible by the Regional Director in qualified cases (not modelled).
- Covers work physically performed in the country (covers_local_work): no.
- Maximum income: none.

## Residence routes

Chile has no digital nomad visa. EU, US and many other nationals can stay as tourists for 90 days, extendable by 90, and may work remotely for foreign employers (not for Chilean clients). Longer stays need a residence permit: a rentista visa needs passive income (freelance income does not qualify, per the source), and work for a Chilean employer needs a work visa. Skilled employees of Chilean employers receive a temporary residence tied to a work contract.

## Open questions

- Rentista income threshold (USD 1,000-1,500 a month) is from a guide and not verified.
- No nomad visa found; the art. 3 three-year rule is recorded as the only relevant regime.
- 2-bedroom rent not found (Cuanto Me Cuesta, Portal Inmobiliario, Toctoc, Expatistan and Global Property Guide gave only 1-bedroom figures or were not reachable).
- Employee rate 0.1905 = 10% pension + 7% health + 1.45% AFP commission (ProVida, July 2026; the highest of the AFPs, cheaper ones lower, other AFPs' commissions not opened) + 0.6% employee unemployment insurance (PwC); the employer-paid SIS (2.00%) is excluded. Unemployment insurance has a separate higher cap (PwC page quotes 113.5 UF, an older figure) and the 90 UF pension cap is from a news report, not the Superintendencia.
- Self-employed 0.1636 depends on ProVida's commission and SIS figures and the 80% base (Ley 21.133 schedule from the ChileAtiende honorarios sheet, whose PDF text could not be extracted); treat as low confidence.
- Cap derived from the 90 UF limit effective 1 Feb 2026 at the 3 July 2026 UF value.
- Chile's annual Global Complementario scale (UTA) was not read; the SII November 2026 monthly Second Category table (re-read 2026-10-15) x 12 is used and matches the bracket thresholds.
- Statute read directly (arts. 3 and 10); SII practice on the three-year period, the extension criteria and the interaction with the 183-day/domicile test were not checked.
- The previous note that this might exist came from a Spanish-language search snippet; it is now confirmed from the statute.
