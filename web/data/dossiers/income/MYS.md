# Malaysia — earned income, nomad and expat dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026

## Summary

Residents pay progressive tax from 0% to 30% (30% above MYR 2 million) after a MYR 9,000 personal relief. Foreign-sourced income received in Malaysia is exempt for residents under an exemption order running to 31 December 2036 (PwC), but not for pay for work done in Malaysia. Foreign employees pay 2% EPF; the self-employed have no mandatory scheme in the sources.

- Notes: Non-Malaysian employees: EPF 2% with no wage limit; SOCSO employee share capped at MYR 29.75 a month (employment injury and invalidity, small). Non-citizens are outside EIS. Self-employed contributions were not covered on the PwC page; set to 0 and flagged.
- Local tax: No local income tax. Rebates for chargeable income below MYR 35,000 are not modelled. Bracket start values are lower bounds of each band (rate applies to income above).

## Digital nomad visa

### DE Rantau Nomad Pass

MDEC-run Professional Visit Pass for remote workers and freelancers with foreign employers or clients. Minimum income USD 24,000 a year for tech talent and USD 60,000 for non-tech professionals; 3-12 months with one 12-month renewal (24 months total). The pass itself does not change tax.

- Treatment: standard
- Covers work performed locally: not established
- Minimum income: USD 60,000; duration: 2 years. USD 24,000 applies to tech roles (non-tech USD 60,000). Pass valid 3-12 months, renewable once for 12 months. Employer or clients must not be registered in Malaysia; passport validity over 14 months at application. A tourist pass cannot be converted.
- Foreign income required: True; local employment required: False; social security applies: False
- Confidence: high (verified)

## Expat regimes

### Foreign-sourced income exemption for resident individuals

Resident individuals are exempt on foreign-sourced income received in Malaysia (all classes except income received from a partnership business in Malaysia), subject to conditions that PwC does not list. PwC gives the period as income received from 1 January 2022 to 31 December 2036. It does not cover a remote worker's pay for work done in Malaysia: PwC's income-determination page says an employee is taxed on employment income earned for work performed in Malaysia regardless of where payment is made, so such pay is Malaysian-sourced, not foreign-sourced. The same sourcing logic applies to freelance services performed in Malaysia. Applies to anyone tax resident, not only pass holders.

- Treatment: exempt
- Covers work performed locally: False
- Minimum income: not stated; duration: 10 years. Exemption period runs to 31 December 2036 per PwC (about 10 years from 2026). Earlier statements of 2026 or 2030 in other sources are superseded; the gazetted order itself was not opened.
- Foreign income required: True; local employment required: False; social security applies: False
- Confidence: medium (partially_verified)

### Iskandar Malaysia knowledge worker 15% rate

Flat 15% on employment income for qualified knowledge workers employed by a designated company in a qualified activity in the Iskandar region (Johor). Applies in a specific region and sector only.

- Treatment: flat_rate (15%)
- Minimum income: not stated; duration: None years. Duration not stated on the PwC page.
- Foreign income required: False; local employment required: True; social security applies: True
- Confidence: low (partially_verified)

## Residence routes

EU, US and other citizens: the DE Rantau Nomad Pass (Professional Visit Pass, up to 24 months) is open to remote workers and freelancers earning USD 24,000 (tech) or USD 60,000 (non-tech) from outside Malaysia. Skilled employees need an Employment Pass sponsored by a Malaysian employer (conditions not researched here). Visa-free entry does not permit work.
