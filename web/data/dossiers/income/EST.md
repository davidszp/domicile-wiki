# Estonia — earned income, nomad and expat dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026

## Summary

Estonia has a flat 22% income tax with a EUR 700 a month (EUR 8,400 a year) basic exemption for everyone. Employees pay 1.6% unemployment insurance plus 2% funded pension if born after 1982. The self-employed pay 33% social tax on net business income (maximum EUR 36,867.60 in 2026). The digital nomad visa needs EUR 4,500 a month (gross per the FAQ, net per the visa page), lasts up to a year plus a separate 6-month visa, and is a temporary stay, not residence; e-Residency gives no right to stay. Estonia-wide rents are about EUR 580 and 760.

## Income tax and social security

Marginal rates on taxable income (EUR):

- from 0.00: 22.00%

Personal allowance deducted before the brackets: 8,400.00 EUR.

Employee social security and health: 3.60% (cap: none stated). Self-employed: 33.00% (cap: 111,720.00).

Flat 22% on all income of residents; minimum monthly basic allowance EUR 700 from 1 Jan 2026, applicable to everybody, with no phase-out. Employee: unemployment insurance 1.6% (no cap stated) and, for those born after 1982, 2% (or 4% or 6%) to the funded pension; health cover comes from the employer's 33% social tax. The recorded 3.6% is 1.6% plus the 2% minimum pension contribution.

Self-employed notes: Sole proprietors pay 33% social tax on net business income (after deductible business expenses; cash-basis accounting). Maximum 2026 liability EUR 36,867.60, equal to a business income of EUR 111,720 (computed: 36,867.60 / 0.33), which is the recorded cap. EMTA's page (re-read 2026-10-15) states the 33% rate and the 2026 maximum of EUR 36,867.60, computed on monthly bases of EUR 8,860 for the first three months and EUR 9,460 for the other nine (EUR 111,720); it gives no minimum. Self-employed also pay 22% income tax on net business income.

Local tax: No municipal income tax in the PwC page.

## Digital nomad visa

The Digital Nomad Visa is a D visa for up to 365 days (plus a possible separate visa of up to 6 months) for remote workers earning EUR 4,500 a month. It is a temporary stay and not a residence permit. e-Residency (a digital identity for running an Estonian company) does not give residence, a visa, or any right to live in Estonia or the EU.

## Expat regimes

No expat or impatriate tax regime was found.

Regimes recorded in the JSON file:

- **Digital Nomad Visa (D visa, temporary stay)** (digital_nomad_visa, treatment: standard): Long-stay (D) visa up to 365 days for people working remotely for a foreign employer, their own foreign company or mainly foreign clients. Income threshold EUR 4,500 a month, shown for the six months before applying (e-Residency FAQ: 'gross of tax', a EUR 150-a-day benchmark, evidence from the past six months; the e-Residency visa page: 'EUR 4,500 net/monthly'). Cannot be renewed: a second visa of up to six months can follow, so total stay is 1.5 years. More than 183 days in 12 months makes the holder an Estonian tax resident taxed at 22%. It is not a residence permit and e-Residency gives no right of residence. Minimum income: 54,000 EUR. Duration (years): 1.5. Social security still applies: False.

## Living costs

Estonia-wide average rents (Tallinn pulls upward): 1-bedroom EUR 580, 2-bedroom EUR 760.

## Residence routes

EU citizens may live and work freely. US and other non-EU remote workers can use the Digital Nomad Visa (up to 1.5 years total); longer stays and local employment need a residence permit, typically employer-sponsored (not researched from an official page).
