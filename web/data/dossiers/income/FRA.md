# France — earned income, nomad and expat dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026

## Summary

French income tax for 2026 (on 2025 income) is calculated per household share: 0% to EUR 11,600, 11% to EUR 29,579, 30% to EUR 84,577, 41% to EUR 181,917 and 45% above, with extra surtaxes of 3% above EUR 250,000 and 4% above EUR 500,000 for a single person (so 48% and 49%). Employees pay roughly 20.5% of gross pay in social contributions including CSG/CRDS; a micro-entrepreneur providing liberal services pays 25.6% of turnover.

## Expat regimes

### Impatriate regime (art. 155 B CGI)

Employees assigned to France by a foreign employer or recruited abroad, who were not French tax residents in the five calendar years before arrival, may exempt either their actual salary supplements linked to the move or a flat 30% of total remuneration (for positions taken up from 16 November 2018); pay for foreign workdays can also be exempt, with total exemptions capped at 50% of total remuneration (or at 20% of taxable remuneration for supplements plus foreign workdays). Taxable pay cannot fall below that of a similar job at a comparable French company.

- Treatment: partial_exemption, exempt share 30%
- Minimum income: none stated; maximum: none stated
- Duration: 8 years. Eight years from the year of arrival.
- Foreign income required: no; local employment required: yes; social security still due: yes

Open points: PwC page does not say whether social security is due on the exempt part; assumed due (the exemption is an income tax measure). No minimum salary stated on the opened page. Tax code text on legifrance not opened.

## Residence routes

EU/EEA and Swiss citizens move freely. France has no digital nomad visa (not confirmed on an official page). Skilled non-EU employees use the Talent Passport - Qualified Employee (gross salary at least EUR 39,582 from 1 January 2026) or the EU Blue Card (EUR 59,373); executives under 'Legal Representative' need EUR 65,629.08. Language (A2) and civic-values exam requirements are tightening in 2026. Non-EU freelancers need a professional-activity permit, not verified here.
