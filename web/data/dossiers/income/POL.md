# Poland — earned income, nomad and expat dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026

## Summary

Poland taxes resident earned income at 12% up to PLN 120,000 and 32% above, with a PLN 30,000 tax-free amount (modelled as a 0% bracket). Employees pay 13.71% social security plus 9% health. B2B contractors can choose the tax scale, a 19% flat tax or a lump-sum (ryczalt) regime with 12% for designated IT services, and pay a fixed ZUS social contribution of about PLN 1,870-2,020 a month plus health. No nomad visa and no expat tax regime were found.

## Income tax and social security

Marginal rates on taxable income (PLN):

- from 0.00: 0.00%
- from 30,000.00: 12.00%
- from 120,000.00: 32.00%

Personal allowance deducted before the brackets: 0.00 PLN.

Employee social security and health: 22.71% (cap: 282,600.00). Self-employed: 17.90% (cap: none stated).

Resident income tax is 12% on income up to PLN 120,000 (tax-free amount PLN 30,000, delivered as a PLN 3,600 tax-reducing amount) and PLN 10,800 plus 32% above that; a 4% solidarity levy applies above PLN 1,000,000. Employee contributions: 13.71% (pension and disability 11.26% up to the PLN 282,600 annual cap, sickness 2.45% uncapped) plus 9% health on income net of those contributions, uncapped. The recorded rate is the combined 22.71%; the cap applies to the 11.26% pension and disability part only.

Self-employed notes: Derived approximate rate on an income of about EUR 54,000 (about PLN 230,000 at an assumed ~4.25 PLN/EUR): fixed ZUS social contributions of PLN 1,870.24 a month (lower end, PLN 22,443 a year) plus 9% health on the tax scale (about PLN 18,700) is roughly PLN 41,100, i.e. about 17.9%. The fixed ZUS part does not scale with income; at EUR 130,000 (about PLN 550,000) the same components give roughly 12-13%. Statutory ZUS social contributions on the minimum base of PLN 5,652 a month are PLN 1,870.24-2,020.58 a month depending on the accident rate (33.09% of the base at the lower end). The pension and disability cap does not change the fixed base. Health, included in the recorded rate at the scale option: 9% of income (min PLN 432.54 a month from Feb 2026) on the tax scale, 4.9% of income on the 19% flat tax, and a fixed amount on ryczalt by annual revenue (PLN 498.35 to 60,000; 830.58 for 60,000-300,000; 1,495.04 above 300,000 on the PwC 2023 labels, which equal the 2026 figures quoted by a secondary source). A preferential ZUS on a PLN 1,441.80 base (PLN 441.76-480.11 a month) is available to new businesses. Business income: flat 19%, 14% for certain professions and 12% for designated IT services on lump-sum basis.

Local tax: No local income tax in the sources opened; the page does not cover local taxes. The 0% bracket from 0 to 30,000 stands in for the tax-free amount, so personal_allowance is 0.

## Digital nomad visa

No digital nomad visa found. Non-EU nationals need a work or residence permit; none was researched from an official page.

## Expat regimes

No expat or impatriate tax regime was found in the sources opened.

Regimes recorded in the JSON file:

- **Ryczalt (lump-sum) for designated IT services** (other, treatment: flat_rate): Self-employed contractors (B2B) can pay a revenue-based lump-sum tax instead of the scale. PwC states certain designated IT services pay 12%; the full rate schedule by activity was not opened. Costs cannot be deducted; health contribution is a fixed amount by annual revenue. Minimum income: none stated. Duration (years): None. Social security still applies: True.
- **Linear 19% flat tax on business income** (other, treatment: flat_rate): Sole traders may elect a flat 19% on business profit (after costs) instead of the 12%/32% scale; health is 4.9% of income. Certain professions pay 14%. Minimum income: none stated. Duration (years): None. Social security still applies: True.

## Living costs

Warsaw 1-bedroom: PLN 3,888 a month (Otodom Analytics asking rent, 40-59 m2 band). 2-bedroom: PLN 6,056 a month (Otodom Analytics asking rent, 60-89 m2 band, city-wide, via Investropa; wider than a strict 2-bedroom).

## Residence routes

EU citizens may live and work freely and register their stay. US and other non-EU citizens need a visa and a work permit or a business route (sole trader or company); no remote-worker visa was found, and no official Polish page was opened for these routes, so the details are unverified. Skilled employees are normally sponsored by a Polish employer.
