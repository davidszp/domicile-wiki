# Denmark — earned income, nomad and expat dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026

## Summary
Top marginal rate 60.5% (including the 8% AM-bidrag). The 27% researcher / highly-paid employee scheme (32.84% all-in) is the main expat regime; there is no digital nomad visa. 1-bed rent about DKK 15,150, 2-bed about DKK 17,300 in Copenhagen.

## Income tax and social security
Marginal rates on income after AM-bidrag: 37.059% (bottom tax 12.01% + average municipal 25.049%), 44.559% above DKK 641,200, 52.059% above DKK 777,900, 57.059% above DKK 2,592,500; personal allowance DKK 54,100. With the 8% AM-bidrag the combined ceiling is 60.5% (PwC). Employees also pay ATP DKK 1,188. Church tax excluded. Self-employed: AM-bidrag 8% also applies to business income (tax.dk C.C.8.2).

## Digital nomad visa
None. Entry as a remote worker depends on ordinary residence rules; no dedicated visa.

## Expat regimes
- Researcher scheme: 27% flat + 8% AM-bidrag = 32.84%, up to 84 months, PhD-level researcher at a Danish employer, no Danish tax liability in the previous 10 years.
- Highly paid employee scheme: same rate, minimum guaranteed salary DKK 65,400 a month (2026), 84 months.

## Living costs
Copenhagen, asking rents: 1-bed DKK 15,150 (range 12,000-17,000), 2-bed DKK 17,300 (range 15,000-22,000).

## Residence routes
EU: free movement. US/other: permit tied to employment or a scheme (not verified here).

## Open questions
- ATP for self-employed persons not confirmed on a page opened; the 8% AM-bidrag on business income is confirmed on tax.dk.
- Copenhagen municipality rate (about 23.4%) not verified; national average used.
- Azets says researchers are exempt from the minimum salary; skat.dk page text opened mentions the DKK 65,400 test only for highly paid employees.
- Minimum is a monthly average test on the guaranteed salary after ATP; annual figure is simply 12 x 65,400.
- Non-EU work-permit schemes and any nomad route not verified.
- Cost data from one aggregator.
- Employment deduction (beskaeftigelsesfradrag) of 12.75% of employment income, capped at DKK 63,300 in 2026 (PwC), is not captured in the brackets or the DKK 54,100 allowance; at the cap it lowers the tax base by about DKK 63,300.
