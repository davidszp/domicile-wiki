# Switzerland — earned income, nomad and expat dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026

## Summary

Income tax in Switzerland is levied federally, cantonally and by municipality. For a single resident of the City of Zurich the combined 2026 marginal rate reaches about 41.0% between CHF 185,100 and CHF 793,900 (federal 13.2% plus Zurich cantonal 13% x 2.14). Employees pay AHV/IV/EO 5.3% and unemployment 1.1% (capped at CHF 148,200) plus non-occupational accident cover and pension fund; the self-employed pay about 10% AHV on net profit. Health insurance is a separate mandatory personal premium.

Combined single-filer schedule for the City of Zurich: federal direct tax scale (marginal 0.77% to 13.2%, 11.5% above CHF 793,900) plus Zurich basic tax (marginal 2% to 13%) multiplied by the cantonal factor 0.95 plus the City of Zurich municipal factor 1.19 = 2.14. Church tax (factor 0.08-0.14) excluded. Both levels are applied to the same income, ignoring different deductions. Cantonal variation is large: municipal factors in Zurich canton range 0.71 to 1.28, and other cantons (Zug, Schwyz) are much lower while Geneva is higher; one guide estimates tax on CHF 100,000 taxable profit at about CHF 15,000-18,000 in Zug versus about CHF 28,000 in Geneva (not verified). Employee 6.4% = AHV/IV/EO 5.3 + ALV 1.1; ALV is capped at CHF 148,200 and nothing is levied above, so the cap is shown as none for the AHV. Not included: non-occupational accident premium 1-4% and pension fund (BVG) contributions.

- Employee social security: 6.40% (no cap).
- Self-employed social security: 10.00% (no cap).
- Self-employed notes: Self-employed pay both halves of AHV/IV/EO: about 10.0% of net profit uncapped (sliding scale from about 5.4% at low income; minimum about CHF 530 a year); second pillar (BVG) is optional for sole proprietors, Pillar 3a up to CHF 36,288 without a pension fund. Rate comes from a secondary guide; PwC says only that the self-employed cover employer and employee shares.

Open points: Self-employed AHV rate from a secondary guide, not the Compensation Office. Federal and cantonal deductions differ so the combined schedule is an approximation. Mandatory health insurance premium (not tax) not included.

## Living costs

City: Zurich.

- 1-bedroom rent: 2,820 CHF per month (2025). neho.ch average asking rent for a 2.5-room apartment in the city of Zurich (2025 data, listing-based; 2.5 rooms is roughly one bedroom plus living room). Numbeo gives a lower crowd-sourced 1-bedroom city-centre average of CHF 2,466.84 (1,154 entries, 165 contributors, 6 October 2026).
- 2-bedroom rent: 3,436 CHF per month (2025). neho.ch average asking rent for a 3.5-room apartment in the city of Zurich (2025 data, listing-based; 3.5 rooms is roughly two bedrooms plus living room).

Stadt Zürich 2026 survey: median net rent of existing contracts is CHF 1,497 (2 rooms), 1,628 (3 rooms), 1,955 (4 rooms), and CHF 30.5 per m2 for new non-subsidised lettings (CHF 2,287 for a typical 75 m2 flat), far below asking rents because of rent control on existing leases. Vacancy was 0.07% in June 2024.

## Residence routes

EU/EFTA citizens have a right to work under the free movement agreement and mainly register with the canton (permit is essentially a registration). Non-EU/EFTA nationals (including US) need a work permit tied to a Swiss employer or business; annual quotas apply to third-country permits (figures not verified from an opened page). Switzerland has no digital nomad visa and non-EU remote workers cannot simply settle. Any person living 90+ days in Switzerland (30 if working) becomes tax resident.
