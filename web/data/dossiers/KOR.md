# South Korea — family-office tax dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026
Reviewer: research agent (Claude), unreviewed by a tax adviser

## Summary

South Korea taxes residents on worldwide income. Gains on foreign shares cost 22%, while Korean listed shares are exempt below major-shareholder thresholds. Financial income above KRW 20 million a year is taxed at up to 49.5%. Foreigners resident five years or less in ten are taxed on foreign dividends only if they are remitted to Korea. Inheritance tax is estate-based at 10%-50%, with a KRW 500 million deduction, and covers worldwide assets of a resident. There is no net wealth tax, but a national holding tax on high-value housing applies. An exit tax reaches foreign shares from 2027.

US citizens: US citizens pay US tax on top; the Korea-US treaty (1976) gives credits. Korean inheritance tax applies to a resident's worldwide estate in addition to US estate tax (no estate tax treaty).

## Residence and eligibility

Resident if domiciled in Korea, residing 183 days or more in a tax year, or (from tax years beginning 2026) residing 183 consecutive days across two tax years; facts-and-circumstances test (family, assets, occupation). Foreigners: worldwide taxation only after more than five years of residence in the last ten.

- **Investor visa (D-8) / immigrant investor (F-2/F-5) and other visas** (EU, US, OTHER): Investor and real-estate/public-fund investment immigration schemes exist (thresholds not verified in this pass).

## Capital gains

### Korean resident (worldwide) (`standard`)

Capital gains are taxed separately from global income. Gains on Korean listed shares are exempt for shareholders below the 'major shareholder' thresholds (1% stake on KOSPI, or KRW 5 billion market value). Gains on foreign shares are taxed at 20% national plus 10% local income tax (22% in total) after a KRW 2.5 million annual deduction. Major shareholders' Korean shares and unlisted shares are taxed at 22% up to KRW 300 million of gain and 27.5% above (local tax included). Real estate gains are taxed at the progressive rates of 6%-45% plus local tax, with a long-term holding deduction.

- **Exemptions and thresholds:** KRW 2.5 million basic deduction per category per year. One house per household exempt under conditions (high-value homes partly taxable).
- **Important exceptions, statute references and announced changes:** The planned financial investment income tax was repealed before taking effect (general knowledge). Exit tax: residents of 5+ years who are major shareholders pay tax on unrealised gains on Korean shares when emigrating; from 1 January 2027 foreign shares are included (above KRW 500 million in total, with carve-outs for foreign employees). Heavier rates for multi-home owners in regulated areas have been suspended by decree (status after May 2026 not verified).
- **Tax year / confidence / verification:** 2026 / medium / partially_verified (checked 2026-10-15)
- **JSON numbers:** headline 49.5% (0.495); listed domestic 0% (0.0); listed foreign 22% (0.22); private business sale 27.5% (0.275); local real estate 49.5% (0.495); foreign real estate 49.5% (0.495).
- **How derived:** Foreign listed shares 20% + 10% local = 22%. Domestic listed 0 for a holding below 1% and KRW 5 billion. Private business (unlisted, major shareholder) 25% + local = 27.5% on gains above KRW 300 million. Real estate: top 45% + local = 49.5% before the long-term holding deduction (up to 30% of the gain for general property held 15 years; more for a qualifying single home). Foreign real estate taxed the same way once resident 5 years.
- **Holding period:** Major-shareholder shares held under one year: 33%. Real estate held under 1-2 years: higher flat rates (not detailed).
- **Trader recharacterisation:** Trading as a business is business income at global rates up to 49.5%.
- **Foreign assets:** Gains on foreign assets other than shares are taxable only if the seller has been resident 5 years or more at the time of sale (PwC). Foreign-share gains taxed at 22%.

### Short-term foreign resident (5 years or less in 10) (`foreign_resident_5y`)

Same as the standard regime.

## Dividends

### Korean resident (worldwide) (`standard`)

Dividends are part of financial income: up to KRW 20 million a year is taxed at 15.4% (14% plus local tax) by withholding; above that, financial income is added to global income at progressive rates up to 45% plus 10% local tax (49.5%). Domestic dividends get a gross-up and dividend tax credit. Foreign dividends of residents taxed on worldwide income are included at global rates with a foreign tax credit. From 2026 to 2028, dividends from qualifying high-dividend Korean companies can be taxed separately at 14%-30%.

- **Important exceptions, statute references and announced changes:** Foreign residents with five years or less of residence in the last ten are taxed on foreign dividends only if paid by a Korean entity or remitted to Korea (special regime).
- **Tax year / confidence / verification:** 2026 / medium / partially_verified (checked 2026-10-15)
- **JSON numbers:** headline 49.5% (0.495); domestic 43.45% (0.4345); foreign gross 49.5% (0.495); foreign tax relief: credit.
- **How derived:** Foreign: 45% + 10% local = 49.5% before credit. Domestic: grossed-up dividend (assumed 10% gross-up) taxed at 45% less a credit equal to the gross-up gives 39.5% national + 3.95% local = 43.45% (gross-up rate not verified); qualifying high-dividend companies 2026-2028: separate 14%-30% national.
- **Outbound withholding:** 22% WHT (20% + local) on dividends to non-residents, reducible by treaty (not opened).
- **Treaty network:** 97 income tax treaties as of January 2026, including the US and EU states (PwC).
- **Holding companies:** CIT 10%-25% plus local tax; dividends received deduction for Korean subsidiaries (not researched).

### Short-term foreign resident (5 years or less in 10) (`foreign_resident_5y`)

Foreign dividends not paid by a Korean entity and not remitted to Korea are not taxed; Korean dividends as standard.

- **Tax year / confidence / verification:** 2026 / medium / partially_verified (checked 2026-10-15)
- **JSON numbers:** headline 49.5% (0.495); domestic 43.45% (0.4345); foreign gross 0% (0.0); foreign tax relief: exemption.
- **How derived:** Foreign dividends 0 if kept abroad (remittance basis); domestic as standard.

## Inheritance and gifts to children

### Korean resident (worldwide) (`standard`)

Korea levies inheritance tax on the whole estate (estate basis) at 10%-50%: 10% up to KRW 100 million, 20% to 500 million, 30% to 1 billion, 40% to 3 billion and 50% above. A lump-sum deduction of KRW 500 million (or a basic deduction plus personal deductions) and a spouse deduction of at least KRW 500 million (up to 3 billion) apply. Gift tax uses the same rates per recipient, with a deduction for gifts from parents to an adult child of KRW 50 million per 10 years (KRW 20 million for a minor). Gifts within 10 years before death are added back to the estate.

- **Exemptions and thresholds:** Lump-sum deduction KRW 500 million per estate; spouse deduction KRW 0.5-3 billion; financial asset deduction up to KRW 200 million; 3% credit for timely filing (general knowledge, not verified).
- **Important exceptions, statute references and announced changes:** Government reform proposals (heir-based taxation from 2028, higher child deductions, top rate cut) were not in force for 2026 as far as checked; PwC still shows 10%-50%. Business succession deduction for family businesses does not cover a listed portfolio.
- **Tax year / confidence / verification:** 2026 / medium / partially_verified (checked 2026-10-15)
- **JSON numbers:** basis `estate`, children `taxed`, scope `worldwide`, exemption 500,000,000 KRW, brackets (marginal): 10% (0.1) from 0 KRW, 20% (0.2) from 100,000,000 KRW, 30% (0.3) from 500,000,000 KRW, 40% (0.4) from 1,000,000,000 KRW, 50% (0.5) from 3,000,000,000 KRW; other transfer costs 0% (0.0); deemed disposal 0% (0).
- **Gifts:** Gift tax 10%-50% on each recipient's gifts (10-year aggregation per donor group); KRW 50 million deduction per 10 years for an adult child from lineal ascendants (KRW 20 million for minors); a separate marriage/childbirth gift deduction was added in 2024 (not verified). (same as inheritance: False; tax-free after years: 10)
- **Business relief:** Family business succession deduction (up to KRW 60 billion) for qualifying SMEs and mid-sized companies; not for portfolio shares.
- **Foreign assets:** If the deceased was a Korean resident (domicile or residence of 183 days or more), worldwide assets are taxed; for a non-resident deceased, Korean assets only.
- **Residence/domicile rules:** Residence of the deceased decides scope; there is no special exclusion for foreign nationals (not verified). Gift tax: a resident recipient is taxed on worldwide gifts.
- **Business relief covers listed shares:** False

### Short-term foreign resident (5 years or less in 10) (`foreign_resident_5y`)

Same as the standard regime.

**Surviving spouse:** No exemption: a spouse allowance of at least KRW 500 million and at most KRW 3 billion is deducted (Inheritance and Gift Tax Act art. 19; EY 2026), and the rest of what the spouse inherits is taxed on the 10%-50% scale.

## Wealth tax

### Korean resident (worldwide) (`standard`)

No net wealth tax (PwC). Korea does levy a national comprehensive real estate holding tax (CRET, 종합부동산세) on individuals whose total official value of housing exceeds KRW 900 million (KRW 1.2 billion for a one-house household); it is treated here as a property tax, not a wealth tax, because it reaches only Korean real estate and its current rate schedule could not be verified.

- **Tax year / confidence / verification:** 2026 / medium / partially_verified (checked 2026-10-15)
- **JSON type:** `none`
- **Property tax (not a wealth tax):** Local property tax 0.07%-5% of statutory value (PwC). CRET (national): on (official housing value - KRW 900 million / 1.2 billion) x fair-market-value ratio, progressive rates (top 2.7% for owners of two homes or fewer and up to 5% for three or more under the 2023 law, from general knowledge, not verified), plus 20% rural special tax. A EUR 5 million home would pay CRET. Acquisition tax 1%-12%.

### Short-term foreign resident (5 years or less in 10) (`foreign_resident_5y`)

Same as the standard regime.

## Special regimes

### Short-term foreign resident (5 years or less in 10) (`foreign_resident_5y`)

Foreign residents who have lived in Korea for five years or less in the last ten are taxed on foreign-source income only if it is paid by a Korean entity or remitted to Korea.

- **Eligible citizenships:** EU, US, OTHER
- **Eligibility:** Foreign national resident in Korea with cumulative residence of five years or less in the preceding ten years.
- **Duration (years):** 5
- **Requires qualifying activity:** False

## Company taxation (kept separate from personal tax)

Headline CIT rate: 25% (0.25). CIT for fiscal years from 1 January 2026: 10% to KRW 200 million, 20% to 20 billion, 22% to 300 billion, 25% above, plus local income tax of 10% of CIT (top 27.5%). Additional 20% tax on excess retained earnings of large conglomerate companies to 2028.
