# Turks and Caicos Islands — family-office tax dossier

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
Tax year: 2026
Reviewer: research agent (Claude), unreviewed by a tax adviser

## Summary

The Turks and Caicos Islands has no direct taxation of any kind: no income, capital gains, dividend, inheritance, estate, gift, wealth or annual property tax. The material cost is stamp duty on buying land (up to 10% of the whole price on Providenciales). Gifts of land to a spouse, parent or child are exempt from stamp duty. Permanent Residence Certificates are available by investment (USD 1 million real estate on Providenciales, USD 300,000 on the smaller islands) plus a USD 25,000 PRC fee.

## Residence and eligibility

No tax residence test needed. PRC by investment: USD 1,000,000 home on Providenciales / USD 300,000 on the smaller islands (IMI Daily), USD 1,500 admin + USD 25,000 PRC fee (TCI Government flowchart, undated). Figures need confirmation with Invest Turks and Caicos.

## Capital gains

None. JSON 0 everywhere.

Stamp duty on land purchases (whole value, not marginal): Providenciales and other islands not listed below: 0% under USD 25,000, 6.5% USD 25,000-250,000, 8% USD 250,000-500,000, 10% above USD 500,000. Grand Turk, North, Middle and South Caicos and Salt Cay: 5% USD 25,000-100,000, 6.5% above. Charged on the higher of price and market value, paid by the purchaser within 30 days. Changes in beneficial ownership of land-holding companies are taxed (8% x share fraction x land value).

## Dividends

None; no withholding; no treaties. JSON 0.

## Inheritance and gifts to children

No inheritance, estate or gift tax; no forced heirship (Chambers Private Wealth 2025). Stamp duty exemption for gifts of land to spouse, parent or child (VisitTCI; Dentons/Chambers 2026). Treatment of land passing on death not confirmed. JSON basis none, fully_exempt, other cost 0.

## Wealth tax

None; no annual property tax.

## Special regimes

None needed.

## Company taxation (kept separate from personal tax)

No corporate income tax or withholding taxes. Economic substance, CRS and FATCA reporting apply; beneficial ownership regime being expanded (Chambers 2025).
